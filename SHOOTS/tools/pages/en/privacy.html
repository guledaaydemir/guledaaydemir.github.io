Privacy policy

The short version: SHOOTS doesn't know who you are, and your photos never leave your devices unless you share them. From version 1.2 it counts how the app is used with Google Analytics for Firebase, without your name, account, advertising ID or photos.

  • No account
  • No ads
  • No tracking
  • Photos stay on device
  • Usage stats not linked to you

Effective 22 October 2026 · updated 14 November 2026 · applies to SHOOTS for iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, version 1.0 and later (usage statistics from version 1.2)

This policy explains how the SHOOTS app ("SHOOTS", "the app") handles information. SHOOTS is made by Gül Eda Aydemir, an independent developer ("I", "me"). If anything here is unclear, email solostaseraofficial@gmail.com.

1. What I collect

SHOOTS has no account or sign-in and no servers of its own, and it shows no ads. Up to version 1.1 it collected nothing at all. From version 1.2, it uses Google Analytics for Firebase, a service from Google, to count how the app is used, so I can see which features help people and where the app gets in their way. This is the only data SHOOTS collects.

What the app records:

  • Which screens and features you use: for example, that a shoot started, what started it (the shutter, a raised palm, “Cheese”, Apple Watch, Siri or a widget), its mode, timer, interval and number of shots, and whether it finished, was stopped or failed, and how many shots it took.
  • The welcome steps, whether a permission was granted, and the app's error codes.
  • When the SHOOTS+ screen is shown and from where, the plan you pick, and whether a purchase, free trial, renewal or cancellation happened, with the product, price and currency. Not your Apple ID, name or payment details.
  • A few settings, such as the app's language and where you save captures, and taps on the links to my other apps (DUMP, Solo.Swipe).

What Google Analytics adds by itself: a random ID created when the app is installed (the app-instance ID), the device model, iOS and app version, language, first launch and session times, and an approximate location (country, region or city) that Google works out from a shortened IP address.

What is never collected: your photos and videos, what the camera sees, what you say, anything you type, file names, your name, email, contacts or precise location, and the advertising identifier (IDFA). SHOOTS uses the version of the Firebase SDK that has no advertising-ID code, and it switches off ad storage, ad personalization, the vendor identifier (IDFV) and ad-network attribution.

How it's used: only to see totals and trends (for example, how many people finish their first shoot or use the palm start) and to fix problems. The data is tied only to the random app-instance ID, never to your identity. I don't combine it with other data, use it for advertising, sell it or share it, and it isn't used to track you across apps or websites. Google processes it on my behalf under the Firebase data processing terms; see also Google's privacy policy. Detailed event data is kept for 14 months and then deleted; totals in reports may be kept longer. Under the GDPR, the legal basis is my legitimate interest in understanding and improving the app.

Apart from Google Analytics, the only internet connections SHOOTS makes are to Apple (purchases and subscription status), and directly between your own devices when you use a remote. I don't receive your photos, videos or settings, and I can't see what you shoot.

Photos kept "App Only" are stored in a folder that SHOOTS excludes from iCloud and device backups, so they stay on that one device. Deleting the app deletes them. Items you delete in SHOOTS move to its Trash and are removed for good after 30 days, or sooner if you empty them yourself. Photos you save to your Photos library follow your Photos and iCloud settings, which are managed by Apple.

3. Permissions and why they're asked

  • Camera — to take your photos and videos. Required for the app to work.
  • Microphone — only when you use a mode that records sound (Video, the + Video modes, Cinematic, Live), or to listen for “Cheese” if you turn on the voice start. Not needed for photos.
  • Speech recognition — only for the “Cheese” voice start. Recognition runs on your device; what you say isn't recorded, stored or sent anywhere.
  • Local network — only to use another iPhone or iPad as a remote. Your devices find each other nearby and connect directly.
  • Photos — only to save your shots to your library and put each shoot in its own album, when you choose that option. SHOOTS does not upload or analyze your library.

You can change these at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security. SHOOTS does not ask for your location, contacts or notifications, and it does not show the App Tracking Transparency prompt because it doesn't track you.

4. On-device image and voice analysis

To start a shoot when you raise your palm, and for Pose Coach to know whether you're in close-up, full body or out of frame, SHOOTS looks at the camera picture using Apple's on-device Vision framework while the camera is open. For the “Cheese” start it uses Apple's on-device speech recognition. This happens only on your device, nothing from it is saved, and it isn't used for anything else. The usage statistics only record that a hands-free start happened (palm or voice), never the picture or the sound.

5. Apple Watch and remotes

The SHOOTS Watch app communicates directly with SHOOTS on your own iPhone using Apple's WatchConnectivity framework. If you use another iPhone or iPad as a remote, the two devices connect directly to each other over your local network (Wi-Fi or Bluetooth nearby), and a device can only connect after you tap Allow on the camera. Commands (start, stop, zoom, lens and mode) and, with SHOOTS+, the live viewfinder preview frames travel between your devices only. They never reach me or any server.

6. Purchases

SHOOTS+ is sold through Apple's App Store. Apple processes the payment and tells the app whether your subscription is active. I don't receive your name, email address or payment details. Apple's handling of purchase information is described in Apple's privacy policy. The app's usage statistics record which plan was chosen and its price (see section 1), without your Apple ID or payment details.

7. Links to other apps and websites

SHOOTS can open the App Store pages of my other apps (DUMP, Solo.Swipe), Apple's subscription settings, and this website. Those pages are run by Apple or are covered by their own policies. Other apps of mine have their own privacy policies, linked on their websites.

This website sets no cookies and shows no ads. To count visits it uses Cloudflare Web Analytics, which stores nothing in your browser and doesn't follow you across other sites or build a profile of you. When a page loads, a small script from Cloudflare reports the page address, the page that linked to it, your browser, operating system and device type, your country (worked out from your IP address) and how quickly the page loaded; I see only totals, never individual visitors. See Cloudflare's privacy policy. These website statistics are separate from the app's usage statistics described in section 1.

8. Sharing

When you share a photo from SHOOTS, iOS shows its standard share sheet and you choose where it goes. What happens next is governed by the app or service you pick.

9. Children

SHOOTS is rated 4+. It doesn't ask anyone for personal information, and its usage statistics (section 1) aren't linked to anyone's identity, including children's.

10. Your rights

I don't hold any data that tells me who you are. Everything SHOOTS stores is on your device, under your control: delete photos in the app, or delete the app to remove all of its data. Deleting the app also resets the random analytics ID, so new statistics can't be connected to the old ones. Because the statistics aren't linked to your identity, I can't look up “your” data without that ID; if you'd like it deleted, or have a question about your rights under laws such as the GDPR and the CCPA, email me.

11. Changes

If SHOOTS ever changes how it handles information (for example, if it added a feature that needs a server), this policy will be updated before that version is released, with a new effective date at the top, and the App Store privacy details will change to match.
