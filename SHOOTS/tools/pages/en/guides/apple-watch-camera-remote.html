If you own an Apple Watch, you already own a camera remote. Apple’s Camera Remote app turns your wrist into a viewfinder for your iPhone, and for a lot of people it’s all they’ll ever need. This guide shows how to use it properly, what it does well, where it stops, and how the SHOOTS Watch app (made by us) fills the gap for hands-free photoshoots.

How to use Apple’s Camera Remote

These steps follow Apple’s Apple Watch User Guide (checked again for watchOS 27 on 24 October 2026).

  1. Steady the iPhone. Apple’s own tip: put it on a stable surface or a tripod. A wall, a shelf or your bag works too (see places to prop your phone).
  2. Open Camera Remote on your Watch. When the Camera is open on your iPhone, a hint also appears at the bottom of the watch face: tap it to open the Smart Stack, then tap Camera Remote.
  3. Frame the shot. Use the Watch as a viewfinder. Turn the Digital Crown to zoom. Tap the key area of the preview to adjust exposure.
  4. Take the photo. Tap the shutter. By default there’s a 3-second timer, enough to lower your arm.
  5. Record video if you want. Touch and hold the shutter until recording begins; tap Stop to end.
  6. Review. Tap the thumbnail at the bottom left and swipe through your shots. Everything is saved to the Photos app on your iPhone.

Under More Options you’ll find four settings: Timer (the 3-second timer on or off), Camera (Front or Rear), Flash (Auto, On or Off) and Live Photo (Auto, On or Off).

What Apple’s app does really well

  • It’s free and already there. Nothing to install, nothing to set up.
  • You see the frame before you shoot. For solo photos, that live preview is the single most useful thing a remote can do. No more walking back to check if your head is cut off.
  • It uses the iPhone’s own Camera. Every photo goes through the same camera and lands in the same library as any other photo you take.
  • Exposure, zoom, flash, Live Photo and video are all on your wrist, plus a review screen.

If you want three or four posed photos at a landmark and you’re happy to tap for each one, Camera Remote is excellent. Use it.

Where it stops

  • One photo per tap. Apple’s guide lists no interval and no photo count. Every frame needs your hand on your wrist, and it shows: the raised arm, the eyes glancing down, the pose reset after each shot.
  • The delay is 3 seconds or nothing. Fine when you’re already in position; tight if you start from the phone and need to walk ten steps.
  • Range. About 33 feet (10 m) of Bluetooth range, so you can’t go far.

That’s the gap an interval camera with its own Watch app fills: you start the shoot once, drop your arm, and the phone keeps shooting while you move.

Tap once, then forget your wrist exists. That’s the whole idea.

The SHOOTS Watch app

SHOOTS is a hands-free interval camera for iPhone, with a companion Apple Watch app. On the iPhone you set the plan: Photoshoot start (Instant to 60 seconds), Timer mode (0.5 seconds to 1 hour between photos) and Photo limit. The Watch starts that plan from wherever you’re standing.

Free on the Watch:

  • Start, stop and resume a shoot.
  • Zoom with the Digital Crown; tap the zoom label to jump to the next lens.
  • Flip between front and back cameras.
  • Options: capture mode, flash and End Shoot.
  • Countdown, progress (like 5 / 10) and a “Done · 10” when the shoot ends.

With SHOOTS+: a live viewfinder on the Watch, so you see exactly what the iPhone sees and frame the shot from your wrist. If you use the ghost overlay on the iPhone (a see-through earlier photo over the viewfinder, for matching a pose), the Watch viewfinder shows it too.

What you need: an Apple Watch with watchOS 10.6 or later, paired with the iPhone you’re shooting with. Apple Watch pairs with an iPhone (Apple’s setup guide, checked 24 October 2026), so SHOOTS on iPad has no Watch remote. In SHOOTS, Settings → Apple Watch shows whether the Watch app is installed.

No Watch, or shooting with an iPad? Use a second iPhone or iPad

Apple’s Camera Remote lives on the Watch only. SHOOTS can also turn another iPhone or iPad into the remote for the device you’re shooting with: an old iPhone, a partner’s phone, or an iPad as a big viewfinder. It also works the other way round, with an iPad as the camera and an iPhone in your hand.

  1. On the camera: open Menu → Remote and turn on “Another iPhone or iPad as remote”.
  2. On the remote: open Menu → Remote → “Use this device as a remote”, and tap the camera in the list.
  3. Allow it once: tap Allow on the camera. You won’t be asked again for that device.

The remote starts and stops shoots and switches lenses and modes for free. The live picture on the remote’s screen is part of SHOOTS+ (on the camera device). The two connect directly over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth nearby, with no server; allow Local Network for SHOOTS on both. The full setup and troubleshooting are in How to use an iPad or second iPhone as your iPhone camera remote.

Tips for wrist-triggered photos

  1. Use a short start delay. When you start from the Watch you’re already in place. Set Photoshoot start to 3 or 5 seconds: just enough to drop your arm and breathe out.
  2. Hide the Watch. Wear it on the wrist away from the camera, or turn your arm so the face points inward. A lit-up screen in a portrait is distracting.
  3. Start once, pose many times. With a 3 second interval, change something on every frame: weight, chin, hands. The 12 poses that work on a timer are a good list to run through.
  4. Stop from the wrist. Someone walks into frame? Tap stop, wait, resume.
  5. Keep the phone close. If the Watch says iPhone not nearby, step closer and check Bluetooth. If it says Open SHOOTS, open the app on your iPhone; it also needs the iPhone unlocked once to connect.
  6. Review on the phone. Each shoot lands in its own album in the SHOOTS gallery. Heart the keepers and turn them into a carousel with DUMP.
Group photo? The Watch is how the photographer gets in the picture. Set everything up at the phone, walk into the group, and start the shoot from your wrist. Full setup in How to take a group photo with everyone in it.

No remote at all?

A few options. In SHOOTS, turn on Hands-free start (in Photoshoot start): raise your open palm to the camera until the ring fills, or say “Cheese”, and the shoot starts with at least a 3-second countdown. Both are free; more ways, including Siri and widgets, are in How to start a photoshoot hands-free. For Apple’s Camera app, on iOS 26 and later, supported AirPods (AirPods 4, AirPods 5, AirPods Pro 2, AirPods Pro 3 and AirPods Max 2) can take a photo in the Camera app with a press of the stem (checked 24 October 2026). Or simply: set a longer Photoshoot start in SHOOTS (10 to 60 seconds), tap the shutter on the phone and walk into place. The interval does the rest. Our guide on taking more than one photo on a timer explains the settings.
