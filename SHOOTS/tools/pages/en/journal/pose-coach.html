Watch any photographer at work and you’ll notice they never stop talking. Not small talk: direction. Chin down. Little more. Turn your shoulder to me. Now walk. Walk. Look back. Laugh, a real one. Half of the job is the camera; the other half is that voice, quietly moving you from one good frame to the next, so you never have to think about what your hands are doing.

Shoot yourself on a timer and that voice is the first thing to go. You get three poses in, then your mind goes blank, and frames fourteen to twenty-five are the same face with increasing panic. The Pose Coach in SHOOTS exists for exactly that moment.

What it does

Between shots, a photographer’s voice calls your next pose. Not at random: SHOOTS looks at the frame first and picks a cue that fits how you’re standing in it. You prop the phone, start the shoot (with the shutter, a raised palm, “Cheese” or a remote, see how to start a photoshoot hands-free), and from then on someone is directing.

How it reads the frame

About once or twice a second, SHOOTS looks at a camera frame, on the iPhone itself, with Apple’s Vision framework. Nothing is uploaded, and nothing is kept. It’s asking one question: how is this person framed?

  • Face and shoulders, legs out of the picture: close-up cues. “Rest your chin on your hand.” “Smile with just your eyes.” “Tuck your hair behind your ear.” “Look up and away. Dreamy!” “Blow me a kiss!”
  • Knees or feet in the picture: full-body cues. “Now walk toward me.” “Hand on your hip.” “Cross one leg in front of the other.” “Turn sideways and look back at me.” “Give me a spin!” “Crouch down low.” “Jump! Big energy!”
  • Either way: cues that work at any distance. “Chin down, just a little.” “Turn your shoulder toward me.” “Look over your shoulder at me.” “Now a soft smile.” “Give me a real laugh!” “Surprise me. Any pose you like!”
  • Nobody there, or half out of the picture: framing help. “Step into the frame so I can see you.” “Come a little closer to the middle.”

One thing it never does is say left or right. Which side is “your left” depends on the camera and the way you’re facing, and a coach that gets it wrong is worse than no coach at all. So it says “turn your shoulder toward me”, and lets your body work out which one.

The rhythm: hold it for a couple of shots

A good photographer doesn’t change the pose every click; they let you settle, then shoot it twice. The Pose Coach works the same way. It calls a new pose right after a photo lands, so the whole interval is yours to move into it, and then lets you hold each pose for at least five seconds, a couple of shots, before the next cue. Recent cues are set aside so none comes back soon, which keeps a 25-photo shoot from feeling like a loop.

That makes the interval matter:

Timer modeHow it feels
1 sSeveral frames per pose. Great for walking, spins and laughs: movement inside one cue.
2–3 sThe sweet spot: two or so frames per pose, one to settle and one to keep.
5 s and upAbout one pose per frame. Calm, editorial, good for outfits.

How to get the most out of it

  1. Frame for the poses you want. Want walks, spins and jumps? Make sure your legs are in the picture, so it reads full body. Want face-and-hands moments? Move the phone closer.
  2. Turn the voice up. The Voice volume slider is in the Sound sheet. Sounds follow the Silent switch, and SHOOTS tells you when Silent Mode is on.
  3. Do it, then make it yours. Take the cue literally on the first frame, then exaggerate it on the second. That second frame is often the keeper.
  4. Start it from your spot. Pair the coach with a raised-palm start and the first instruction you hear is from the coach, not from your own nerves.
  5. Keep a favorite list anyway. The coach is great at filling gaps; your own signature poses still belong in the set. Steal a few from 12 poses that work on a self-timer.
Talk to me stays quiet in the modes that record video (Video, Cinematic and the + Video modes), so the voice doesn’t end up in your clips. The recorded voice is English; when the app is set to one of its other ten languages, the iPhone’s own most natural voice for that language reads the cues.
Your face can’t freeze if someone keeps giving it something to do.
