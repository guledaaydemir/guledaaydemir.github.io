No tripod? Places and tricks to prop your phone for photos

Anything that holds still is a tripod, if you’re a little creative and a little stubborn.

By Gül Eda Aydemir22 October 20265 min read
The short answer
  • Best everyday props: a wall or ledge at chest height, your bag, a glass or bottle on a café table, a stack of books.
  • Wedge it: something under it, something behind it, something stopping it from sliding. Tilt it back slightly.
  • Level it with the level guide before you step away.
  • Never prop it over water, a drop or a busy path, and stay within a few steps.
  • A cheap phone clamp or mini tripod fixes almost everything else.

Tripods are great, right up until you’re out for the day and yours is at home. Good news: most of the best solo photos ever taken on a phone were taken from a wall, a bag or a coffee cup. Here’s where to put your phone, how to keep it there, and how to make sure the photo comes out straight.

Outdoors

  1. Walls and ledges. The classic. At chest height they’re perfect for full-length photos. Lay the phone on the wall with the lens just past the edge, or stand it up against a stone and a bag.
  2. Your bag or backpack. Put it on a bench or the ground and tuck the phone into the top opening. It’s soft, grips the phone, and you can squash it to adjust the angle.
  3. Steps and stairs. One step is a low tripod; the step above is its backrest. Great for dramatic, low-angle shots on the 0.5× lens.
  4. Benches. Lean the phone against the backrest, or prop it on the armrest with your bag behind it.
  5. Railings and fences. Only with a clamp. Never balance a phone on a railing, especially one with a view, because views usually come with a drop.
  6. Rocks, tree stumps, fence posts. For hikes and beaches. Wedge the phone with smaller stones and keep it off the sand.
  7. The ground. Lay the phone almost flat, leaning on your sunglasses case or a shoe, for a low angle that makes legs and skies look endless.

At a café or restaurant

  • A glass or bottle is the best phone stand ever invented. Lean the phone against it, with a napkin under the bottom edge so it can’t slide.
  • A sugar jar, the menu holder, the napkin dispenser: anything heavy and upright.
  • Your cup. Lean the phone on your mug and shoot yourself across the table. Hold the coffee for the photo.

Café portraits work best seated, from about eye level, with the window to your side. Ask the staff if you’re worried about blocking anyone; most people don’t mind for a minute.

At home

  • Books on a chair. Stack them until the lens reaches the height you want. The most adjustable tripod in the house.
  • Shelves and window sills. Instant waist or chest height.
  • An ironing board. Its height adjusts, it’s stable, and it folds away. Honestly underrated.
  • A laundry basket or a stool, with a book on top for fine-tuning.
  • Doors. A closed door is a flat, vertical surface at every height. Push a chair against it, put books on the chair, and lean the phone back against the door: it can’t fall backward.
  • Mirrors. Lean the phone on a dresser facing a big mirror and shoot your reflection hands-free. You get the mirror-selfie look, but with both hands free and the phone out of your face.

Shooting outfits at home? The light and height tricks are in How to shoot outfit photos at home by yourself.

Cheap gear that’s worth it

  • A phone clamp or mini tripod with bendy legs. Wraps around railings, branches, chair backs and door handles. The single best upgrade for solo photos.
  • A small phone stand that folds flat into a pocket. Perfect for café tables and ledges.
  • A hair tie or rubber band. Strap the phone to a water bottle or a thick book for an instant stand.
  • A grippy case. A smooth case slides off everything; a rubbery one stays put.

Whatever the height, try to keep the phone upright and level rather than tilted up or down. Tilting makes walls and door frames lean. If you need to see more, raise or lower the phone instead of tilting it, or step back and use a wider lens.

Straighten it before you walk away

A wonky horizon is the number one giveaway of a propped phone. Fix it before the shoot, not after:

  1. Turn on the grid and the level guide. In SHOOTS: the Viewfinder button on the camera screen (Grid, Level), or Settings, then Framing, then Rule-of-thirds grid and Level guide. The horizon line lights up when the phone is level. (The iPhone Camera app has a Grid and Level setting too, under Settings, Camera.)
  2. Shim it. Slide a folded napkin, a coin or a receipt under one corner until the line lights up.
  3. Frame with room to spare. Space above your head and below your feet. You can crop later.
  4. Lock focus. Tap where you’ll stand, then touch and hold to lock focus and exposure.
  5. Give yourself time. A 10 second Photoshoot start is enough to walk five or six steps and settle. Or don’t race at all: turn on Hands-free start and raise your palm (or say “Cheese”) once you’re in place.

Keep your phone safe

  • The wobble test. Tap the surface next to the phone. If it moves, it’s not ready.
  • Wind. Outdoors, a phone standing upright is a small sail. Lay it lower or wedge it harder.
  • Edges. Never over water, a drop, a road or a busy path.
  • Sand, water, spilled drinks. Use your bag as a buffer.
  • Sun and heat. A phone on a hot wall or a car in summer heats up fast. SHOOTS pauses the shoot if the iPhone overheats and continues when it cools, but shade is kinder to your battery.
  • People and pets. Pick quiet spots, stay within a few steps, and keep the phone in sight. An Apple Watch or a second iPhone lets you start and stop the shoot without leaving your spot: see the Apple Watch camera remote guide.

Questions people ask

How can I prop up my phone to take a picture without a tripod?
Lean it against something heavy on a flat surface at the right height: a glass or bottle on a table, a stack of books on a chair, the top of a wall, or the inside of an open bag on a bench. Wedge it so it can’t slide, check that it’s level, set a start delay and step into place.
How do I stop my phone from falling over when I prop it up?
Give it three points of contact: the surface underneath, something behind it, and something stopping the bottom from sliding (a book, a bag strap, a folded napkin). Tilt it back slightly rather than standing it perfectly upright, and tap the surface next to it before you walk away. If it wobbles, it will fall.
What can I use as a phone stand at home?
Books, a mug, a shelf, a window sill, a chair with books on it, an ironing board (handy because its height adjusts), a laundry basket or a closed door with a chair against it. A cheap phone clamp or mini tripod with bendy legs turns door handles and chair backs into stands too.
What angle is best for full-body photos with a propped phone?
Put the lens between waist and chest height and keep the phone upright and level. Much lower looks up at you; much higher makes your legs look shorter. For portraits from the waist up, raise it to about eye level.
How do I keep the horizon straight without a tripod?
Turn on a level guide. In SHOOTS it’s under Settings, Framing: a horizon line that lights up when the phone is level. The iPhone Camera app also has a Grid and Level setting. Adjust the phone with a folded napkin or a coin under one corner until the line lights up.
