How to take more than one photo on an iPhone timer (and space them out)

Apple’s timer counts down and shoots one moment. Here’s how to squeeze more out of it, and when to switch to an interval camera.

By Gül Eda Aydemir22 October 20267 min read
The short answer

The iPhone Camera app’s timer counts down 3, 5 or 10 seconds, then captures one moment. To get more than one photo:

  • Live Photo: leave it on, then pick a different key photo in the Photos app.
  • Re-fire from your wrist or ears: Apple Watch Camera Remote, or the stem of supported AirPods on iOS 26 and later.
  • iPhone Duo: Smart Take shoots up to 20 photos over a couple of minutes while you pose.
  • Interval camera: set a start delay, a gap between photos and a photo count, and the phone keeps shooting while you move. In SHOOTS: Photoshoot start 10 s, Timer mode 3 s, Photo limit 25.

You prop the phone on a wall, set the timer, run into place, and get exactly one moment. Maybe your eyes are closed. Maybe your hair is mid-blow. Maybe it’s actually good, and you want ten more like it with a slightly different pose. Either way, you walk back to the phone and start again.

This guide covers what the built-in timer really does, three ways to get more out of it without installing anything, and how interval shooting works, the setting that turns one timed photo into a whole photoshoot. We’ll show the settings in SHOOTS, the hands-free camera we make, but the ideas work in any interval camera.

What the built-in iPhone timer actually does

According to Apple’s iPhone User Guide (checked again for iOS 27 on 24 October 2026):

  1. Open Camera. Tap or swipe up from the camera modes at the bottom of the screen.
  2. Tap Timer. Choose 3, 5 or 10 seconds.
  3. Tap the Shutter button. The countdown starts and you get into the frame.

The 5 second option is newer: Apple’s iOS 18 version of the same page lists only 3 and 10 seconds. To turn the timer off, open Timer again and switch it off.

What’s missing is just as important. There’s no setting for how many photos to take and no gap between them. Apple’s guide doesn’t say exactly what the timer captures, and depending on your iPhone and whether Live Photo is on, you may find a single photo, a Live Photo or a short burst in your library. Whatever lands, it all comes from the same second or two: one pose, one expression, one chance.

iPhone Duo is the exception. On iPhone Duo, Apple suggests Smart Take instead of the timer: stand the phone up, tap the Smart Take button, then strike a pose or hold still, and it takes up to 20 photos over a couple of minutes (checked 24 October 2026). It decides when to shoot; there’s no gap or photo count to set.
If you do find a Burst after a timer shot, you can keep only the frames you like. Apple’s Burst mode guide shows how: tap the Burst thumbnail, tap Burst, mark the photos you want and tap Done.

Three ways to get more photos without another app

  1. Use Live Photo as a safety net. Apple says a Live Photo captures what happens just before and after you take the photo. If you blinked on the main frame, open it in Photos, tap Edit and set a different key photo. It’s still the same couple of seconds, but it often rescues a blink.
  2. Fire the shutter from your Apple Watch. The Camera Remote app shows a live preview on your wrist, and every tap of its shutter takes another photo (with a 3-second timer by default). You can shoot ten photos without walking back to the phone. Our Apple Watch camera remote guide covers it step by step.
  3. Use your AirPods as a shutter. On iOS 26 and later, AirPods 4, AirPods 5, AirPods Pro 2, AirPods Pro 3 and AirPods Max 2 can take a photo in the Camera app when you press (or press and hold) the stem. Set it up in Settings, under your AirPods’ name, then Camera Remote.

All three work, and all three have the same catch: you trigger every photo. Your hand moves to your wrist or your ear before each frame, your eyes go with it, and you spend the shoot thinking about the button instead of the pose.

A timer gives you one moment. An interval gives you a whole photoshoot.

Interval shooting: photos spaced out, hands-free

An interval camera (photographers call it an intervalometer) takes a photo, waits, takes another, and keeps going on its own. You set three numbers:

  • Start delay: how long before the first photo. Enough to walk into place.
  • Interval: the gap between photos. Enough to change pose, but short enough to stay in the flow.
  • Photo count: how many photos before it stops.

Total time is roughly the start delay plus the interval times the number of gaps. A 10 second delay, a 3 second interval and 25 photos is 10 + 24 × 3 = 82 seconds: about a minute and a half of posing, with no walking back and forth.

How to set it up in SHOOTS

  1. Prop the phone. Chest height, back camera facing where you’ll stand. No tripod? See places to prop your phone.
  2. Set Photoshoot start. Instant, 3, 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 seconds before the first capture.
  3. Set Timer mode. High-quality intervals from 0.5 s, 1 s, 2 s, 3 s, 5 s, 10 s, 30 s, 1 min and 5 min up to 1 h. Burst intervals of about 42 ms, 100 ms and 200 ms are part of SHOOTS+.
  4. Set Photo limit. 5, 10 or 25 photos in the free version; 50 up to 1,000, or Forever (keeps going until you tap stop), with SHOOTS+.
  5. Tap the shutter and step in. A 3-2-1 countdown with beeps runs, then SHOOTS fires shot after shot. The on-screen counter shows progress like 13 / 25. Tap again to stop; a paused shoot can be resumed. A volume button works as the shutter too.
  6. Or start it from where you stand. Turn on Hands-free start in Photoshoot start, walk to your spot, and raise your palm or say “Cheese”. The countdown is at least 3 seconds, so you can drop your hand first. Free, like Siri, the widgets and the remotes in How to start a photoshoot hands-free.

The honest version: for one quick group photo, the built-in timer is fine and already on your phone. The moment you want to pose, move between frames, or come home with more than one usable shot, the interval is what makes the difference.

Questions people ask

Can the iPhone camera timer take multiple photos?
Not in a way you can control. Apple’s guide describes a 3, 5 or 10 second countdown (3 or 10 seconds before iOS 26), with no setting for how many photos to take or how far apart. Whatever the timer captures comes from the same moment. The exception is iPhone Duo: its Smart Take takes up to 20 photos over a couple of minutes when you strike a pose or hold still. For photos spaced out on a gap you choose, on any iPhone, use an interval camera app.
How do I take a photo every few seconds on iPhone?
Use an interval camera app. In SHOOTS, set Photoshoot start to the time you need to get into place, Timer mode to the gap between photos (0.5 seconds to 1 hour), and Photo limit to how many photos you want, then tap the shutter. Free shoots go up to 25 photos; 50 and more, Forever and burst intervals are part of SHOOTS+.
Can I set the iPhone timer longer than 10 seconds?
Not in the built-in Camera app: Apple lists 3, 5 and 10 seconds. Camera apps can go longer. SHOOTS offers 15, 30 and 60 second start delays, which helps for big groups or when the phone is far away.
Does interval shooting make a timelapse video?
Not in SHOOTS: it takes separate still photos. If you want a video, the iPhone Camera app has a Time-lapse mode, and Solo.Shots can turn a series of photos into a timelapse.
Can I take more photos from my Apple Watch?
Yes. Apple’s Camera Remote app takes another photo every time you tap the shutter on your wrist, with a 3-second timer by default. The SHOOTS Watch app starts a whole interval shoot from your wrist; the remote is free and the live viewfinder on the Watch is part of SHOOTS+.
