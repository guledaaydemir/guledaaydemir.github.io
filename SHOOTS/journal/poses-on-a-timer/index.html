Journal · Posing

12 poses that work on a self-timer

No photographer to say “chin down”. No problem. These are the poses that hold up when the only direction you get is a beep.

By the SHOOTS Journal · edited by Gül Eda Aydemir22 October 20265 min read

Posing for a timer is its own skill. With a photographer, you react: they talk, you laugh, they say "there!" and you hold it. On a timer there's nobody to react to, you can't see the screen, and your hands suddenly feel like they belong to someone else. What you do get is a photographer with infinite patience, who will happily take twenty-five frames while you figure it out.

So the trick isn't one perfect pose. It's a handful of poses that look good from a fixed camera, with a rhythm that matches each one. If you haven't set up a solo shoot before, start with how to take good photos of yourself alone, then come back with the phone propped and ready.

Three rules before the poses

  1. Move a little on every frame. Weight to the other foot, chin left, chin right, hand to hair. Small changes give you 25 different photos instead of one photo 25 times.
  2. Look away more than you look at the lens. Candid-looking frames come from looking at something real: the view, your coffee, your shoes.
  3. Match the interval to the pose. One photo a second for anything that moves, every 3 seconds for anything posed, 5 seconds or more for anything that needs a reset. The why behind each one is in interval shooting on iPhone.

The 12 poses

1. The walk-in

Start five or six steps behind your spot and walk slowly toward the phone, arms loose, eyes just past the lens. Interval: 1 s. Mid-step frames almost always look natural.

2. The look-back

Walk away from the phone, then turn your head over your shoulder on the beep, as if someone called your name. Interval: 1 s.

3. The wall lean

One shoulder on the wall, weight on the back foot, front knee softly bent; change where your hands are each frame. Interval: 3 s.

4. The ledge sit

Sit on a wall, a step or a bench, cross your ankles or pull one knee up, and look out at the view instead of at the camera. Interval: 3 s.

5. The look-away

Stand still and give your eyes a real target off to the side: the sea, a sign, a passing bike. Switch targets every frame. Interval: 3 s.

6. The busy hands

Fix an earring, push up your sunglasses, roll a sleeve, adjust a collar. Hands with a job never look awkward. Interval: 2 s.

7. The coffee break

Sit at a table, cup in both hands, eyes down at the cup, then up past the lens, then down again. Interval: 3 s.

8. The crouch

Squat low, forearms on your knees, and look down at something on the ground (draw in the sand if you're on a beach), then glance up. Interval: 3 s.

9. The hair flip and spin

Flip your hair or turn a full circle; do it twice, because the first one is always too careful. Interval: 1 s, or a burst interval with SHOOTS+ to catch the hair in mid-air.

10. The laugh

Start with a fake laugh, a big one, and keep going; somewhere around the third beep it turns into a real one. Interval: 1 s.

11. The paparazzi hand

Sunglasses on, palm half-raised toward the lens, chin down, as if the cameras caught you leaving. Best at night with the flash on. Interval: 1 s. The full look is in Be your own paparazzi.

12. The view shot

Turn your back to the camera and face the view, and let the place be half of the photo; on the 0.5× lens, stay near the center of the frame, where it stretches you least. Interval: 5 s. Perfect for trips; more in solo travel photos.

Put them together: the five-by-five

A 25-photo round splits neatly into five poses with five frames each. Pick five from the 3 second group, and you have a complete posed set that never stalls halfway because you ran out of ideas. Then do a second, faster round with the 1 second poses.

The Beep shutter sound in SHOOTS plays one beep the moment each photo is taken, so you always know when to switch. Count five beeps, change pose. It's the closest thing to a photographer saying "next".

Or let someone call the poses for you. With SHOOTS+, turn on Talk to me and set Coaching to Pose coach (or Both, for compliments in between). Between shots a photographer's voice calls your next pose, and it looks at the frame first: up close you get face-and-shoulder cues like "Rest your chin on your hand." or "Smile with just your eyes.", with your legs in the picture you get "Now walk toward me.", "Crouch down low." or "Give me a spin!" Each pose holds for a couple of shots. It covers several of the twelve above, and it never runs out of ideas at frame fourteen. More in Never run out of poses, and on why a voice helps at all.

The camera doesn't need you to be a model. It needs you to keep moving.

After the shoot

Twelve poses over two rounds is fifty frames. In the SHOOTS gallery, heart the ones you like, drag across the thumbnails to select the rest and delete them (they wait in Trash for 30 days). Save the keepers to Photos, then turn them into a carousel with DUMP and clear the rest of your camera roll with Solo.Swipe. The full recipe, with timings, is in the 10-minute solo photoshoot. Shooting outfits? The lean, the busy hands and the walk-in are the core of outfit photos at home.

Questions people ask

How do you pose for a self-timer photo?
Choose a pose, then change one small thing on every frame: your weight, your chin, your hands or where you look. Look away from the lens more often than at it, and give the pose a matching rhythm: a photo every second for movement, every 3 seconds for posed shots.
Is there an app that tells you how to pose?
SHOOTS has one built in: with SHOOTS+, turn on Talk to me and choose Pose coach. Between shots a photographer’s voice calls your next pose, picked for whether the camera sees you close up or full body, and each pose holds for a couple of shots. You can also choose compliments, or both.
What is the best self-timer interval for posing?
3 seconds for posed shots, 1 second for walking, spins and laughing, and 5 seconds or more when you need to reset between frames, for example to walk back to a mark or show more of a view.
