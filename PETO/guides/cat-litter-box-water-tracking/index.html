How to track your cat’s litter box, water and toilet habits (and why vets care)

By Gül Eda Aydemir · Updated November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Reading font
Quick answer

To track your cat’s litter box, note each day how many pee clumps there are and roughly how big, what the stool looks like, any straining, blood or accidents outside the box, and how much water your cat drinks. After a week or two you know your own cat’s normal, so changes stand out: contact your vet about any change in peeing habits, and treat a cat (especially a male) straining with little or no urine as an emergency that needs a vet right away. Why track your cat’s litter box and water?

Your cat can’t tell you when something feels wrong, but the litter box often can. A change in how often your cat pees, how much comes out or what the stool looks like is one of the first things you can actually see. That’s why vets ask about it. Cornell Feline Health Center lists difficult or painful urination, peeing more often, crying out while peeing, blood in the urine, peeing outside the litter box and frequent licking of the genital area as common signs of feline lower urinary tract disease.

Water matters in both directions. Cornell notes that cats that drink less water are more likely to show these signs, and PDSA lists drinking more than usual among the signs of a urine problem. A short daily log turns “I think she’s been drinking a lot lately” into something you can show your vet.

What is normal for your cat? Start with a baseline

There is no single “normal” that fits every cat. How often a cat pees, how big the clumps are and how much it drinks vary with the cat, its age, the weather, the litter and especially its food: wet food contains water, so the amount your cat drinks from the bowl can change when the food changes. So instead of general numbers, learn your own cat’s baseline.

Log the litter box and the water bowl the same way every day for a week or two, while everything is fine. After that you know what an ordinary day looks like for your cat, and a change stands out on its own: more trips with smaller clumps, a day with no pee at all, a bowl that suddenly empties twice as fast. Cats Protection puts it simply: it’s always good to check for any changes in your cat’s litter tray behaviour.

To measure water, fill the bowl with a measuring jug, and before you refill it, measure what’s left. The difference is roughly what your cat drank (some evaporates, and other pets may share the bowl). With several bowls or a fountain, just measure the same way every time.

When is it urgent? Warning signs and emergencies

Contact your vet as soon as you notice a change in your cat’s peeing habits. PDSA’s advice is clear: call your vet, and never wait to see whether the symptoms improve. Signs to take seriously include:

  • straining in the box, or crying while peeing,
  • many trips with only small amounts, or nothing at all,
  • blood in the urine,
  • peeing outside the box, for example in the bath, the sink or on cold floors,
  • a lot of licking around the genitals, hiding, eating less or low energy.

A cat straining with little or no urine is an emergency. It can mean the urethra is blocked, which Cornell calls a true medical emergency. Male cats are at greater risk of a blockage than females, and Cats Protection says a cat that can’t pass any pee needs immediate veterinary attention. Don’t wait until the morning: call your vet or the nearest emergency vet straight away.

How to keep the litter box clean

A clean box also helps the record: fresh clumps are easier to see in clean litter. Cats Protection suggests scooping twice a day and cleaning the whole tray at least once a week. Cornell’s advice on lower urinary tract disease includes keeping litter boxes clean, having enough of them, placing them in quiet, safe areas of the home, and providing clean, fresh water at all times.

How free cat care works in PETO

For cats, PETO adds free cat care to the usual feeding, playtime and attention routines:

  • Litter cleaning reminders. Litter is a normal routine: pick the times, the repeat (every day, set days, or every N days counted from the day it was actually done) and “Remind me”. Mark it done right from the notification, snooze it for 15 minutes or skip it.
  • Water in ml. Log how much your cat drank, in millilitres.
  • Toilet log. Log pee, poop or both; the urine amount (none, small, normal, large); the stool (normal, soft, diarrhoea, hard); and warning signs: straining, blood, outside the box, frequent trips, pain. The toilet log is log-only, not a routine, so it never sends you reminders.

Everything comes together on the cat-only “Litter & water” card on your cat’s page: today’s numbers, a 7-day average and when the litter was last cleaned. The 7-day average is your cat’s baseline at a glance.

The card also shows gentle prompts based on what you logged. If warning signs were logged in the last 48 hours, it says “These can be signs of a urinary problem. Show this log to your vet.” If no pee has been logged for over 24 hours, it says “If you haven’t seen any in the box either, call your vet.” And it reminds you that “A cat straining with little or no urine needs a vet right away.” PETO doesn’t diagnose anything; the prompts only point you to your vet.

PETO tip

Log the toilet entry while you scoop, scoop still in hand: pick the urine amount and the stool, and tick any warning sign you noticed. It takes ten seconds and keeps your 7-day average honest.

How to show the log to your vet

The “Litter & water” card links to your cat’s Health record and to the vet report. The health record itself is free: vaccines with booster reminders, a weight chart and vet visits. With PETO Premium, Vet report (PDF) creates an A4 report to show or send to your vet, and for cats it includes a 14-day “Litter & water” table. Without Premium, just show the card and your logs on your phone.

Appetite belongs in the picture too: PDSA lists eating less among the signs of a urine problem. A feeding log can record whether your cat ate all, some or little of the meal or skipped it, plus a note.

Before the appointment, ask your vet whether to bring a urine sample, and write down when you first noticed the change.

Questions people ask

How much water should my cat drink a day?

It varies with the cat, its size, the weather and especially its food: wet food contains water, so the amount from the bowl can change with the diet. That’s why a log of your own cat’s usual amount is more useful than a general number. If your cat suddenly drinks clearly more or less than usual, ask your vet.

How often should a cat pee?

There’s no single number that fits every cat. Learn your own cat’s usual pattern by noting the number and size of pee clumps for a week or two, and contact your vet if it changes, especially if your cat goes more often and passes less.

Is a cat straining in the litter box an emergency?

It can be. If your cat strains and passes little or no urine, treat it as an emergency and contact a vet straight away, day or night. Male cats are at greater risk of a urinary blockage. Don’t wait to see whether it improves.

Is PETO’s cat care free?

Yes. Litter cleaning reminders, water in ml, the toilet log and the Litter & water card are free for cats. The vet report PDF, which includes a 14-day Litter & water table, is part of PETO Premium.

Can PETO tell me if my cat is ill?

No. PETO keeps your log and shows gentle prompts based on what you logged, such as suggesting you show the log to your vet after warning signs. It doesn’t diagnose anything; for any health question, ask your vet.
