Health record

How to keep your pet’s vaccines, weight and vet visits in one place

By Gül Eda Aydemir · Updated November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Reading font
Quick answer

A pet health record should hold vaccines and preventives with the date given and the next due date, weight over time, current medication, vet visits and notes, plus the microchip number and any allergies. Keep it in one place, set booster reminders using the dates your vet gives you, and bring it to every appointment. In PETO the health record is free (vaccines with booster reminders, a weight chart, vet visits and appointments); the A4 vet PDF report and the medication routine are Premium.

What should a pet health record include?

A pet health record is one place where you, your family and your vet can see the essentials: which vaccines and preventives your pet has had and when the next ones are due, how its weight has changed over time, what medication it takes, what happened at past vet visits, and basics like the microchip number and known allergies. A folder, a notebook or an app all work. What matters is that it’s complete, dated and easy to bring to the vet.

Vaccines and preventives: record dates, not just names

There’s no single vaccine list that fits every pet. Which vaccines your pet needs, and how often, depends on the species, age and health of the animal, on how it lives (indoors or outdoors, kennels, travel, other pets at home) and on local rules and disease risks. The WSAVA vaccination guidelines say this openly: they are broad guidance that vets adapt to local conditions, and lifestyle-based vaccines are chosen in a conversation between vet and owner. So your vet sets the plan. Your part is to keep a clean record of it.

For each vaccine or preventive, note:

  • what it was, as written on the vet’s card or certificate,
  • the date it was given,
  • the next due date your vet gave you,
  • where it was given, and anything you noticed afterwards.

Keep the vet’s own vaccination card or certificate as well. For travel, boarding or official requirements, that document may be what you’re asked to show.

Why booster reminders help

Boosters are easy to forget because they come months or even years apart, long after the last appointment has faded from memory. PDSA’s advice on dog vaccines is that dogs need regular boosters throughout their life, and that a dog that misses one may stay protected for only a couple of months after the due date before it is at risk again. A reminder set on the day of the vaccination, using the date your vet gave you, takes that worry off your mind.

The same goes for flea, tick and worm preventives. A dated record shows at a glance whether the last treatment was three weeks or three months ago, which is exactly what your vet will ask.

Why tracking your pet’s weight over time matters

A single weight says little on its own. A series of weights taken the same way shows a trend: slow gain over the winter, a loss hidden under a fluffy coat, a change after switching food. PDSA recommends checking your dog’s body shape regularly to know whether it has gained or lost weight, and using shape as well as the scales, with your vet as the best person to ask for advice. Vets look at this too: WSAVA’s nutrition guidelines ask vet teams to assess body condition at every visit.

Weigh at home in a consistent way, with the same scale at a similar time of day, or ask whether your vet practice lets you use their scale between appointments. Write the date next to every weight. If the trend moves in a way that worries you, that is a question for your vet, not for an app.

Medication, vet visits, microchip and allergies

Write medication down exactly as your vet prescribed it: the name, the amount, how often, until when and why. Never change or stop a medication without asking your vet first. Vet visit notes can be short: the date, the reason, what was done, what to watch for and when to go back.

Keep the microchip number, known allergies and your vet’s phone number where the whole household, and anyone who looks after your pet, can find them quickly.

How to keep your pet’s health record in PETO

Each pet in PETO has its own Health record, and it’s free. You open it with the Health record button next to the pet’s Today’s Log. Inside you can:

  • Add vaccines and preventives to get booster reminders.
  • Log weights and see them as a trend chart.
  • Keep vet visits with the pet’s record, and add upcoming vet appointments (also free) with reminders. They appear as a red cross in the Calendar.
  • Set up a medication routine with PETO Premium. It works like any other routine, with times, a repeat rule and a reminder, and you enter what your vet prescribed. PETO reminds you; it doesn’t advise you.
  • For cats, use the free litter and water log: water drunk plus a toilet log (pee, poop, stool and warning signs), summed up in a “Litter & water” card that links to the health record.

Your pet’s data is stored on your device. There’s no PETO account and PETO has no server of its own. PETO’s anonymous usage statistics never include health records, and you can turn them off in Settings → Data. And PETO isn’t medical advice: its friendly status labels come only from your schedule and logs, so for any health question, see your vet.

How to bring your pet’s health record to the vet

Before the appointment, take five minutes:

  1. Check the weight chart and note any change since the last visit.
  2. Scroll through recent logs for anything unusual, such as an “Ate little” appetite or a note someone at home wrote.
  3. List current medication and the questions you want to ask.
  4. Pack the vet’s vaccination card or certificate.

At the practice, you can simply show the Health record on your phone. With PETO Premium, Vet report (PDF) creates an A4 report you can show or send to your vet. For a cat, it includes a 14-day “Litter & water” table.

After the visit, update the record while it’s fresh: the new vaccine and its next due date, today’s weight, any new medication and the next appointment.

PETO tip

Do the update before you leave the waiting room: add the vaccine, the weight and the next appointment while the vet’s words are still fresh. Thirty seconds now saves a lot of guessing next year.

Share the essentials with everyone who cares for your pet

If several people look after your pet, make sure health information doesn’t live in one person’s head. In a shared home, PETO’s Family sharing lets everyone see who fed, walked or played. Questions people ask

What should a pet health record include?

Vaccines and preventives with the date given and the next due date, weight over time, current medication as prescribed, vet visits with short notes, the microchip number, known allergies and your vet’s contact details.

How often does my pet need booster vaccines?

That depends on the animal, the vaccine, its lifestyle and local rules, so your vet sets the schedule. Write down the next due date your vet gives you and set a reminder for it.

Is PETO’s health record free?

Yes. Vaccines and preventives with booster reminders, the weight log with its trend chart, vet visits and vet appointments with reminders are free. The A4 vet report (PDF) and the medication routine are part of PETO Premium.

Does PETO give medical advice?

No. PETO keeps records and sends reminders. Its friendly status labels come only from your schedule and logs. For health questions and vaccination plans, ask your vet.

Where is my pet’s health data stored?

On your device. PETO has no account and no server of its own, so it never receives your pet’s health record; its anonymous usage statistics never include health records either. Deleting the app or using Settings → Reset all data removes everything PETO stored on the device.
