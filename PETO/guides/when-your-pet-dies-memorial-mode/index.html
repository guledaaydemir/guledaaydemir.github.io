What should you do with your pet’s app when your pet dies?

Nothing has to happen today: you don’t need to delete the app, the photos or the years of records. The one thing worth doing soon is stopping the reminders, because a notification about feeding a pet who has died can catch you off guard on an ordinary morning.

In PETO, memorial mode does exactly that. It’s free for everyone and never behind Premium. Every reminder for that pet stops quietly, everything you logged is kept, and the pet’s page becomes a simple memorial. This guide explains how it works, what to tell the people who shared the care, and where to find support when the grief is heavy.

Why reminders that keep arriving hurt

A care app is meant to be persistent. While your pet is alive, that’s the point: a reminder at 7:30 every morning, a gentle follow-up when a walk isn’t logged, a playtime countdown on the Lock Screen. After a death, the same persistence lands differently. A “Meal time” alert or a nudge saying your pet misses you can arrive in a meeting or on the first quiet morning, and each one brings the loss back at a moment you didn’t choose.

The American Veterinary Medical Association writes about how loud the silence at home can feel after a pet dies. A reminder breaks into that silence without warning. Stopping the reminders isn’t forgetting. It simply lets you choose when to think about your pet, and how.

How to turn on memorial mode in PETO

Memorial mode lives where you edit your pet’s profile:

  1. Open your pet’s page and choose Edit Pet.
  2. Tap Memorial mode. A short sheet opens with the words “We’re so sorry. [Name] was deeply loved.” and explains what will happen.
  3. Check the date, add a few words if you like (they’re optional) and tap “Create memorial”, or “Not now” if you aren’t ready.

From that moment, every reminder, nudge, Live Activity and Apple Watch entry for that pet stops quietly. There’s no farewell notification and nothing to dismiss. Your other pets’ routines carry on as before. If you had recorded your pet’s bark or meow for reminders in their own voice, those reminders stop too, so the recording won’t play unexpectedly as a notification sound.

And nothing is final: you can leave memorial mode at any time, and the reminders resume.

What the memorial page shows

Instead of a list of jobs, the pet’s page becomes a quiet memorial page. It holds:

  • Dates and years of love: the time you had together.
  • A few words you write yourself, such as “Forever our little sunshine”.
  • Memories together, drawn from your own log: walks together, meals shared, play sessions and a favourite park.
  • A shareable memorial card (“Share memorial”) to send to family and friends.

The pixel pet is drawn still, without the everyday animations. The few words can be as short as you like. Some people write a nickname, others a line that only the family understands.

Logs, photos and the health record are all kept

Memorial mode doesn’t delete anything. Every log, every photo and the whole health record stay on your device as they were. That can matter in a few ways:

  • Memories. The log turns out to be a diary you didn’t know you were keeping: walks, park visits, evenings of cuddles.
  • Questions later. If you want to talk through the last months with your vet, the vaccines and the weight log are still there to look at.
  • Future pets. One day, perhaps not soon, the record may help you remember which food suited, which routine worked and what you would do again.

The health record guide explains what the record holds.

What to tell family members who shared the care

If others helped with meals and walks, they should hear the news from you, not from a reminder that suddenly goes quiet. That includes a partner, children, flatmates and anyone you share the care with through Family sharing. A few things worth saying:

  • That you’re creating a memorial, so the silence that follows makes sense to them.
  • What changes: the reminders and nudges for this pet stop; the logs, photos and health record are kept; and everything can be undone.
  • That they can help with the few words, if that feels right. Children often want to add something of their own; the RSPCA’s pet bereavement toolkit has a page on children and pet loss.

Remember people outside the home too: a sitter, a dog walker or a neighbour who looked after your pet. A short message, or the memorial card, lets them know.

PETO tip

There’s no deadline. If creating a memorial feels too soon, you can turn off the reminder switch on each of the pet’s routines for now and come back to memorial mode later. Memorial mode is the one step that stops everything for that pet at once.

Gentle ways to remember your pet

There’s no right way to remember, and no schedule for grief. A few ideas people find helpful:

  • Write a few words. The AVMA suggests writing a tribute or a letter to your pet as a way to keep memories close. The memorial page has room for a short one.
  • Share the memorial card with the people who knew your pet: family, the sitter, the neighbour who always said hello.
  • Look back through the log when you feel ready. “Memories together” brings out moments like a favourite park.
  • Visit a place you shared. The RSPCA lists favourite walks and places among the ways people remember.
  • Keep the health record for the day another animal joins your home. The RSPCA calls that a personal decision with no fixed timing.

Where to find support after your pet dies

Grief for a pet is real grief, yet in an RSPCA survey more than half of the people who answered said they felt they had to hide it. Talking to someone who understands can help. These free services are run by animal charities and a veterinary college:

  • Blue Cross Pet Loss Support (UK, formerly the Pet Bereavement Support Service): free, confidential support from trained volunteers on 0800 096 6606, 8.30am to 8.30pm every day, or by email.
  • Cats Protection’s Paws to Listen (UK): a free, confidential phone line, 0800 024 94 94, on weekdays.
  • Cornell Pet Loss Support Hotline (US): 607-218-7457, answered by veterinary student volunteers trained with professional grief counsellors. Hours vary, so check the website.
  • RSPCA pet bereavement toolkit (UK): guidance on coping, children and pet loss, and supporting someone who is grieving.
  • AVMA: Coping with the loss of a pet (US): a short, kind read.

These lines offer a listening ear, not counselling. If grief feels overwhelming or you’re struggling to cope, please reach out to your doctor or a mental health professional.

Questions people ask

What happens to reminders when I turn on memorial mode?

Every reminder, nudge, Live Activity and Apple Watch entry for that pet stops quietly, with no farewell notification. Reminders for your other pets carry on as before.

Does memorial mode delete my pet’s data?

No. Every log, photo and the full health record are kept on your device. The pet’s page becomes a memorial page with dates, years of love, a few words you write, memories together from the log and a shareable memorial card.

Is PETO’s memorial mode free?

Yes. Memorial mode is free for everyone and never behind Premium. You’ll find it under Edit Pet → Memorial mode → “Create memorial”.

Can I undo memorial mode?

Yes. You can leave memorial mode at any time, and the pet’s reminders resume. Nothing is deleted while the memorial is in place.

Where can I find support after my pet dies?

Free options include Blue Cross Pet Loss Support in the UK (0800 096 6606, every day), Cats Protection’s Paws to Listen in the UK and the Cornell Pet Loss Support Hotline in the US. They offer a listening ear, not counselling; if grief feels overwhelming, talk to your doctor or a mental health professional.
