How do you share pet care without double meals?

Most “who fed the dog?” mix-ups don’t happen because someone forgot. They happen because two people each assumed the other one did it, or both did it to be safe. The fix works with or without an app: one shared plan, one clear owner for each job, and the habit of logging every job the moment it’s done, somewhere everyone can see.

This guide walks through that system and shows how PETO’s Family sharing fits in, so a family or a flat full of housemates can see at a glance what is already done.

Why shared pet care goes wrong

A pet that lives with three people gets three sets of habits. The usual problems:

  • Double meals. One person feeds the cat at 7, the next one sees a clean bowl at 8 and feeds again.
  • Missed walks. Everyone thought someone else had the lunchtime walk.
  • Lost information. The vet said something important, but only one person heard it.
  • Nagging. “Did you feed him?” messages all day, which wear everyone out.

None of this is laziness. It’s a visibility problem: the information lives in one person’s head instead of in a place the whole household can check.

Step 1: agree on one shared plan

Sit down together once and write the routine: how many meals and at what times, when the walks happen, when there’s playtime and quiet cuddle time, and any medication your vet has prescribed. Plan for your busiest weekday, not your ideal one, and pick times that suit the people who will actually be home.

In PETO this plan is each pet’s Care Plan. Every routine (Feeding, Walk, Playtime, Attention) has one or more times a day, a repeat rule (“Every day”, “Weekdays” or “Custom”) and a “Remind me” setting. If you’re starting from scratch, the daily routine guide helps you build it.

Step 2: give every task a clear owner

“We all feed the dog” usually means nobody is quite sure. Give each recurring job a named owner, plus a backup for the days the owner is away. For example:

  • Morning meal and walk: whoever leaves the house last.
  • Evening meal: whoever gets home first.
  • Litter tray, cage or tank cleaning: one person per week, on a rota.
  • Vet appointments and medication: one adult, so nothing falls between two people.

Say the plan out loud at the start of the week or pin it to the fridge. Owning a job doesn’t mean doing everything alone; it means making sure the job gets done or is clearly handed to someone else.

Step 3: log it right after you do it

One habit prevents most double meals: log the job immediately, while the bowl is still in your hand or before you take off the lead. A log written two hours later is a guess, and a job that isn’t logged looks undone to everyone else.

PETO keeps logging to a single tap: the big FEED / WALK / PLAY / CUDDLE buttons on the pet page, the Quick Actions on Home, or the “Fed ✓” and “Walked ✓” buttons right on a reminder. You can also tell Siri “I fed Buddy in PETO” or tap Feed on an Apple Watch. Extra details such as amount, food, appetite or a short note are optional, and a log can be edited or deleted later if someone makes a mistake.

How PETO Family sharing shows that it’s already done

With Family sharing, everyone uses one shared PETO household instead of separate copies on separate phones. Everyone sees who fed, walked or played, so the second person doesn’t feed the cat again. When someone logs a job, the others can get a notification such as “Eda fed Buddy 🐾 — already done, no need to do it again.”

  • Free vs Premium: creating a shared household needs PETO Premium. Joining is free: the people you invite open the invitation link on their iPhone.
  • Privacy: sharing runs over each person’s own iCloud (Apple’s CloudKit sharing). PETO has no server of its own and no PETO account, and it cannot read the shared data.
  • Freedom to leave: you can stop sharing or leave the household at any time.

You’ll find it under Settings → Family sharing. Reminders keep working as before: iOS schedules them on the phone from your routines, with “Snooze 15 min” and “Skip” when the timing is off, and Settings → Care reminders has a switch for each care type. The Calendar is handy for a weekly check-in.

PETO tip

Agree on one house rule: if it isn’t logged, it isn’t done. Before you feed, glance at the log or check for an “already done” notification. It takes two seconds and ends the “did you feed him?” messages.

Pet care jobs for kids by age

Children can be real helpers, and a pet is a lovely way to learn routines. Match the job to the child and to the animal, and remember that an adult stays responsible for the pet’s care: the RSPCA’s advice when children help to feed pets is to supervise. As a general guide:

  • Young children: help an adult check the water bowl, put toys away, sit calmly for gentle cuddle time, and press the log button together with you.
  • School-age children: scoop a measured meal while an adult watches, brush a patient pet, walk alongside while an adult holds the lead, and log what they did.
  • Teenagers: own a whole routine such as the evening meal or a weekend walk, if the pet and your neighbourhood allow it, and log it like everyone else.

Keep medication, vet decisions and anything involving a large, strong or nervous animal with adults. If a child’s job is missed, the adult backup steps in. The pet shouldn’t have to wait for a lesson to be learned.

Handovers: shift changes, trips and sitters

Many mistakes happen at handovers: a parent leaves for a work trip, a flatmate goes home for the weekend, a neighbour comes in to feed the cat. Before you hand over:

  • Check today’s log so the next person knows what is already done.
  • Write anything unusual in a log note, for example “ate little this morning” or “short walk, it rained”.
  • Agree who takes over each of your jobs, and until when.

Vet appointments added to the Calendar come with their own reminders, and the health record guide covers keeping vaccines and weight in one place.

Questions people ask

Is PETO Family sharing free?

Creating a shared household needs PETO Premium (monthly, yearly or lifetime). Joining is free: the people you invite open the invitation link on their iPhone.

Does Family sharing need a PETO account?

No. There is no PETO account. Sharing runs over each person’s own iCloud through Apple’s CloudKit sharing; PETO has no server of its own and cannot read the shared data. You can stop sharing or leave the household at any time.

How do I know if someone already fed the dog?

Check the pet’s log: everyone in the household sees who fed, walked or played. With Family sharing you can also get a notification like “Eda fed Buddy — already done, no need to do it again.”

What pet care jobs can children do?

It depends on the child and the animal. Young children can help an adult with water and toys, school-age children can serve a measured meal or brush the pet with supervision, and teenagers can own a routine such as the evening meal. An adult stays responsible for the pet.
