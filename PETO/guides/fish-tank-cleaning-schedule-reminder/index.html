Aquarium care

How often should you clean a fish tank, and how do you never forget it?

By Gül Eda Aydemir · Updated November 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Reading font
Quick answer

Most fish and turtle care guides recommend regular partial water changes, often weekly, plus rinsing the filter media lightly in old tank water rather than under the tap, so the helpful bacteria survive. The right interval for your tank depends on its size, how many animals live in it, how much you feed and the filter, so ask your aquarium shop or vet and then keep to it. How often should you clean a fish tank?

There isn’t one interval that fits every tank, but care guides agree on the method: small, regular partial water changes and gentle care of the filter. For goldfish, RSPCA Australia suggests weekly partial water changes of 10 to 25% of the water together with a gravel clean, and the Ornamental Aquatic Trade Association (OATA) recommends a partial change at least once a week. For terrapins such as red-eared sliders, the RSPCA suggests weekly partial changes too.

Treat those figures as a starting point, not as a rule for your tank. How often your tank needs attention depends on its size, how many fish or turtles live in it, how much you feed and the filter you use. Ask your aquarium shop or vet to help you set your interval. The RSPCA recommends buying from a reputable aquatics retailer who will advise you, and OATA says: if in doubt, ask. Then turn the answer into a routine you can actually keep.

Why partial water changes and filter care matter

Fish and turtles live in their own waste. Uneaten food and droppings break down in the water, and the filter is home to helpful bacteria that break down waste such as ammonia. Without those bacteria, waste builds up and, as RSPCA Australia warns, can kill fish. Partial water changes dilute the waste the filter can’t break down; that’s exactly how the RSPCA explains them for terrapin tanks. A gravel siphon lifts droppings and leftover food out of the substrate while you change the water.

The filter needs care, but not too much. RSPCA Australia says not to clean the filter with tap water, and OATA explains why: chlorine or chloramine in tap water may kill the helpful bacteria. Instead, rinse the media lightly in the tank water you’ve just taken out. RSPCA Australia also warns that over-cleaning the filter media removes those bacteria. And treat the new water with a tap water conditioner (dechlorinator) before it goes in.

What decides the right interval for your tank?

  • Tank size. A small volume of water has less room to dilute waste.
  • Stocking. More fish, or a bigger turtle, means more waste.
  • Feeding. Uneaten food rots and fouls the water. RSPCA Australia calls overfeeding the most common problem with goldfish, and the RSPCA names over-feeding as the main cause of poor water quality in terrapin tanks.
  • Filter. The type and capacity of the filter change how much work is left for you.
  • A new tank. New tanks need closer checks while the helpful bacteria settle in; the RSPCA recommends fishless cycling when you set one up.

Water test kits show how your tank is coping. OATA advises setting a routine for testing and recording your results, so you spot changes quickly. If you’re not sure what a reading means, take it to your aquarium shop. PETO doesn’t give water-chemistry advice: the schedule is yours, set with someone who knows your tank.

Turtle tanks: more waste, same idea

Turtles are messy. The RSPCA says red-eared sliders produce lots of waste, so their water needs powerful filtration and regular maintenance. The basics are the same: weekly partial water changes, swishing the filter sponge in tank water rather than under the tap, and not overfeeding. The RSPCA also recommends registering with a reptile vet, so you have someone to ask when something looks wrong. Whatever the tank, wash your hands when you’re done.

A simple tank-cleaning routine you can keep

  1. Prepare the replacement water and treat it with a conditioner, as your shop recommends.
  2. Siphon out part of the water, cleaning the gravel or sand as you go.
  3. If the filter needs it, rinse the media lightly in the water you removed.
  4. Top up with the treated water.
  5. Write down what you did: roughly what percentage of the water you changed, and whether you cleaned the filter.

The last step is the one most people skip, and it’s the one that makes the next clean easy to plan. “Did we change the water last weekend or the one before?” has a real answer when it’s written down.

How to set an aquarium cleaning reminder in PETO

PETO’s aquarium care is free for fish and turtles. When you add a fish, PETO suggests a feeding and an aquarium routine; for a turtle, feeding, attention and aquarium. They’re only suggestions: keep, change or remove them.

  • Every N days. Set the aquarium routine to repeat every N days, from 2 to 60, using the interval you agreed with your shop or vet. It’s counted from the day the clean was actually done, so if you clean a day late and log it, the next reminder moves too.
  • Time and “Remind me”. Pick a time when you usually have half an hour free, and get the reminder at that time, 10 minutes before or 30 minutes before.
  • Log the clean. Record the % of water changed and whether the filter was cleaned.
  • Act on the reminder. Mark it done, “Snooze 15 min” or “Skip” right from the notification.

The “Aquarium” card on your fish’s or turtle’s page shows the last clean and the next date, with a short reminder of how it works: “Counted from the last clean: log it a day late and the next reminder moves too.”

Feeding works like any other routine: one or more feeding times, a default portion if you like, and one-tap logging. Since overfeeding fouls the water, a shared record of who already fed the fish is worth having in a busy home.

PETO tip

Log the clean before you put the bucket away. The next date counts from your log, so logging right away keeps the reminder in step with your tank, not with the plan.

Questions people ask

How often should I clean my fish tank?

It depends on the tank’s size, how many fish live in it, how much you feed and the filter. Care guides for goldfish from RSPCA Australia and OATA recommend a partial water change about once a week, with a gravel clean. Ask your aquarium shop or vet what suits your tank, then keep to that interval.

Can I rinse the aquarium filter under the tap?

Care guides advise against it: chlorine or chloramine in tap water may kill the helpful bacteria in the filter. Rinse the media lightly in tank water you’ve removed during a water change, and don’t over-clean it.

How often should a turtle tank be cleaned?

Turtles such as red-eared sliders produce a lot of waste, so their tanks need powerful filtration and regular maintenance. The RSPCA suggests weekly partial water changes for terrapins. Ask a reptile vet about your own setup.

How does PETO count the next cleaning date?

From the day you actually log the clean. You set the aquarium routine to repeat every N days (2 to 60); if you clean and log it a day late, the next reminder moves by a day too. The Aquarium card shows the last clean and the next date.

Is PETO’s aquarium care free, and does it give water advice?

Aquarium care is free for fish and turtles: the every-N-days cleaning routine, logging the % of water changed and filter cleaning, the Aquarium card and feeding reminders. PETO doesn’t give water-chemistry advice; ask your aquarium shop or vet.
