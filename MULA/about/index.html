Why MULA

A plant shows its leaves, but it lives through its roots.

MULA began with a simple wish: a plant app that feels like paper and quiet mornings, and that never makes you water a plant that isn’t thirsty.

The problem with “water every Sunday”

Most plant apps work like an alarm clock: water this plant every seven days. But a plant’s thirst changes with the light, the pot, the season and the room. In winter the same monstera may need half as much. A fixed schedule is how many houseplants end up overwatered, with soggy roots and yellow leaves.

Many of those apps are also loud: ads between screens, streaks, accounts, badges. Caring for something living should feel calmer than that.

Ask, don’t order

So MULA asks. When a plant’s interval is up, it asks how does the soil feel? Dry means it’s time for a drink and the watering is logged. A little moist means MULA will ask again in two days. Wet means this round is skipped. Your finger in the soil decides, not a calendar.

Read more in how often to water houseplants.

Follow the seasons

Every plant in MULA has a plan for spring, summer, autumn and winter: how often to check the soil, when to feed and when to let it rest. When the season turns, the reminders turn with it. Balcony plants get a seasonal temperature check, and with Premium, a warning the evening before a frosty night.

The name: Mūlādhāra

MULA comes from Mūlādhāra, the root chakra in Sanskrit. Mūla means root and ādhāra means support. It is the first of the seven, the one associated with feeling grounded, safe and steady.

Looking after something living is grounding for us too. That is the whole idea of MULA: no noise, only the next small thing your garden needs.

Paper, not plastic

MULA is drawn like cut paper: warm washi grain, a sun, a few hills, a soil bowl with roots. Two paper moods, Light Paper and Dark Forest. Serif type that reads like a book. Soft sounds such as a kalimba or a singing bowl instead of alarms, and quiet garden music with birdsong while the app is open. Each plant gets a cut-paper portrait to sit beside its photographs.

Private by design

There is no MULA account and no MULA server. Your plants, photos and history stay on your iPhone. There are no ads and no tracking; MULA only sends anonymous usage statistics, never your plants’ names, notes or photos. Photo identification and the light meter work on the device. Read the privacy policy.

Who makes MULA

MULA is made by Gül Eda Aydemir, an independent iOS developer who also makes Solo.Shots and Solora. Every screen, sound and word in the app is made with care, slowly. If you have an idea or something isn’t right, write to solostaseraofficial@gmail.com or say hello on Instagram at @solo.stasera.
