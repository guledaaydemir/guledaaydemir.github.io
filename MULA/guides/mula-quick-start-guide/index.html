Getting started

MULA quick-start guide: add your first plants, answer soil checks and set gentle reminders

Set up MULA in a few minutes: add your first plants, answer soil checks instead of fixed watering days, choose reminder times and see what is free.

By Gül Eda Aydemir · Updated November 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer

To start with MULA, add a plant with a nickname, an optional species (or the “General care plan”), where it lives and how much light it gets, then allow notifications. When a plant’s interval is up, MULA asks how the soil feels instead of ordering a watering, and it only logs one when you answer “Dry”. The basics are free for up to 10 plants; MULA Premium adds unlimited plants and extras such as plan editing, the light meter and Vacation mode.

Key points

  • Add a plant with a nickname, an optional species, where it lives (Indoor, Balcony or Outdoor) and its light.
  • MULA asks “How does Momo’s soil feel?” and logs a watering only when you answer “Dry”.
  • Three tabs: Today for what needs you, Garden for your plants, Settings for reminders and the look of the app.
  • Pick a reminder time and quiet hours; reminders never come at night or more than once a day per plant.
  • Free keeps up to 10 plants and 5 photos each; Premium is Annual (with a 1-week free trial) or Lifetime.

How do I start using MULA?

MULA is a calm plant and flower care app for iPhone (iOS 17 or later) and iPad, which also runs on Macs with Apple silicon, with an Apple Watch app, widgets and Siri. Getting started takes a few minutes: add a plant, tell MULA where it lives and how much light it gets, and allow a gentle reminder. There is no account to create, and your garden is stored on your iPhone, not on a server.

On first launch, after a short welcome (“Every plant has its rhythm.”), MULA asks “Would you like a gentle reminder?”. That is the iPhone’s notification permission. Then you tap “Begin my garden”. The app’s interface is in English.

How do I add a plant in MULA?

MULA asks for four things, and only the species is optional.

  • Name: a nickname, the way you talk about the plant at home, like Momo, Tsubaki or Mame in our sample garden.
  • Species: the catalog has 20, from Monstera deliciosa and the moth orchid to the snake plant, peace lily, pothos and rosemary. Any other plant can use the “General care plan”.
  • Where it lives: Indoor, Balcony or Outdoor. Balcony and outdoor plants also get a seasonal temperature check in the cold and hot months.
  • Light: Low light, Bright, indirect or Direct sun.

Not sure what your plant is? Tap “Not sure what it is?” and take or pick a photo. MULA looks at it with Apple’s on-device image classifier, which recognises plant kinds rather than exact species, so you might see “Looks like: Orchid”. When it is not confident, it says so and you choose the species yourself. The photo never leaves your phone.

Unsure about the light? “Measure”, next to the light choice, opens MULA’s light meter, a Premium feature that estimates light with your iPhone’s camera. The light guide explains what the three light levels mean for houseplants.

Every new plant starts with “A gentle starting plan”: how often to water and feed it in each season. These numbers are starting points, not botanical rules. The free version keeps up to 10 plants.

How does MULA decide when to water?

It does not decide alone. MULA’s core idea is “A soil check, never an order”. When a plant’s interval is up, MULA asks “How does Momo’s soil feel?” and lets your answer decide:

  • “Dry” (“Time for a drink”): tap “I watered Momo”. The watering is logged and the next check is planned from today.
  • “A little moist”: nothing is logged, and MULA asks again in two days.
  • “Wet”: nothing is logged and this round is skipped.
  • “Not sure”: MULA shows how to check: “Push a finger 3 cm into the soil. If it comes out clean and dry, it is time to water. If soil sticks to it, wait.”

“Remind me later” postpones the question.

Why a question instead of a fixed watering day? The University of Maryland Extension says indoor plants should be watered when they need it rather than on a schedule, and the RHS advises checking that a pot needs water before watering. The University of Illinois Extension lists plant type, temperature, humidity and light among the things that change how often a plant needs water. The watering guide goes into more detail.

Feeding is just as gentle. MULA asks “A little nourishment for Momo?” with “Fed today” or “Later”. Fertilizer is optional, and feeding pauses in a plant’s resting season.

What are the Today, Garden and Settings tabs for?

MULA has three tabs and nothing more. Today shows the season, a short status such as “Everyone is cared for.”, and Upcoming Tasks: watering, feeding and seasonal checks. If a check is very late, an Emergency Care card gives one extra nudge, such as “Momo has waited 7 days for water.” You can change that threshold with “Emergency care after” in Settings; it starts at 7 days.

Garden shows every plant as a card with its picture. Tap one to see this season’s plan, its care history as a calendar and its Journal: “Little changes are worth keeping.” The free version keeps 5 photos per plant.

Settings holds your reminders, the seasons and the look of the app: Light Paper or Dark Forest, “Evening calm”, the paper texture, Quiet motion and a soft garden music track of birdsong.

How do I set the reminder time and quiet hours?

MULA’s reminders are local notifications scheduled by your iPhone, with no server behind them. They keep a simple promise: “Only when a plant needs you. Never at night, never more than once a day for the same plant.” In Settings you choose:

  • Remind me at: the time reminders arrive, 9:00 by default.
  • Quiet hours: a stretch of the day when MULA sends nothing.
  • Sound: Kalimba (the default), Wind chime, Water droplet, Singing bowl or the iPhone’s default sound.

You can answer on the notification itself, without opening the app: “Dry · I watered it”, “A little moist” or “Wet” for a soil check, “Fed today” or “Later” for feeding. Tapping it opens that plant. A reminder that groups three or more plants has no buttons. The widgets, Apple Watch and Siri guide covers the other ways to answer.

MULA tip

Pick a reminder time when you are usually at home and near your plants, such as early morning. With the pots right in front of you, a soil check takes a few seconds.

With “Follow the seasons automatically”, reminders shift when the season changes. If you live south of the equator, turn on Southern hemisphere in Settings (“Seasons are reversed”). The seasonal care guide explains the four-season plans.

What should I do after setting up MULA?

Let the first week run. Answer each soil check honestly, even when the answer is “Wet”: a skipped round is care too. Add a photo now and then, and the Journal will slowly show each plant changing with the seasons.

If a plant looks unwell, remember that MULA’s plans are starting points and the app cannot diagnose pests or diseases; a local garden centre can help with anything serious. Questions about the app itself are answered in the FAQ and on the support page.

Questions

Is MULA free?

Yes. MULA is free to download and has no ads. The free version keeps up to 10 plants and 5 photos per plant, with all reminders, soil checks, seasonal plans to view, the temperature check, small and Lock Screen widgets, the Apple Watch list and Siri. MULA Premium, Annual with a 1-week free trial or Lifetime, adds unlimited plants and extras such as plan editing, Vacation mode, the light meter and weather alerts.

Do I need an account to use MULA?

No. MULA has no account and no server of its own. Your plants, care history, photos and settings are stored on your iPhone, shared with MULA’s own widgets and Apple Watch app, and backed up and synced through your own iCloud.

What if my plant is not in MULA’s list?

Add it with the “General care plan”. MULA’s catalog has 20 species, and any other plant can be added this way. You can also try “Not sure what it is?”, which suggests a plant kind from a photo, entirely on your iPhone.

What happens when I answer “A little moist”?

Nothing is logged and MULA asks again in two days. A watering is only recorded when you say the soil was dry, so the plan follows what the soil tells you rather than the calendar.
