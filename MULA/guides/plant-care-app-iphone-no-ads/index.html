Choosing an app

How to choose a plant care app for iPhone: a calm checklist

How to choose a plant care app for iPhone: soil checks over fixed schedules, seasonal plans, no ads or account, data on your device, and honest limits.

By Gül Eda Aydemir · Updated November 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer

Look for a plant care app that asks you to check the soil before it logs a watering, follows the seasons, shows no ads, needs no account, keeps your data on your iPhone and lets you answer reminders from a widget or your watch. MULA was made around those ideas and is free for up to 10 plants. It runs only on Apple devices (iPhone, iPad, Apple silicon Mac and Apple Watch), its interface is in English, it recognises plant kinds rather than exact species, and it cannot diagnose pests or diseases.

Key points

  • A good watering reminder asks about the soil instead of insisting on a fixed day.
  • Seasonal plans matter: many houseplants need less food, and often less water, in winter.
  • Check the App Store privacy section for tracking, and whether an account is required.
  • MULA has no ads, no account and no tracking, and keeps your garden on your iPhone.
  • Know the limits: Apple devices only, English interface, no pest or disease diagnosis.

What should I look for in a plant care app?

A plant care app should make looking after plants calmer, not noisier. Before you download one, it helps to know what you want it to do and what you would rather it didn’t. Here is a short checklist that works for any app, followed by honest notes on how MULA, the app this site is about, fits it and where it does not.

Should a plant app tell me when to water, or ask?

MULA’s maker started from a simple observation: many plant apps tell you to water on a fixed day, and that is exactly how many houseplants end up overwatered. Horticultural advice points the same way. The University of Maryland Extension says indoor plants should not be watered on a schedule, because a schedule can give them too much or too little water. The University of Illinois Extension recommends testing the soil with your finger first, and the RHS puts it plainly: don’t water routinely, check that the pot needs water.

So a good reminder is really a question. MULA’s is “How does Momo’s soil feel?” Answer “Dry” and tap “I watered Momo” to log the watering. “A little moist” asks again in two days, “Wet” skips the round, and “Not sure” shows how to check with a finger. A watering is only recorded when you say the soil was dry, whether you answer in the app, on a notification, on a widget, on Apple Watch or with Siri. The watering guide covers the soil check itself.

Does the app adjust care with the seasons?

Many houseplants grow more slowly when the days are short and cool. The RHS advises feeding houseplants mainly in the growing season and limiting feeding in winter, and notes that in a cool room many can go a week or two without water in winter. An app with one number for the whole year will be wrong for part of it.

In MULA, every plant has a four-season plan with a watering interval, a feeding interval (or “Resting”), a short tip for each season and a temperature range. With “Follow the seasons automatically”, reminders shift when the season changes, and a Southern hemisphere switch reverses the seasons. Viewing the plans is free; editing the numbers is part of MULA Premium. The plans are starting points, not botanical rules, which is why you can change them. The seasonal care guide explains them in detail.

Is there a plant care app with no ads and no account?

Before installing any app, open its App Store page and scroll to the privacy section. Apple’s support article explains the categories you will find there, such as “Data Used to Track You”, meaning data combined with other companies’ data to show you ads or shared with a data broker, and “Data Linked to You”. It is also worth noticing whether an app asks you to sign up before you can add a single plant.

MULA has no ads, ever, no account and no tracking. It has no server of its own: your plants, care history, photos and settings are stored on your iPhone, shared with MULA’s own widgets and Apple Watch app, and backed up and synced through your own iCloud, which only you can see. Plant identification and the light meter run on the device too. The optional weather alerts (Premium) send only a location rounded to about 1 km to Apple Weather. MULA also sends anonymous usage statistics to Google Analytics for Firebase, such as which screens are opened, never your plants’ names, notes or photos and without the advertising ID; you can turn them off in Settings → Your garden → “Share anonymous usage”.

Without an account, your garden still travels with you. MULA backs it up to your own iCloud, photos included, so it can come back on a new iPhone or after the app is deleted, and “Sync with iCloud” keeps every device signed in to your Apple Account showing the same plants. With Premium you can also share the garden with a partner or flatmate. The privacy page has the details.

Can I answer reminders from a widget or my Apple Watch?

The easier a reminder is to answer, the less it feels like a chore. Look for notification buttons, widgets and watch support, and check which of them are free.

In MULA, reminders arrive at a time you choose, stay silent in quiet hours, never come at night and never more than once a day for the same plant. Notifications carry the buttons “Dry · I watered it”, “A little moist” and “Wet”. The small and Lock Screen widgets, the Apple Watch list and Siri are free; answer buttons on the medium and large widgets and on Apple Watch come with Premium. The widgets, Apple Watch and Siri guide has the details.

What doesn’t MULA do?

No app suits everyone, and it is better to know the limits before you start.

  • Apple devices only. MULA needs iOS 17 or later on iPhone or iPadOS 17 or later on iPad, also runs on Macs with Apple silicon, and has an Apple Watch app (watchOS 10 or later). There is no Android version.
  • English interface. The app is currently in English; this website is in English, Turkish and Spanish.
  • Kinds, not species. “Not sure what it is?” uses Apple’s on-device image classifier and recognises about 20 plant kinds, such as orchid, rose, cactus, fern or palm, not exact species. When it is not confident, it says so and you choose.
  • No diagnosis. MULA cannot diagnose pests or diseases, and its plans are not professional horticultural advice.
  • Estimates, not instruments. The light meter (Premium) gives a rough reading in lux from the camera’s exposure, not a lab measurement.
  • A small free garden. The free version keeps up to 10 plants and 5 photos per plant.

How do free and paid plant care apps compare?

Look at what the free version really includes and how the paid one is billed. A subscription renews until you cancel it; on iPhone you can see and cancel it in Settings by tapping your name, then Subscriptions, as Apple’s support article explains. A free trial should say clearly how long it lasts.

MULA is free to download. The free version includes all reminders and soil checks, viewing the seasonal plans, the temperature check for balcony and outdoor plants, the small and Lock Screen widgets, the Apple Watch list and Siri. MULA Premium is Annual, with a 1-week free trial and yearly renewal until you cancel, or Lifetime, a single purchase. It adds unlimited plants and portraits, plan editing, the full photo journal and posters, Vacation mode and care sheets, the light meter, frost and heat alerts, and answer buttons on the medium and large widgets and on Apple Watch. Prices are shown in the App Store for your country.

MULA tip

Try any plant app with two or three plants for a couple of weeks before adding your whole windowsill. You will soon know whether its reminders feel like help or like noise.

Questions

Is there a plant care app for iPhone without ads?

Yes. MULA, for example, has no ads, ever, needs no account and does not track you; it only sends anonymous usage statistics, which you can turn off. For any app, the privacy section on its App Store page shows whether it collects data used to track you.

Does a plant watering app need an account?

Not necessarily. MULA has no account and no server of its own; your garden is stored on your iPhone and backed up and synced through your own iCloud, using the Apple Account you are already signed in with.

Can MULA identify the exact species of my plant?

No. “Not sure what it is?” uses Apple’s on-device image classifier to recognise about 20 plant kinds, such as orchid, rose or fern, not exact species. When it is not confident it says so, and you choose the species yourself. The photo never leaves your phone.

Can MULA tell me what is wrong with my plant?

No. MULA cannot diagnose pests or diseases, and its care plans are starting points rather than professional horticultural advice. For a plant that looks unwell, a local garden centre or extension service is a better place to ask.

Is MULA available on iPad or Mac?

Yes. The same app runs on iPhone (iOS 17 or later), iPad (iPadOS 17 or later) and Macs with Apple silicon, and comes with an Apple Watch app (watchOS 10 or later). With “Sync with iCloud” on, every device shows the same garden. The light meter needs a back camera, so it is not available on a Mac.
