Widgets, Watch and Siri

Answer plant watering reminders without opening the app: notifications, widgets, Apple Watch and Siri

Answer MULA’s plant watering reminders without opening the app: notification buttons, Home and Lock Screen widgets, Apple Watch and Siri, free or Premium.

By Gül Eda Aydemir · Updated November 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer

In MULA you can answer a plant’s soil check without opening the app: tap “Dry · I watered it”, “A little moist” or “Wet” on the notification, use a widget, glance at your Apple Watch or ask Siri, for example “Soil check for Momo in MULA”. Notification buttons, the small and Lock Screen widgets, the Apple Watch list and Siri are free; answer buttons on the medium and large widgets and on Apple Watch come with MULA Premium. Wherever you answer, a watering is only logged when you say the soil was dry.

Key points

  • Notification buttons let you answer “Dry · I watered it”, “A little moist” or “Wet” without opening MULA.
  • The small widget and the Lock Screen widgets are free; answer buttons on the medium and large widgets are Premium.
  • The Apple Watch app lists the plants that need you for free; answering from the wrist is Premium.
  • Siri works with no setup, for example “What needs care in MULA” or “I watered Momo in MULA”.
  • Everywhere, a watering is only logged when you say the soil was dry.

Can I answer plant reminders without opening the app?

Yes. MULA is built around one short question, “How does Momo’s soil feel?”, and you can answer it in several places: on the notification, on a widget, on your Apple Watch or by asking Siri. The rule is the same everywhere. A watering is only logged when you say the soil was dry; “A little moist” means MULA asks again in two days, and “Wet” skips this round. Which one you use depends on what is closest at hand: your phone, your watch or just your voice.

How do I answer a MULA reminder from the notification?

MULA’s reminders are local notifications from your iPhone. They arrive at the time you set with “Remind me at” in Settings, stay silent during your “Quiet hours”, and come only when a plant needs you: never at night, and never more than once a day for the same plant. For the sound, you can pick Kalimba, Wind chime, Water droplet, Singing bowl or the iPhone’s default; the calm ones are MULA’s own.

Touch and hold a notification to see its buttons; Apple’s iPhone User Guide describes this as the way to reach an app’s quick actions. MULA offers:

  • For a soil check: “Dry · I watered it”, “A little moist” or “Wet”.
  • For feeding: “Fed today” or “Later”.

These buttons are part of the free version, and your answer is saved without opening the app. If you would rather look first, tap the notification and MULA opens that plant. A reminder that combines three or more plants has no buttons; tap it and answer each plant in the app.

Which MULA widgets are free?

MULA has four kinds of widget for the Home Screen and the Lock Screen.

  • Small (free): the next plant. Tap it to open that plant’s soil check.
  • Lock Screen, circular, rectangular and inline (free): how many plants are waiting and the first one’s name.
  • Medium: the plant and its question. The Dry, Moist and Wet buttons are part of MULA Premium; without it, the widget shows “Answer here with Premium”.
  • Large: today’s four tasks and the upcoming checks, with buttons on each row that are Premium.

With a Lock Screen widget, you can see whether a plant is waiting before you even unlock your phone. Widgets read from the same place as the app. Your plants and care history are stored on your iPhone, in a container shared with MULA’s own widgets and its Apple Watch app, and backed up and synced through your own iCloud. There is no MULA server and no account.

How do I add a plant widget to my Home Screen or Lock Screen?

MULA’s widgets are added like any other. Apple’s support article on widgets gives these steps for the Home Screen:

  1. Touch and hold a widget or an empty area until the apps jiggle, then tap Edit in the upper-left corner.
  2. Tap Add Widget, choose MULA and a size, then tap Add Widget again.
  3. Tap Done.

For the Lock Screen, touch and hold it until Customize appears and tap it. Tap Add Widgets, tap or drag MULA’s widget into place, then tap Done. Wording can change between iOS versions, so if your screen looks different, Apple’s guide has the current steps.

MULA tip

Add the inline Lock Screen widget, and keep the small widget on the first page of your Home Screen. You see a waiting plant at a glance, and one tap on the small widget opens its soil check.

How does the MULA Apple Watch app work?

MULA’s Apple Watch app (watchOS 10 or later) lists the plants that need you, and that list is free. With MULA Premium you can also answer from your wrist: Dry, Moist or Wet for a soil check, Fed or Later for feeding. With Premium, you can answer while standing by the pots without taking your phone out. If your iPhone is not nearby, the answer waits and is applied when it is.

Watch-face complications show how many plants are waiting and the first plant’s name. Apple’s Apple Watch User Guide describes how to add one: with the watch face showing, touch and hold the display, tap Edit, swipe left all the way to the end, tap a complication and turn the Digital Crown to choose MULA, then press the Digital Crown to save.

What can I ask Siri in MULA?

MULA works with Siri and Shortcuts with no setup, and it is free. The phrases are in English:

  • “I watered Momo in MULA” or “Log watering in MULA”: logs a watering and tells you the next check day.
  • “What needs care in MULA”: reads today’s soil checks and feedings.
  • “Soil check for Momo in MULA”: asks how the soil feels; only an answer of dry logs a watering.
  • “I fed Momo in MULA”: logs a feeding.

Siri recognises a plant by its nickname, its species or its common name, so you can use whichever name comes naturally. Logging by voice is handy when your hands are covered in soil or holding a watering can. Apple’s Shortcuts User Guide explains more generally how Siri runs a shortcut when you say its name and then tells you the result.

Is answering from a widget, watch or Siri the same as in the app?

Yes. MULA uses the same rule on Today, in notifications, in widgets, on Apple Watch and with Siri, so a soil check means the same thing wherever you answer it. “Dry” logs a watering and plans the next check from today. “A little moist” logs nothing and asks again in two days. “Wet” logs nothing and skips the round. Logged waterings end up in the plant’s care history in the app, wherever you answered.

The moment matters more than the place: answer once your finger has actually been in the soil. If you are not sure how dry is dry, the watering guide explains how to check, and the quick-start guide covers the reminder time and quiet hours. Going away? Vacation mode (Premium) pauses reminders or keeps them on for a helper, and never marks anything as done for you.

Questions

Are MULA’s widgets free?

The small widget and the Lock Screen widgets (circular, rectangular and inline) are free. The answer buttons on the medium and large widgets are part of MULA Premium; without Premium, the medium widget shows “Answer here with Premium”.

Can I answer soil checks on my Apple Watch?

The MULA Apple Watch app lists the plants that need you for free. Answering Dry, Moist or Wet, or Fed or Later, from the wrist is part of MULA Premium. If your iPhone is not nearby, the answer waits and is applied when it is.

What do I say to Siri to log a watering in MULA?

Say “I watered Momo in MULA” or “Log watering in MULA”, using your plant’s nickname, species or common name. Siri logs the watering and tells you the next check day. No setup is needed, it is free, and the phrases are in English.

Why does a MULA notification have no buttons?

A reminder that combines three or more plants has no buttons. Tap it to open MULA and answer each plant there. Other soil-check reminders show “Dry · I watered it”, “A little moist” and “Wet” when you touch and hold them, and feeding reminders show “Fed today” and “Later”.
