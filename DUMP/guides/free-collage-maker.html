Free collage maker:
7 options, and the catch with each.

5 min read·Beginner

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Published 3 Nov 2026

Quick answer: For a free collage maker on iPhone, start with Google Photos for a one-tap grid, Apple Freeform for a loose scrapbook, or a dedicated collage app such as DUMP, PicCollage or PhotoGrid for layouts and frames. On a computer, Canva and Adobe Express are the usual picks, and both need an account. "Free" usually comes with a catch: a watermark, an ad, a locked template or an upload. Do one test export before you spend time on a layout.

Which free collage tools do you already have?

The iPhone's Photos app has no collage button, but Shortcuts and Freeform are free Apple apps that can do the job. If you use Google Photos, it can also make a quick collage.

Google Photos: select a few photos, tap Create (or Add to), choose Collage, and save the result. It takes seconds, but you have little control over the layout.

Apple Freeform: drop photos on a board, then drag, rotate and overlap them. It is the most flexible free option and suits a scrapbook look. The drawback is sizing, because it exports the board rather than a fixed canvas.

Apple Shortcuts: a small automation with Combine Images makes uniform grids. There are step-by-step instructions in how to make a collage on iPhone.

Looking for Instagram's old Layout app? Instagram discontinued Layout in May 2024 and moved its features into Stories, so apps with similar names today are from other developers.

Which collage apps have a usable free version?

DUMP, PicCollage and PhotoGrid are all free to download with optional paid upgrades. They differ in style: DUMP is built around carousels, PicCollage around playful cards, and PhotoGrid around many grid layouts.

DUMP has freeform collage pages and 28 grid layouts, edge-to-edge or spaced, with ratios for feed posts and Stories. It renders pages at 2160 px on the long edge, adds no ads or watermark on images, and needs no account. Its difference is that it can choose the photos for you. Some templates, frames and stickers are Premium.

What is the best free online collage maker?

Canva and Adobe Express are the usual choices on a computer because of their template libraries. Both ask you to sign in and upload your photos to the service.

A browser is the better option if you are on a laptop, you need text and graphics, or the collage is for print or a presentation. For personal photos, remember that an online maker normally means uploading them. If that bothers you, stick with a tool that works on your device.

What are the catches with free collage makers?

Watch for six things: watermarks, low-resolution exports, locked templates, ads, trial screens that become subscriptions, and uploads you did not expect.

  • Watermark: check the exported file, not the preview.
  • Resolution: for Instagram you want at least 1080 px wide.
  • Locked templates: the attractive ones are often the paid ones.
  • Trial screens: read what the button does before you tap. On iPhone you can see and cancel subscriptions in Settings.
  • Uploads and tracking: the App Privacy section on the App Store shows what an app says it collects.

How do you get a sharp collage?

Start from originals, export at 1080 px wide or more, and never screenshot the result. Every extra compression shows up in faces and skies.

A collage hides a problem: each photo appears smaller, but it also gets compressed along with everything else. Use camera originals rather than saved copies from messaging apps, choose the highest quality on export, and post that file. Set the ratio before you place photos, because changing it later crops them. For Instagram, 4:5 (1080 × 1350) uses more of the feed than a square. See the Instagram post size guide for every shape.

Which free collage maker should you pick?

Use the simplest tool that does what you need. Use Google Photos for a quick grid, Freeform for a scrapbook, Canva for a designed layout with text, and DUMP if you want the photos chosen for you.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free collage maker?

+

For a quick grid on a phone, Google Photos. For overlapping layouts on Apple devices, Freeform. For designed collages on a computer, Canva or Adobe Express. For having the photos chosen for you on iPhone, DUMP. The best one is the one whose free export meets your needs, so test it first.

Is there a free collage maker with no watermark?

+

Yes. Apple Freeform and Apple Shortcuts add no watermark, and DUMP adds none to exported images. Other apps vary, so check a test export.

Does iPhone have a built-in collage maker?

+

Not in the Photos app. The free Shortcuts and Freeform apps from Apple can both make collages.

Can I make a collage without downloading an app?

+

Yes. Browser tools such as Canva and Adobe Express work without an install, though both need an account and upload your photos.

What happened to Instagram's Layout app?

+

Instagram discontinued it in May 2024 and moved its features into the Stories tools in the main app. Apps named Layout today are from other developers.
