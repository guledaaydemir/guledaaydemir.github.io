How to post a photo dump on Instagram.

8 min read·Beginner

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Updated 15 Oct 2026

Quick answer: An Instagram photo dump is a carousel post of several casual photos and videos shared together. Pick 8 to 12, then open Instagram, tap + → Post, tap the Select Multiple icon, choose up to 20 items in the order you want them to appear, then edit, caption and share. The order you tap them is the order they post in, which is where dumps often go wrong.

A photo dump is the easiest post to make and the hardest to make well. The concept is casual — a handful of unpolished photos from a week, a trip, a night out — but the execution has real friction: scrolling months of camera roll, remembering which nine you picked, selecting them in the right order inside a picker of small thumbnails, and watching Instagram flatten your best shot on upload.

This guide covers the whole process: what to include, how to order it, the technical limits that actually apply in 2026, and how to stop quality loss before it happens.

What counts as a photo dump?

A photo dump is a carousel of loosely related photos posted as one unit instead of one curated hero shot. Most use 8 to 12 slides, mix photos with the odd video, and include at least one deliberately unimpressive frame.

The format caught on because it solves a real problem: most good photos aren't good enough to carry a post alone, but together they tell a story that a single image can't.

The unimpressive frame (a meal, a receipt, a blurry hand) is the point. A dump that looks art-directed stops being a dump and becomes a portfolio.

How many photos can you put in a photo dump?

Instagram allows a maximum of 20 photos or videos per carousel, with a minimum of 2. The limit was raised from 10 in August 2024 (TechCrunch), so a lot of advice online is still out of date.

The numbers that matter:

RuleValue
Maximum slides per carousel20 photos or videos
Minimum slides2 (a single image posts as a normal photo)
Mixing photos and videosAllowed, in any combination
Aspect ratioSet by slide one, applied to every following slide
Best portrait size1080 × 1350 px (4:5); 3:4 also works
Posting from other appsOnly through Meta's API, for professional accounts, up to 10 items
Editing after postingReorder or delete slides; you can't add one

Two of these are worth reading twice.

The first slide sets the crop for the entire carousel. If slide one is a square and slide two is a tall portrait, slide two gets cropped to square — top and bottom cut off. You can tap a slide to reposition it inside the crop, but it's easy to miss that the first slide decided the shape for all of them.

Twenty is the ceiling, not the target. 8 to 12 slides is enough to tell the story and short enough that people reach the end. Save the long carousels for a big trip or a year in review.

Step 1 — How many photos should you pick?

Pick 8 to 12, and fewer for a single evening. Instagram permits 20, but a tight dump people swipe all the way through beats a long one they abandon halfway.

The instinct is to include everything worth keeping. Resist it. A dump works because each slide earns its place; a dump of eighteen near-identical beach shots is a camera roll with extra steps.

A structure that consistently works:

  • Slide 1 — the hook. Your strongest image. It's the only one that appears in the feed, so it does all the work of earning a swipe.
  • Slides 2–4 — the story. The people, the place, the thing that happened.
  • Slides 5–7 — the texture. Food, hands, a sign, a mirror shot. The unglamorous frames that make it feel real.
  • Final slide — the punchline. The funny one, the blurry one, the screenshot. Give people a reason to reach the end.

If two photos say the same thing, keep one. Usually that's the sharpest, but sometimes the imperfect one wins: if it's funnier or more honest, keep it, because honesty is what the format trades on.

Step 2 — How do you control the order of photos?

Instagram posts photos in the order you tap them, not the order they appear in your library. Instagram lets you drag slides into a new order on the edit screen, but only as small thumbnails, so it's easier to fix the sequence before you open Instagram.

Here is the part that wastes the most time. The picker shows small thumbnails, so you're choosing the order before you can see it swipe. Since March 2026 you can also reorder slides after posting (Social Media Today), but it's easier to get it right the first time.

Two ways around it:

  1. Favorite as you go. Heart the photos you want during the week, then work from the Favorites album instead of scrolling months of camera roll.
  2. Build the sequence outside Instagram. Assemble and reorder the dump in a separate app, export it into a dedicated album, then select that album in Instagram and choose everything at once. The order is already correct, so there is nothing to get wrong.

Step 3 — Does Instagram reduce photo quality?

Yes. Instagram re-compresses every photo and scales anything wider than 1080 pixels down to 1080, and you can't switch that off. What you control is whether your photo is compressed once, from a clean original, or twice.

If your image was already re-compressed by another app, or is a screenshot, it takes that hit twice, and the second pass is where visible mush appears in gradients, skin tones and fine detail.

Practical steps:

  • Post originals. A clean camera original is ideal; a larger file is fine, because Instagram resizes it once.
  • Turn on Upload at highest quality. In Instagram: Settings and privacy → Data usage and media quality. It's off by default (Business Today).
  • Don't export as PNG. A much larger file gains nothing once Instagram re-compresses it.
  • Avoid apps that re-compress. Any editor that re-saves your photo before it reaches Instagram has already spent quality you can't get back.
  • Never post a screenshot of a photo. It's a re-capture at screen resolution, and it shows.

If you edited nothing and simply want the originals posted in order, the ideal tool touches the file as little as possible — ideally not at all.

Step 4 — How do you post a carousel on Instagram?

Tap +, choose Post, tap the Select Multiple icon, tap your photos in sequence, set the crop on slide one, add a caption and share. The full version, with the parts that matter:

  1. Open the composer. Tap + at the bottom of Instagram and choose Post.
  2. Switch to multi-select. Tap the stacked-squares icon labelled Select Multiple.
  3. Choose your album. Tap the album name at the top and pick the album holding your prepared dump, so you aren't hunting through your whole library.
  4. Select in order. Tap each item in the sequence you want. Numbers appear as you go — check them before continuing.
  5. Set the crop on slide one. Whatever ratio you choose here applies to all slides. 4:5 portrait is the safe default; 3:4 shows iPhone photos uncropped.
  6. Skip the filters. Filtering a dump defeats the format. If you want cohesion, it should come from the photos, not a preset.
  7. Write the caption, then share. Keep it short — a date, a place, a joke. Dumps rarely need paragraphs.

What are the most common photo dump mistakes?

Five common ones: leading with a weak first slide, mixing aspect ratios, using too many slides, selecting out of order, and uploading a screenshot or an already-compressed export.

  • Leading with your weakest photo. Slide one is the entire post as far as the feed is concerned.
  • Mixing aspect ratios. Slide one's crop is imposed on everything behind it.
  • Too many slides. A dump people finish beats a long one they abandon.
  • Selecting out of order and posting anyway. Since March 2026 you can reorder slides after publishing, but you still can't add one, so a forgotten photo means deleting and reposting.
  • Uploading a screenshot or a re-compressed export. Two rounds of compression is where "why does this look blurry" comes from.

Frequently asked questions

How many photos can you post in an Instagram carousel?

+

Up to 20 photos or videos, and at least 2. Instagram raised the limit from 10 in August 2024.

How many photos should a photo dump have?

+

8 to 12 for most dumps, and fewer for a single evening. Twenty is the ceiling, not the target.

Can I change the order after posting?

+

Yes, since March 2026: edit the post, then press and hold a slide and drag it. You can also delete a slide from a post that has three or more, but you can't add a new one to a published carousel.

Can DUMP post the carousel to Instagram for me?

+

No. Instagram only lets approved tools publish carousels to professional accounts through its API. DUMP has no account or server, so it saves the dump to a new Photos album in posting order and opens Instagram with your caption copied; the last tap is yours.

Why do my photos look worse after posting?

+

Instagram re-compresses every upload and scales anything wider than 1080 px down. A clean original survives that well; a screenshot or a copy another app already compressed is what turns soft and blotchy. Turning on Upload at highest quality also helps.
