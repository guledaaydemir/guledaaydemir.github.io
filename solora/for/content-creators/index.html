How to plan your week as a content creator, without burning out

I post on Instagram as @solo.stasera and build iPhone apps on my own. Content can swallow as much time as you give it, so here's a small system that treats it as one part of your life instead of all of it.

GABy Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer·Updated 3 Nov 2026·4 min read

Quick answer

Give content its own role with a small weekly target (for example, two posts), split each post into idea, shoot, edit and publish, and do similar steps in one batch. Keep only one content task in your daily top three, and decide now what your minimum post is for a bad week. A planner like Solora has a Creator role and creator habit templates for this. It plans your work, but it doesn't schedule or publish posts.

Why does content planning fall apart?

Usually because "post something" sits on your list as one giant task, competing with your job, family and everything else.

A post isn't one task. It's an idea, a shoot, an edit, a caption and a publish, and those need different energy. It's also unlimited work: you could always post more, so you never feel finished. A plan for creators has to do two things: break the work apart, and set a limit that lets you stop.

What's a simple weekly system for creators?

  1. Give content its own role.

    Add a Creator role alongside your others, such as Professional or Parent. Now you can see content competing with the rest of your life, instead of guessing.

  2. Set a target you could hit in a bad week.

    Two posts is better than a heroic seven you'll abandon by Thursday. You can always beat it, and the target is what tells you when to stop.

  3. Keep an ideas habit that takes one minute.

    Capture one idea a day, wherever you are. Ideas come in passing, and you don't want to start from zero on shoot day.

  4. Batch the same kind of work.

    Shoot once, edit once, write captions once. One good session with a couple of outfits can cover a week or more.

  5. One content task in your daily Big 3.

    On normal days, one step forward is enough: "edit carousel", not "do content".

  6. Choose your minimum post.

    For weeks when life wins: a story, a photo from the archive, a simple carousel. Decide now, while you're calm.

  7. Review for 15 minutes a week.

    What did you post, what worked, and what got squeezed to make it happen? The weekly review makes that visible.

What won't a planner do for creators?

Solora doesn't schedule or publish posts, connect to Instagram or TikTok, or show analytics. It's also for one person, so it's a poor fit for a team.

If you need a shared content calendar, a tool such as Notion or a dedicated scheduler will serve you better. Solora is for the question that comes before that: how do I fit this into my actual week?

Frequently asked questions

What is the best planner app for content creators?

It depends on the job. For fitting content into your whole week on iPhone, Solora has a Creator role, creator habit templates and a daily Big 3. For a shared content calendar, a flexible tool such as Notion or a dedicated scheduler is a better fit, since Solora doesn't schedule or publish posts.

How many times a week should a small creator post?

There's no universal number. Choose the amount you could keep up in a bad week, even if it's only two posts, and increase it once that feels easy. Consistency you can sustain beats a pace you drop after a week.

How do I plan content with a full-time job?

Keep the target small, batch shooting and editing into one or two sessions, and keep just one content task in your daily top three. Capture ideas in a one-minute daily habit so you never start from zero.

Can Solora post to Instagram or schedule content?

No. Solora plans the work with tasks, habits and projects, but it doesn't connect to social platforms, publish posts or show analytics.
