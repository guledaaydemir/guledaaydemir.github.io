How to plan a side project around a full-time job

I'm a computer engineer who builds iPhone apps alone, Solora included. A side project can easily vanish, or take over everything. The fix isn't more willpower. It's a smaller, clearer plan.

GABy Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer·Updated 3 Nov 2026·4 min read

Quick answer

Treat your job and your side project as separate roles, give the project a small weekly target you could hit in a bad week (such as two focused sessions), and always know the one next step. Put no more than one side project item in your daily top three on workdays. Then check once a week whether it's moving and what it's costing. Solora has Founder and Developer role templates, a Launch a Product project template, and a Weekly Review that shows when one role gets squeezed.

Why do side projects stall?

Mostly because there's no decision about when they happen, and because the next step is too vague to start when you're tired at 9 pm.

"Work on the app" can't be started after a full day. "Write the empty state for the settings screen" can. The same is true of the opposite failure: without a limit, a project you love quietly takes your sleep, your friends and your weekends.

What's a simple system for a side project?

  1. Separate the roles.

    Your employer's work and your own should never share a list. Besides making planning easier, it's a good boundary. Check your employment contract about outside work, too.

  2. Choose a small weekly target.

    Two focused sessions, not "as much as possible". Solora counts completed tasks against a role's weekly target, so you can see it land.

  3. Claim the time in advance.

    Tuesday evening and Saturday morning beats "when I have energy". Protect the slot, and let it be short.

  4. End each session by writing the next step.

    Stop with one specific, startable task. That makes the next session much easier to begin.

  5. One side project item a day, at most.

    On a workday, your Big 3 might be one work outcome, one thing for you or your family, and one small project step.

  6. Review for 15 minutes each week.

    Did it move? What did it cost? If your health or relationships paid for it, lower the target. See the weekly review.

What does a realistic week look like?

A sample week with a full-time job
DaySide projectNote
MonNoneJob first; write down ideas only
Tue45-minute sessionThe next step is already written
Wed–ThuNone or a tiny taskFamily, friends, exercise
FriNoneLeave work at work
Sat2-hour sessionDo the hardest step here
Sun15-minute reviewPick the next step for Tuesday

That's around three hours a week. It doesn't sound like much, but it adds up to over 150 hours a year, and you can keep it up.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours a week should I spend on a side project?

Choose an amount you could keep up in a bad week. Around three focused hours, split over two sessions, adds up to more than 150 hours a year, and it's more sustainable than bursts of late nights.

How do I work on a side project when I'm tired after work?

Finish each session by writing one specific next step so you don't have to decide anything later, and keep the first task small enough to start while tired. Save harder work for a weekend session.

What is a good app for planning a side project with a full-time job?

For keeping a side project in balance with your job and life on iPhone, Solora offers separate roles, weekly targets, a Launch a Product template and a Weekly Review. For bugs, sprints or teamwork, use a dedicated project tool too.

Can I work on my side project during my job?

Check your employment contract. Many employers have rules about outside work, intellectual property and using company equipment, so keep the two separate unless you've confirmed otherwise. This isn't legal advice.
