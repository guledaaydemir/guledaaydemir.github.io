Todoist alternatives for planning your personal life on iPhone

Todoist is a great task manager, and for many people it's enough. People usually start looking elsewhere for one of three reasons: the free limits, the subscription, or a list that never gets shorter. Each has a different answer.

GABy Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer·Updated 3 Nov 2026·5 min read

Quick answer

It depends on why you're leaving. Hit the free limits? Try TickTick, which has a generous free plan plus habits and a Pomodoro timer. Don't want a subscription? Things 3 is a one-time purchase, and GoodTask builds on Apple Reminders. Buried by an endless list? Solora groups life by role and has you choose three things a day. Stay with Todoist if you need team projects or work across Android, Windows and web.

What are Todoist's free limits?

Todoist's free Beginner plan allows 5 personal projects, 3 custom filters, 5 MB file uploads and one week of activity history. Custom reminders aren't included. Pro costs $7 a month, or $60 billed yearly.

That's reasonable value, so if the limits are the only problem, Pro may be cheaper than the time you'd spend moving. If the list itself is the problem, a paid plan won't change anything.

Which Todoist alternative has the best free plan?

TickTick is the strongest like-for-like free option: tasks, calendar views, habit goals and a Pomodoro timer in one synced app, across a very wide range of languages.

If you want your tasks on every device and you just want more app for the money, this is where I'd look first.

Is there a Todoist alternative without a subscription?

Yes. Things 3 is $9.99 once on iPhone, with its Mac and iPad versions sold separately, and it's organized around areas and projects. GoodTask is free with a $19.99 unlock and works on top of Apple Reminders and Calendar, so your tasks stay in Apple's apps.

Solora also has a one-time Lifetime purchase ($99.99), but its free plan is enough to find out whether the approach suits you first.

What if the problem is the list itself?

Then another list won't help. Most task managers treat "buy milk", "call mom" and "ship the report" the same way, so your job and your errands crowd out everything else without you noticing.

Solora starts from the roles you live: Parent, Student, Creator, Professional and so on, from 23 templates or your own. Each role can have a small weekly target. Each day you choose a Big 3, and everything else stays out of sight. The Weekly Review then shows which role got ignored, which a flat list can't tell you.

It isn't a Todoist replacement for everyone: there are no shared lists and no sync. It's for one person trying to balance a life rather than manage a queue. The method is explained in role-based planning.

What can't Solora do?

Solora is a personal planner for one person on iPhone and iPad, and it has real gaps.

  • No cloud sync between devices, no web app and no data export. Your data stays on the device and in its normal backup.
  • No shared or team lists, and no Apple Watch app or native Mac app.
  • The AI assistant needs iOS 26 and an Apple Intelligence device (iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 16 or later, or an M1 iPad or later) with Apple Intelligence turned on. Everything else works without it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free alternative to Todoist?

TickTick is the closest free alternative, with tasks, calendar views, habits and a Pomodoro timer. Solora is a different kind of free option, built around roles and a daily Big 3 instead of a long list.

Is there a Todoist alternative with a one-time purchase?

Yes. Things 3 costs $9.99 once on iPhone, and GoodTask has a $19.99 unlock. Solora offers a $99.99 Lifetime purchase, along with a free plan and monthly or yearly options.

Which Todoist alternative doesn't need an account?

Solora needs no account and its App Store label is Data Not Collected. GoodTask builds on Apple Reminders, so it uses the lists already on your device and in iCloud.

Can I import my Todoist tasks into Solora?

Solora has no importer. You can add tasks by typing or, on supported devices, ask its on-device assistant to create several from plain language. Starting small is the point, so I'd only bring what you need this week.
