Notion alternatives for planning your life on iPhone

A Notion life dashboard is satisfying to build and easy to abandon. If you've spent more time on the template than on your week, a planner that already has an opinion may serve you better.

GABy Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer·Updated 3 Nov 2026·5 min read

Quick answer

If you want a ready-made system instead of a template to maintain, choose Griply for goals that flow down to daily tasks and habits, Solora for roles, a daily Big 3 and habits with no account, Structured for a day on a timeline, or TickTick for a straightforward synced list. Keep Notion for documents, wikis, databases and anything shared. Many people do both, with Notion for reference and a phone app for today.

Why does a Notion life planner stop working?

Notion is a blank workspace, so you're both the user and the designer of your system. It's powerful, but every change in your life becomes a maintenance job.

Notion's own listing describes an "all-in-one workspace" for notes, tasks and projects, and it's free for personal use with thousands of templates. That's exactly why people love it. It isn't built to open on your phone in the morning and tell you what today is about unless you build that yourself, and checking off a habit in a database takes more taps than in an app designed for it.

If your dashboard works and you enjoy it, stay. This page is for people who've noticed they tinker more than they do.

Which alternative is best for goals?

Griply is the closest to a goal-tracking Notion template. Its listing describes a "golden thread" from long-term vision to today's tasks and habits.

Pick it if your dashboard was mostly about annual goals and you want to keep that top-down view, plus access from desktop and web.

Which alternative is best for juggling several parts of life?

Solora, if your dashboard's real job was keeping work, family and your own projects in balance. Those sections become roles, each with a weekly target.

  • Your "areas" database becomes roles, from 23 templates or your own.
  • Your "today" view becomes a Big 3 of 1, 3 or 5 items, and unfinished ones carry over.
  • Your habit table becomes habit tracking with heatmaps and 52 templates, including a flexible streak that forgives one missed day.
  • Your project pages become projects with steps, 9 templates (trip, move, launch and more) and a moodboard of images, notes, links and colors.
  • Your weekly review template is built in, and it counts for you.

What you lose is everything Notion is best at: free-form documents, linked databases, web access, sharing and exports. So keep Notion for those.

Can you use Notion and a planner together?

Yes, and it's often the sensible answer. Keep long-term reference material in Notion and let a phone app handle today.

Just don't copy tasks between them. Decide which app is the only place today's tasks live. Two sources of truth is how the original dashboard went stale.

What can't Solora do?

Solora is a personal planner for one person on iPhone and iPad, and it has real gaps.

  • No cloud sync between devices, no web app and no data export. Your data stays on the device and in its normal backup.
  • No shared or team lists, and no Apple Watch app or native Mac app.
  • The AI assistant needs iOS 26 and an Apple Intelligence device (iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 16 or later, or an M1 iPad or later) with Apple Intelligence turned on. Everything else works without it.

Frequently asked questions

Is Notion good for life planning?

It can be, if you enjoy building and maintaining your own system. Notion is free for personal use and has thousands of templates. If upkeep has become the problem, a ready-made planner such as Griply, Solora or Structured needs less setup.

What is a simpler alternative to Notion on iPhone?

For a personal planner, Solora gives you roles, a daily Big 3, habits and projects with no account or template to set up. TickTick is a simple option if you want a synced list, and Structured if you want a timeline.

Can Solora replace Notion?

For planning days, habits and personal projects, yes. For documents, databases, sharing and web access, no. Solora has no web app, no sync and no export, so keep Notion for those jobs.

Does Notion work without an account?

No. Notion needs an account. Solora doesn't: you can start planning right after installing it, and your data stays on the device.
