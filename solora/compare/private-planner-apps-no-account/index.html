How do you check what a planner app collects?

Open the app's App Store page and scroll to App Privacy. There are four kinds of label, from most to least data shared.

  • Data Used to Track You: data may be combined with other companies' data for advertising or shared with data brokers.
  • Data Linked to You: collected and tied to your identity.
  • Data Not Linked to You: collected but not tied to your identity.
  • Data Not Collected: the developer declares that it collects nothing.

These are summaries of Apple's definitions. The App Store itself notes that labels aren't verified by Apple, so treat them as a starting point and read the privacy policy for anything sensitive.

For contrast, among the big planners I checked for these comparisons, Todoist, TickTick, Notion, Sunsama and Akiflow all declare data linked to you, and Tiimo's label includes identifiers used to track you. That doesn't make them bad apps. Sync and collaboration need a server, and a server needs some of your data.

Why does no account usually mean no sync?

Syncing needs a copy of your data somewhere other than your phone. Without an account or server, there's nowhere to send it.

GoodTask gets around this by using Apple's Reminders and iCloud, and Things 3 runs its own sync service. Solora has no server of any kind, so your data lives on the device and in its normal backup. If you use an iPhone and an iPad, each has its own separate plan. That's the real price of this approach, and for some people it's a dealbreaker.

What does "Data Not Collected" mean in Solora?

There's no account, no server and no analytics or advertising SDK. The AI assistant and speech recognition run on the device.

Calendar access is optional. Solora only adds events if you turn on "Add tasks to calendar" and schedule AI breakdown steps. You can read the details in the privacy policy.

Because nothing leaves the device, I can't restore your data or see how you use the app. If you lose your phone without a backup, your plans are gone. Keep your iCloud or computer backup turned on.

Is paper the most private planner?

Yes, and it's a good choice. Everything in the Big 3 method and role-based planning works in a notebook.

An app earns its place by counting for you, carrying tasks over and sending reminders. If you don't need that, keep the notebook.

What can't Solora do?

Solora is a personal planner for one person on iPhone and iPad, and it has real gaps.

  • No cloud sync between devices, no web app and no data export. Your data stays on the device and in its normal backup.
  • No shared or team lists, and no Apple Watch app or native Mac app.
  • The AI assistant needs iOS 26 and an Apple Intelligence device (iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 16 or later, or an M1 iPad or later) with Apple Intelligence turned on. Everything else works without it.

Frequently asked questions

Which planner app doesn't require an account?

Solora needs no account at all. Apple Reminders uses your existing Apple Account, and GoodTask builds on Apple Reminders rather than an account of its own.

Which planner apps have the Data Not Collected label?

Solora shows Data Not Collected on the App Store. Among the planners I compared on 3 November 2026, it was the only one with that label. Labels are declared by developers and can change, so check the App Store page.

Is there a private AI planner?

Yes. Solora's AI runs on the device with Apple Intelligence, needs no account and works without internet. It requires iOS 26 and a supported device.

Can a private planner sync between iPhone and iPad?

Some can. GoodTask syncs through iCloud using Apple Reminders. Solora can't, because it has no server or account, so each device keeps its own data.

What happens to my Solora data if I delete the app?

Your data is stored on the device and in its normal backup, and there's no server copy. Deleting the app removes it from that device, so keep a device backup if you want to be able to restore it.
