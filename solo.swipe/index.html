The case file

What is Solo.Swipe?

Solo.Swipe is an iPhone photo cleaner and photo organizer designed around swipe-based review. Instead of tapping photos one by one in the Photos app's selection mode, Solo.Swipe shows you a single photo at a time as a card. You swipe left to mark it for deletion, right to keep it, up to add it to an album, and down to skip it and decide later.

You never have to clean the whole library at once. Solo.Swipe splits your camera roll into small, finishable sets: a specific day, month or year, an album, a location, screenshots, selfies, videos, panoramas, RAW or burst photos, your largest files, or a random batch of twenty. Smart Modes go further and surface photos worth a second look — blurry shots, photos where someone blinked, documents and receipts, food photos, pet photos and more — while a separate duplicates view groups near-identical photos so you can keep one and clear the rest.

It is built for people whose libraries got out of hand: anyone sitting on 10,000, 30,000 or 100,000 photos who wants to free up iPhone storage without spending a weekend on it. Daily goals and streaks turn the cleanup into a short habit, and Detective Raccoon keeps it from feeling like a chore.

The method

How Solo.Swipe Works

Solo.Swipe cleans an iPhone photo library in six steps.

  1. Pick a set of photos

    Choose a day, month, year, album, location, category or Smart Mode from the home screen. Each set is small enough to finish in one sitting.

  2. Review photos one at a time

    Photos appear as a card stack. Videos play in place and Live Photos animate, so you can judge each item properly before deciding.

  3. Swipe to keep, delete, file or skip

    Swipe right to keep, left to mark for deletion, up to add the photo to an album, and down to skip it without a decision.

  4. Let Smart Modes find the clutter

    Run a Smart Mode to pull out blurry photos, closed-eye shots, screenshots, documents or large files — or open the duplicates view to compare near-identical photos side by side.

  5. Confirm before anything is deleted

    At the end of a session Solo.Swipe shows everything you marked and asks you to confirm. Deletion goes through iOS, and deleted items land in Apple's Recently Deleted album for 30 days.

  6. See the storage you freed — and keep the streak

    Each session reports how many photos you handled and roughly how much space you recovered. Hit your daily goal to extend your cleaning streak and come back tomorrow.

The routine

The Camera Roll Reset

A Camera Roll Reset is a short routine for reviewing the photos, screenshots and videos that have accumulated on your phone and deciding what deserves to stay. It's the term we use at Solo.Swipe for a recurring cleanup session — five or ten minutes, one batch, a clear end point.

It works because a camera roll refills every week. A once-a-year purge buys a few months; a small routine keeps the library permanently manageable. There's a five-minute daily version, a Sunday version that sits inside a wider digital reset, a post-shoot version for content days, and a travel version for coming home with four hundred photos.

Confidential case file

Your Photos Stay Yours

Photos are the most personal thing on a phone — and for creators, often the most confidential. Here is exactly how Solo.Swipe handles them.

  • Photos are processed on your device. Solo.Swipe reads your library through Apple's PhotoKit framework and analyses images with Apple's on-device Vision framework.
  • Your photos are never uploaded. There is no Solo.Swipe photo server. Images are not copied off your iPhone, sent to a cloud service, or shared with third parties — including unpublished shoots and client work.
  • Deletion goes through iOS. When you confirm a cleanup, items move to Apple's Recently Deleted album, where you can restore them for 30 days before iOS removes them permanently.
  • App data stays local. Preferences, session statistics, streaks and album references are stored on your device and are removed when you delete the app.
  • Photo library access is optional and revocable. Solo.Swipe asks for photo access when you start, and you can change or withdraw it at any time in iOS Settings.

Solo.Swipe is free to download and the free version is supported by advertising delivered through Google AdMob. Ads are requested by Google's SDK and may involve device-level advertising data depending on the tracking permission you grant; your photos are never part of that. The optional paid upgrade removes ads. Full details are in the Solo.Swipe privacy policy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Solo.Swipe?

Solo.Swipe is an iPhone photo cleaner and organizer. It shows the photos in your library one at a time and lets you clear them out with swipe gestures instead of tapping through Apple's Photos app in selection mode.

How does Solo.Swipe clean iPhone photos?

You pick a group of photos — a day, a month, an album, a location, screenshots, videos, or a Smart Mode — and review them as a card stack. Swiping left marks a photo for deletion, swiping right keeps it, swiping up files it into an album, and swiping down skips it. At the end of the session Solo.Swipe asks you to confirm before anything is deleted.

Can I swipe to delete photos on iPhone?

Apple's built-in Photos app does not have a swipe-to-delete review mode, so you need a third-party app. Solo.Swipe adds that gesture: swipe a photo left to mark it for deletion and right to keep it. Deletion happens through Apple's system confirmation, and deleted items go to the Recently Deleted album.

Does Solo.Swipe find duplicate or unwanted photos?

Yes. Solo.Swipe groups likely duplicates and near-identical shots so you can pick one to keep, and its Smart Modes surface categories such as blurry photos, photos with closed eyes, screenshots, selfies, documents, food, pets and large files. The features page covers all of them.

Does Solo.Swipe upload my photos?

No. Photos and videos are read and analysed on your iPhone using Apple's PhotoKit and Vision frameworks. Solo.Swipe has no photo server: your images are not uploaded, copied off the device, or shared with third parties.

Can Solo.Swipe help free up iPhone storage?

Yes. Solo.Swipe shows how much space a cleaning session recovered and includes a storage breakdown plus a Large Files mode so you can start with the videos and photos taking up the most space. Space is reclaimed once the items leave the Recently Deleted album, which happens automatically after 30 days or immediately if you empty it.

Can I organize photos into albums with Solo.Swipe?

Yes. Swiping up during a session adds the current photo to an album you choose, so you can sort keepers while you clean instead of doing a second pass later.

Does Solo.Swipe work with large photo libraries?

Solo.Swipe is designed for large libraries. Photos load in batches rather than all at once, and you can break a big library into small sessions by day, month, year, album, location or category so you never have to face tens of thousands of photos in one sitting.

Is Solo.Swipe useful for content creators?

It can be. Content creation produces far more photos and videos than get published — multiple takes of one shot, reference screenshots, unused clips and edited versions saved alongside originals. Solo.Swipe gives creators a fast way to review that leftover material after a shoot and file the keepers into albums in the same pass. There's more detail on photo cleanup for content creators.

What is a Camera Roll Reset?

A Camera Roll Reset is a short routine for reviewing the photos, screenshots and videos that have accumulated on your phone and deciding what deserves to stay. It is deliberately small and repeatable — usually five to ten minutes — rather than an attempt to clean an entire library at once. Solo.Swipe uses the term to describe this kind of recurring photo cleanup session.

Can Solo.Swipe be part of a digital declutter or Sunday reset?

Yes. Solo.Swipe covers the camera roll portion of a wider digital decluttering routine, alongside steps like clearing your inbox, downloads folder, browser tabs and notifications. It does not organize anything outside your photo library.

Solo.Swipe is an iOS app for iPhone and is distributed through Apple's App Store.
