What Is System Data on iPhone? Can You Delete It?

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Updated 1 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

System Data (Apple's storage page also calls this category Other) holds things like Siri voices, fonts, dictionaries, logs, caches, the Spotlight index and system data such as Keychain. There's no delete button, and Apple says you don't need to take any action: your iPhone removes cached and temporary files itself when it needs space. To get room back, delete apps and content you don't need and follow the Recommendations in iPhone Storage.

You open iPhone Storage and there's a big grey block called System Data that you can't tap, can't open and didn't ask for. It's natural to want it gone. Here's what it actually is, what Apple says about it, and an honest list of what's worth trying.

What is System Data on iPhone?

It's the storage iOS uses for its own working files, as opposed to your apps and content. Apple's storage page describes this category, under the name Other, as “non-removable mobile assets, like Siri voices, fonts, dictionaries, non-removable logs and caches, Spotlight index, and system data, such as Keychain and CloudKit database”.

Row in the barWhat it isCan you reduce it?
System (iOS)The operating system itselfNo
System Data / OtherVoices, fonts, logs, caches, search index, KeychainNot directly; iOS manages it
Apps, Photos, Media, MessagesThings you installed or createdYes, and this is where to work

Apple's support pages use both “Other” and “System Data”; the label you see can depend on your iOS version.

Why is System Data so big on my iPhone?

Part of it is cache, which comes and goes. Apple says your device creates cached files when you stream or view content, so they load faster next time, and removes them when it needs more space. So the number can rise and fall without you doing anything.

Apple doesn't publish a normal size for this category, and iOS doesn't show what's inside it. Anyone quoting an exact “healthy” figure, or naming the one cause of your particular number, is guessing.

Can I delete System Data on my iPhone?

Not directly, and Apple says you don't have to. Its advice is plain: “If you see Other or System Data in the graph that shows your device storage, you don't need to take any action. Instead, delete apps and content that you don't need, and follow the recommendations.”

What's worth trying if System Data won't shrink?

Start with the free, safe steps, and leave erasing your iPhone for last. Apart from the first row, these are common things people try, not fixes Apple promises for System Data.

If it stays huge and is stopping you from updating or using your phone, contact Apple Support before you erase anything.

Can a cleaner app remove System Data?

No. Apple sandboxes all third-party apps, which is designed to stop them from gathering or modifying what other apps store or making changes to the device. An app from the App Store has no way to reach System Data, including mine. Be skeptical of anything promising otherwise, especially behind a paywall.

Questions

Is it safe to leave System Data alone?

Yes. Apple says you don't need to take any action when you see Other or System Data in the storage graph, and that your device removes cached and temporary files itself when it needs more space.

Does restarting an iPhone clear System Data?

It's harmless and worth a try, but Apple doesn't promise it will reduce System Data, and iOS doesn't show what's inside. Check the bar afterwards rather than assuming.

Will a factory reset remove System Data?

Erasing an iPhone removes everything on it, but Apple doesn't promise a specific result for this category after you restore your data. Treat it as a last resort, only with a verified backup.

Is System Data the same as iOS?

No. The System row is the operating system itself. System Data, or Other, is the separate block of voices, fonts, logs, caches and similar files.

Why can't I see what's inside System Data?

iPhone Storage lists how much space the category uses, but not its contents, and there's no setting to open it. iOS manages it for you.
