iCloud Storage Full? Free Space Without Losing Photos

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Updated 1 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Go to Settings › your name › iCloud to see what's using the space. Start with Recommended for You, delete backups of old devices, then remove photos, large files and message attachments you don't need. If you've cleaned up and still need more, iCloud+ is the honest fix. One catch: when you're over your limit, deleted photos skip Recently Deleted and can't be recovered, so copy anything you're unsure about first.

Every Apple Account starts with 5 GB of free iCloud storage, which doesn't go far once backups and photos are using it. When it runs out, it's tempting to either pay straight away or start deleting at random. You don't have to do either yet. First find out what's actually in there.

Is iCloud storage the same as iPhone storage?

No, and they fill up separately. iPhone storage is the space inside your phone. iCloud storage is space on Apple's servers that keeps your data backed up and in sync.

If your alert says iPhone Storage, go to iPhone storage full instead. Buying iCloud space won't fix that one by itself.

What happens when iCloud storage is full?

Backups and syncing stop. Apple lists what happens if you run out:

  • Your device won't back up to iCloud.
  • New photos and videos won't upload to iCloud Photos.
  • iCloud Drive and other iCloud apps won't stay up to date across your devices.
  • You won't be able to send or receive email with your iCloud address.

Deleting what's already stored isn't on that list. The real risk is quieter: with uploads and backups stopped, your newest photos may exist only on your phone. If you lost or broke it now, they'd be gone. That's the reason to fix this soon, not the alert itself.

What should I delete first to free iCloud storage?

Start with what you don't need at all: backups of old devices, then large files, then photos.

  1. See what's using it

    Open Settings › your name › iCloud. Tap Storage or Manage Account Storage for the breakdown. This decides everything else: there's no point cleaning photos if Backups is the biggest row.

  2. Check Recommended for You

    On iOS 17 or later, tap Recommended for You in iCloud settings. It offers things you might not need anymore, like photos, large files or old backups.

  3. Delete backups of devices you no longer use

    Go to Storage or Manage Account Storage, then Backups, tap the old device, then Turn Off and Delete from iCloud. Only do this for devices you're sure about. Deleting a backup also turns off iCloud Backup for that device.

  4. Slim down this iPhone's backup

    In the same list, tap this iPhone and turn off apps you don't need backed up. Apple says this removes that app's information from iCloud, and that some apps are always backed up.

  5. Clean out photos and videos

    If iCloud Photos is on, deleting from the Photos app frees iCloud space. Long videos are the fastest win. Remember it deletes on every device using iCloud Photos, and read the catch below first.

  6. Clear iCloud Drive and message attachments

    In the Files app, go to Browse › iCloud Drive and delete large files you don't need. In a Messages conversation, tap the contact's name, then Photos, to delete big attachments.

The catch: over your limit, deleted photos can't be recovered

Normally a deleted photo waits in Recently Deleted for 30 days. Not when you're over your limit. Apple's wording: “If you exceed your iCloud storage limit, your device immediately removes any photos and videos you delete and they won't be available for recovery in your Recently Deleted album.”

So while you're over the limit, treat every photo deletion as final. Clear backups and files first, copy anything you're unsure about to a computer, and check your selection twice.

What if I don't use iCloud Photos?

Then your photo library is part of your iCloud backup, and it's often the biggest thing in it. To check, go to Backups, tap your device and look at Photo Library.

Apple's options are to save your photos and videos to a computer and then back up manually, turn off Photos in the backup, or upgrade to iCloud+. If you turn it off without another copy, those photos exist only on your iPhone.

Does turning off iCloud Photos free iCloud space?

Not if you only turn it off on one device. That just stops that device syncing. Turning it off for your whole account is different: Apple says your photos delete from iCloud 30 days after you stop using the service.

So that route does free space, but only by removing your library from iCloud. Download your originals first, and make sure they fit where you're saving them.

Should I just buy iCloud+?

If you want everything kept in iCloud and you've already removed the junk, yes. That's what it's for. Cleaning first may let you pick a smaller plan. Check the plans and local prices in iCloud settings, since they vary by country.

One thing iCloud+ doesn't change: iCloud Photos is sync, and Apple says to keep backup copies of your library, because your photos aren't duplicated in your iCloud backup.

Questions

Will I lose my photos if iCloud storage is full?

Apple's list of what stops doesn't include deleting what's already stored. But new photos won't upload, and backups stop, so your newest photos may exist only on your iPhone until you free space or upgrade.

Does deleting an iCloud backup delete my photos?

With iCloud Photos on, no: Apple says your photos aren't duplicated in your iCloud backup. Without iCloud Photos, your library is part of the backup, so deleting it removes that copy, while the photos on your iPhone stay put. Make sure you have another copy first.

Why is iCloud still full after I deleted photos?

Check the breakdown: the biggest row is often Backups, not Photos. If iCloud Photos is off, photos you deleted can remain in your last backup until a new one runs.

How do I stop the “iCloud storage is full” alert?

Apple gives two options: manage your storage or buy more. Getting usage back under your limit with the steps above is the free route.

How much free iCloud storage do I get?

Apple gives you 5 GB automatically when you set up iCloud. Anything beyond that needs an iCloud+ plan.
