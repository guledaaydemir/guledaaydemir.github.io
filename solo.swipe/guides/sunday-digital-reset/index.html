A Simple Sunday Digital Reset Routine

By Gül Eda Aydemir · Updated September 2026

A Sunday reset is a short weekly routine for tidying up and setting yourself up for the week ahead. The digital version applies that to the things that quietly accumulate all week — unread email, downloads, photos, browser tabs, notifications — so Monday doesn't start inside a mess you made on Thursday.

The whole routine below takes about half an hour, and it works because each step is small and the week's backlog is small. This is maintenance, not a project. If you're starting from a serious backlog, do the one-off pass in the iPhone declutter guide first, then use this to keep it from coming back.

The routine

  1. Clear the inbox
  2. Review downloads
  3. Clean your camera roll
  4. Close unnecessary tabs
  5. Clean the home screen and desktop
  6. Review notifications
  7. Plan next week's digital priorities

  1. Clear the inbox 5 min

    Not inbox zero — just get it manageable. Archive or delete everything that needs no action from you, which on a normal week is most of it. Anything that does need a reply and will take under two minutes, reply now; anything longer goes on next week's list in step 7.

    Then do one small structural thing: unsubscribe from a single newsletter you never open. One a week is painless and compounds fast — after two months the inbox is receiving noticeably less.

  2. Review downloads 3 min

    Open the Files app on iPhone, or the Downloads folder on a computer, and look at the past week. Boarding passes for flights already taken, receipts, a PDF you opened once, three versions of an attachment someone emailed.

    Delete the expired, move the two or three worth keeping somewhere sensible. This step takes three minutes weekly and prevents the folder from ever becoming a thousand-item archaeological dig.

  3. Clean your camera roll 5–10 min

    The camera roll refills faster than almost anything else on a phone, which is exactly why it belongs in a weekly routine rather than an annual purge. A week's worth of photos is a manageable batch; a year's worth is not.

    Review only the last seven days. Delete the duplicate takes, the blurry shots, and the screenshots whose purpose has expired — the address you already navigated to, the joke you already sent, the receipt you already filed. File anything worth finding again into an album while you're looking at it.

    If your camera roll accumulates dozens of temporary photos during the week, Solo.Swipe can make this the fastest step rather than the one you skip: it shows each photo full-screen as a card and turns the decision into a single swipe — left to delete, right to keep, up to file into an album. The Camera Roll Reset is the same step written out in more detail.

  4. Close unnecessary tabs 2 min

    Open tabs are a to-do list you never agreed to. Skim what's there, save the two or three genuinely useful pages to bookmarks or a note, and close everything else in one action.

    Then make it structural: in Safari, Settings › Apps › Safari › Close Tabs lets you close tabs automatically after a week. Once that's set, this step mostly takes care of itself.

  5. Clean the home screen and desktop 5 min

    On the phone: remove anything you installed this week and didn't use, and check that your first home screen still matches what you actually open. Apps you use twice a year belong in the App Library, which is searchable.

    On a computer, this is the desktop-files step. Screenshots, downloads dragged out of a browser, a document called final_v3. File them, or delete them. A desktop with fewer than a dozen items is a reasonable target.

  6. Review notifications 3 min

    Ask one question: what interrupted me this week that shouldn't have? Then turn that app's notifications off, or move it into a Scheduled Summary so its alerts arrive in a batch instead of mid-conversation.

    Doing one or two a week is more sustainable than a single overhaul, and after a couple of months the only things that interrupt you are the things you chose.

  7. Plan next week's digital priorities 5 min

    End by looking forward. Open the calendar for the coming week, check what's actually scheduled, and write down the two or three things that genuinely need to happen — including whatever you deferred in step 1.

    Keep the list short enough to be real. Three priorities you finish beats twelve you carry into the following Sunday.

The ten-minute version

When the week has run over, do only these three. They capture most of the benefit:

  • Clear the inbox of everything that needs no action (5 min)
  • Review the week's photos and screenshots (3 min)
  • Write down next week's two or three priorities (2 min)

A short reset you actually do is worth more than a thorough one you skip.

Why the weekly version works

Each of these areas refills at its own rate, and all of them refill faster than most people clean them. That mismatch is the entire problem — not disorganisation, just arithmetic.

A weekly cadence fixes the arithmetic. It also makes the work trivially easy: deciding about seven days of photos is a different task from deciding about seven years of them, because you still remember the context. Recent decisions are fast decisions.

Two things make it stick. Attach it to something that already happens — Sunday coffee, laundry, meal prep, the evening before the week starts. A routine without a trigger doesn't survive a busy week. And keep the steps smaller than feels sufficient, because finishing is what makes you come back. A reset you finish in twenty minutes gets repeated; one that sprawls into ninety does not.

If you make content

People who shoot for work need a heavier version of step 3. A content day can add several hundred photos in an afternoon — takes, references, screenshots, unused clips — which is more than a weekly pass comfortably absorbs.

The usual fix is a short post-shoot review in addition to the Sunday one: clear the obvious rejects while you still remember which take you chose, then let the weekly reset handle everyday accumulation. That split is covered in photo cleanup for content creators.

Common questions

What is a Sunday reset?

A Sunday reset is a short weekly routine for tidying up and preparing for the week ahead. A digital Sunday reset applies the same idea to phones and computers: clearing the inbox, downloads, camera roll, browser tabs and notifications, then setting priorities for the coming week.

How long should a digital reset take?

About thirty minutes is enough for a weekly reset once you have done it a few times. The first one takes longer because there is a backlog. If thirty minutes is too much, a ten-minute version covering inbox, camera roll and tabs captures most of the benefit.

Does it have to be Sunday?

No. The day matters far less than the consistency. Friday afternoon works well for people who want to close out a work week, and Monday morning suits people who prefer to start fresh. What matters is attaching the reset to something that already happens every week.

Can photo cleanup be part of a Sunday reset?

Yes. The camera roll refills faster than almost anything else on a phone, which makes it a natural weekly step. Reviewing just the past week's photos and screenshots takes five to ten minutes and prevents a backlog from forming.
