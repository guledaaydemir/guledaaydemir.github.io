4-7-8 breathing for sleep: how to do it properly

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Updated 15 Oct 2026 · 6 min read

Quick answer

Exhale fully. Inhale through your nose for four seconds, hold for seven, then exhale slowly through your mouth for eight. Start with four cycles (76 seconds); some teachers suggest building to eight as it gets comfortable. Keep the inhale small — about seventy percent of capacity — because a full inhale before a seven-second hold is what makes people dizzy and conclude the technique doesn't work.

How do you do 4-7-8 breathing?

In through your nose for 4, hold for 7, out through your mouth for 8. One cycle takes 19 seconds. Here it is step by step:

  1. Empty your lungsOne full exhale through the mouth before the first cycle. Skipping this is why the first inhale often feels like a gulp.
  2. Inhale for fourThrough the nose, quietly, belly rising. Aim for about seventy percent full, not maximum.
  3. Hold for sevenShoulders down, jaw loose, no bracing. The hold should feel like pausing, not like gripping.
  4. Exhale for eightThrough the mouth, lips slightly pursed. Let the air leave; do not push it out.
  5. Repeat four to eight timesFour cycles take 76 seconds. Stop early if you feel lightheaded.

How many cycles of 4-7-8 should I do?

Four to start. Cleveland Clinic suggests three cycles twice a day, and four cycles take just 76 seconds. Some teachers suggest building up to eight once it feels easy. More isn’t better: the long hold makes this a demanding pattern.

What are the most common 4-7-8 mistakes?

  • Inhaling too deeply. A common error. A maximal inhale plus a seven-second hold produces air hunger and dizziness. Seventy percent is the target.
  • Rushing the count. Anxiety speeds up counting. If four cycles take you under a minute, your seconds have shrunk. Use a guide rather than your own count.
  • Forcing the exhale. Blowing air out hard recruits the muscles you are trying to relax. Release, don't expel.
  • Doing too many cycles. Eight is plenty. Twenty rounds of an effortful pattern is a workout, not a wind-down.
  • Watching a bright screen. If you use a timer in bed, dim it or close your eyes and follow taps or sound instead.

What if 4-7-8 doesn't suit me?

The seven-second hold is what makes this pattern distinctive, and also what makes it unsuitable for some people. If holding your breath triggers tension, panic, or a feeling of being unable to get enough air, do not persist with it — you will train exactly the wrong association.

Does 4-7-8 breathing actually work?

It can help you settle, but be wary of anyone who promises it will knock you out in 60 seconds. The evidence is for slow breathing in general rather than this exact count. A 2018 systematic review linked slow breathing (under ten breaths a minute) with calmer physiological and psychological signals, while noting the evidence is still limited. At 19 seconds per cycle, 4-7-8 is about three breaths a minute, so it sits comfortably inside that range.

What 4-7-8 adds is structure: a count long enough to occupy a busy mind, and an exhale twice as long as the inhale. What it doesn’t do is force sleep. Think of it as removing an obstacle (a revved-up body, a looping mind), not pressing an off switch.

When should I use 4-7-8?

  • In bed, lights off. The primary use. Run it once you are already lying in your sleeping position.
  • After waking at 3am. Especially when the problem is a mind that has switched on rather than a body that is alert. The 3am guide covers the rest of that night.
  • Before something stressful. It works outside bedtime too, though four cycles is enough — you want composed, not drowsy.
  • Not while driving or standing. Any breath-hold pattern should be done seated or lying down.

Common questions

How many rounds of 4-7-8 should I do?

Start with three or four cycles, about a minute; some teachers suggest building up to eight once it feels easy. Cleveland Clinic suggests three cycles twice a day. More isn’t better: the hold makes this a demanding pattern.

How long is one cycle of 4-7-8 breathing?

19 seconds: 4 in, 7 hold, 8 out. Four cycles take 76 seconds and eight take 2 minutes 32 seconds.

Why does 4-7-8 breathing make me dizzy?

Usually because the in-breath is too big for the long hold. Inhale to about seventy percent, or shorten the hold to four or five seconds. Cleveland Clinic notes that some light-headedness is common at first and eases with practice; stop if it doesn’t.

Does 4-7-8 breathing really work for sleep?

It can help you settle, but it doesn’t force sleep. The research supports slow breathing in general rather than this exact count, and 4-7-8 is a slow pattern at about three breaths a minute.

Is it seconds or counts?

Seconds, but the ratio matters most. If eight seconds strains, halve everything and keep the proportions. A strained count raises tension and defeats the purpose.
