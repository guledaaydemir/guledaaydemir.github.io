How many breaths per minute is normal? Respiratory rate explained

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Updated 3 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

A normal respiratory rate for a resting adult is 12 to 18 breaths per minute, according to Cleveland Clinic, which adds that under 12 or over 25 at rest may point to an underlying health condition. Children breathe faster: the median is about 44 breaths a minute at birth, falling to about 26 by age 2. To count your own, rest, then count your breaths for a full minute. Slow breathing exercises deliberately go lower for a few minutes, and that’s a different thing.

First, a safety check

If you have severe trouble breathing, a tight or heavy chest, pain spreading to your arms, back, neck or jaw, very pale, blue or grey lips or skin, or sudden confusion, call your local emergency number now (999 in the UK, 911 in the US, 112 in the EU and Türkiye). Breathing exercises are not a treatment and not a substitute for medical help.

What is a normal respiratory rate?

Your respiratory rate is how many breaths you take in one minute, counting an in-breath and out-breath as one. It’s one of the four main vital signs, alongside temperature, pulse and blood pressure.

For a resting adult, it’s 12 to 18 breaths per minute. Work that out over a day (1,440 minutes) and you get roughly 17,000 to 26,000 breaths, almost all of which you didn’t notice.

How do you check your breathing rate?

  1. Rest firstSit quietly for a few minutes. Right after climbing stairs is not your resting rate.
  2. Set a 60-second timerIt’s tempting to count 15 seconds and multiply, but a full minute is more accurate.
  3. Count each riseWatch or feel your chest or belly. One rise equals one breath.
  4. Try not to control itThis is the hard part. As soon as you notice your breath, you’ll want to steer it. Getting someone else to count helps.

What is a normal respiratory rate for children?

It’s higher, and it falls quickly in the early years. A large Lancet review of children’s vital signs found a median of 44 breaths per minute at birth, down to 26 at age 2, with the rate continuing to fall into early adolescence.

Published charts vary, so we’re not reproducing a table of “normal” bands for each age. If you’re worried about a child’s breathing, look at how they’re breathing as well as the number, and ask a doctor. Grunting, sucking in under the ribs, or blue or grey lips or skin mean you should call your emergency number.

When is breathing too fast or too slow?

Cleveland Clinic flags a resting rate under 12 or over 25 as possibly linked to an underlying condition. A single number isn’t a diagnosis. Fever, exercise, anxiety and many other things change your rate. But pay attention to the whole picture:

  • You’re more short of breath than usual, or struggling to get words out
  • You have tightness or pain in your chest
  • Your lips or skin look pale, blue or grey
  • You feel suddenly confused

Any of those: treat it as urgent. There’s more in the safety note at the top of this page.

Is breathing six times a minute bad for you?

Done on purpose for a few minutes, if you feel comfortable, it’s what many breathing exercises involve. Slowing your breath deliberately while awake is very different from a resting rate that sits low with no effort, which is the kind doctors want to look at.

Researchers usually define slow breathing as under 10 breaths a minute, and a 2018 review linked it with higher heart-rate variability, while noting small, mixed studies. Stop if you feel dizzy or uncomfortable, and if you have a heart or lung condition, ask your doctor first.

Common questions

Is 20 breaths per minute normal?

It is a little above the 12 to 18 resting range. After activity, or with a fever or stress, that can be expected. If it is your resting rate and you feel unwell or short of breath, check with a doctor.

Is 10 breaths per minute too low?

It is under the usual resting range for adults. Doing it on purpose in a breathing exercise is different from having a resting rate that low without trying. If your resting rate is low and you feel unwell, talk to a doctor.

Does your respiratory rate change when you sleep?

Yes. It generally slows during sleep. Your brain keeps adjusting your breathing automatically.

Can an Apple Watch measure respiratory rate?

Apple Watch can record a respiratory rate during sleep through Apple’s own Health features. ATHA doesn’t measure your breathing: it only guides a rhythm.

Why does my breathing change when I think about it?

Breathing runs automatically, but you can override it. As soon as you pay attention, you start steering it. That’s why it’s more accurate if someone else counts for you.

Where does ATHA fit in?

When I built ATHA, I wanted people to be able to pick a rhythm without doing any of that maths. It’s why the app has moments like I need a moment and Slow down, and why you’re never asked to count. It doesn’t measure your breathing, so it can’t tell you your rate. For that, use a timer, or ask a doctor.
