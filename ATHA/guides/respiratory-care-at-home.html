Respiratory care: what it means, and daily lung care at home

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Updated 3 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

Respiratory care means two things. In a hospital or clinic, it’s the treatment given by respiratory therapists, who assess and treat people with breathing difficulties. At home, it means the daily habits that protect your lungs. The ones that matter most are not smoking, avoiding polluted air, staying active, getting recommended vaccines and getting symptoms checked early. Breathing exercises can make a useful addition, but they come after those.

First, a safety check

If you have severe trouble breathing, a tight or heavy chest, pain spreading to your arms, back, neck or jaw, very pale, blue or grey lips or skin, or sudden confusion, call your local emergency number now (999 in the UK, 911 in the US, 112 in the EU and Türkiye). Breathing exercises are not a treatment and not a substitute for medical help.

What does respiratory care mean?

Search for it and you’ll find job listings next to lung-health tips, because the term covers both. If you need help with a diagnosis, you want the clinical meaning. If you just want healthier lungs, you want the daily one. This guide covers both, starting with the professionals.

What does a respiratory therapist do?

According to the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, respiratory therapists care for people with breathing difficulties, including those with asthma or COPD. They assess patients, run diagnostic tests, give treatments and teach people to use their medicines and equipment. In the US, most need an associate degree in respiratory therapy, and all states but Alaska require a licence.

Your doctor may also refer you to pulmonary rehabilitation, which the WHO lists as one of the options for COPD. If you’ve been told you have a lung condition and nobody has mentioned either of these, it’s reasonable to ask.

How do you take care of your lungs every day?

This is the list from the American Lung Association and Cleveland Clinic, roughly in order of how much it matters. Notice it isn’t very glamorous.

  1. Don’t smoke or vapeAnd limit secondhand smoke. The ALA says it’s never too late to benefit from quitting.
  2. Watch the air you breatheLimit household chemicals, mould and radon indoors, and outdoor pollution on poor air-quality days.
  3. Mask up for dust and fumesAt work, and for DIY or hobbies that create them.
  4. Move regularlyRegular exercise helps keep your lungs strong, whatever your age or ability.
  5. Prevent infectionsWash your hands often and get the vaccines recommended for you.
  6. Stay hydratedCleveland Clinic says enough water helps keep mucus thin.
  7. Go to your check-upsLung conditions are easier to treat when they’re caught early. Don’t sit on a lingering cough.

Can you increase your lung capacity?

Be a bit sceptical here, because this is where a lot of wellness content overpromises. The American Lung Association says lung capacity peaks at around age 20 to 25 and starts a gradual decline after about 35, even in healthy people who have never smoked. Total capacity is about 6 litres, and it reflects your body as well as your habits.

What you can do is protect what you’ve got. The ALA says staying active, avoiding smoke and keeping up with vaccines can help you “protect and even strengthen” your lungs. Breathing exercises help you use your breathing muscles well and take fewer, slower breaths. That can feel like more room, but it isn’t evidence of bigger lungs, so be wary of anyone promising a bigger number.

One to take seriously: sudden trouble breathing isn’t normal ageing. Call a doctor.

What’s a simple daily breathing routine?

The ALA suggests 5 to 10 minutes a day of pursed-lip or belly breathing for people who get short of breath, and it’s a nice habit even if you don’t. Here’s a minimal version:

  1. Pick an anchorAfter your morning coffee, or before you brush your teeth at night.
  2. Sit tallShoulders down, one hand on your belly.
  3. Breathe in for 4, out for 6Through your nose if you can. Stop if you feel dizzy.
  4. Five minutes, then get on with your dayThat’s 30 breaths. Miss a day, and just start again.

Common questions

What is the difference between respiratory care and respiratory therapy?

The terms are often used interchangeably for the clinical treatment of breathing problems. In everyday searches, respiratory care can also mean looking after your own lungs.

How can I keep my lungs healthy naturally?

Don’t smoke or vape, avoid polluted air where you can, stay active, keep up with recommended vaccines and get symptoms checked early.

Do breathing exercises increase lung capacity?

There’s no good reason to expect a big change in total capacity. Breathing exercises can make your breathing slower and more efficient, and regular physical activity helps keep your lungs strong.

At what age does lung capacity decline?

According to the American Lung Association, it peaks at about 20 to 25 and gradually declines after about 35.

How long should I do breathing exercises each day?

The American Lung Association suggests 5 to 10 minutes a day for pursed-lip or belly breathing. If you have a lung condition, check with your care team first.
