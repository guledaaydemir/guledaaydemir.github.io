Diaphragmatic breathing: how to do belly breathing properly

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Updated 3 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

To do diaphragmatic breathing, lie on your back with your knees bent, one hand on your upper chest and the other just below your ribs. Breathe in slowly through your nose so your belly lifts your lower hand while your chest hand stays still. Then breathe out through pursed lips and let your belly fall. Cleveland Clinic suggests starting with 5 to 10 minutes, three or four times a day. It feels like work at first, and that’s normal.

First, a safety check

If you have severe trouble breathing, a tight or heavy chest, pain spreading to your arms, back, neck or jaw, very pale, blue or grey lips or skin, or sudden confusion, call your local emergency number now (999 in the UK, 911 in the US, 112 in the EU and Türkiye). Breathing exercises are not a treatment and not a substitute for medical help.

What is diaphragmatic breathing?

It’s slow breathing led by your diaphragm, the dome-shaped muscle under your lungs, rather than your upper chest and shoulders. You’ll also see it called belly breathing or abdominal breathing. They’re the same thing. Your stomach isn’t filling with air: as the diaphragm moves down, it nudges your belly out of the way.

How do you breathe from your diaphragm?

Start lying down, which Cleveland Clinic recommends for beginners:

  1. Lie on your backOn a bed or flat surface, knees bent and head supported. A pillow under your knees is fine.
  2. Place your handsOne on your upper chest, one just below your rib cage.
  3. Breathe in through your noseSlowly. Your lower hand rises and your chest hand stays as still as it can.
  4. Breathe out through pursed lipsTighten your stomach muscles gently and let your lower hand fall.

That top hand is your feedback. If it’s the one moving, you’re chest breathing. No drama. Slow down and try again.

How do you do it sitting down?

Same hands, same breath. Sit comfortably with your knees bent and your shoulders, head and neck relaxed. Once it’s easy lying down and sitting, try it at your desk, on the bus or in a queue. No one will notice, and that’s when it starts to be useful.

How long? Start with 5 to 10 minutes, three to four times a day, and build up slowly as it gets easier. If that’s too much for now, one session beats zero. Cleveland Clinic also says you can add a book on your belly later on for extra effort.

What are the common mistakes?

  • Pushing your belly out. Let the breath move it. Don’t perform it.
  • Lifting your shoulders. If they rise toward your ears, you’ve gone back to chest breathing.
  • Taking giant breaths. Slow matters more than big. If you get lightheaded, you’re overdoing it. Stop and breathe normally.
  • Waiting until you’re stressed. Learn it when you’re calm, so it’s there when you need it.

What are the benefits of diaphragmatic breathing?

Cleveland Clinic lists helping you relax, lowering heart rate and blood pressure, and reducing the work of breathing, among others. It also says the technique can help with stress, anxiety, asthma and COPD, but that it shouldn’t be the only treatment, and that people with those conditions should check with their provider first.

A fair reading: great as a low-risk way to settle down. Slow breathing is linked in research with calmer body signals, although a 2018 review found the evidence still limited and mixed. Treat bigger claims with caution.

What rhythm should you use?

The classic instructions don’t give you a count, and that’s fine. Some people find a rhythm helps. Pick a pattern where the exhale is at least as long as the inhale. The American Lung Association suggests breathing out for two to three times as long as you breathe in, if you’re practising it with pursed lips.

Common questions

Is belly breathing the same as diaphragmatic breathing?

Yes. Belly breathing, abdominal breathing and diaphragmatic breathing all describe the same technique.

How long does it take to learn diaphragmatic breathing?

It varies. Cleveland Clinic says it can feel tiring at first and gets more automatic with practice, so give it a few days of short sessions before you judge it.

Why do I feel dizzy when I breathe deeply?

You may be breathing too big or too fast. Stop, breathe normally and try again later with smaller, slower breaths. If dizziness keeps happening, talk to a doctor.

Can I do diaphragmatic breathing before sleep?

Yes. Lying down is the recommended position for beginners, which makes bedtime a natural slot.

Does diaphragmatic breathing help anxiety?

It can help you feel calmer in the moment, and Cleveland Clinic lists anxiety among the conditions it may help. It isn’t a stand-alone treatment. If anxiety is affecting your life, see a doctor.
