Pursed-lip breathing: how to do it and when it helps

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Updated 3 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

Pursed-lip breathing means breathing in through your nose for about 2 seconds, then out gently through lips shaped like you’re about to whistle, for 4 seconds or longer. It slows your breathing down and keeps the out-breath longer than the in-breath. Cleveland Clinic says it can ease shortness of breath for people with asthma, COPD or pulmonary fibrosis, and can help anyone with stress, but it doesn’t treat lung disease.

First, a safety check

If you have severe trouble breathing, a tight or heavy chest, pain spreading to your arms, back, neck or jaw, very pale, blue or grey lips or skin, or sudden confusion, call your local emergency number now (999 in the UK, 911 in the US, 112 in the EU and Türkiye). Breathing exercises are not a treatment and not a substitute for medical help.

How do you do pursed-lip breathing?

Picture cooling a hot drink. That’s most of it.

  1. Sit and relaxSit in a chair and let your neck and shoulders drop.
  2. In through your nose for 2Mouth closed, slow and easy. Not a big breath. Your belly should rise.
  3. Make a small OShape your lips as if you’re about to whistle.
  4. Out through your lips for 4 or moreLet your belly fall. Gentle: you’re not blowing out candles.

Why is the out-breath longer?

The key rule from both the American Lung Association and Cleveland Clinic: exhale for at least twice as long as you inhale, and never force the air out. Slower, longer out-breaths mean fewer breaths each minute. At 2 in and 4 out, a breath takes 6 seconds, which is 10 breaths a minute. A resting adult usually takes 12 to 18.

Who does pursed-lip breathing help?

  • People with asthma, COPD or pulmonary fibrosis. It can ease shortness of breath and make activity easier. It doesn’t treat the disease, and it doesn’t replace your inhaler. Check with your care team before you start.
  • Anyone with stress or tension. It’s a simple way to slow down, and one of the easiest long exhales to learn.

If you’ve landed here because you’ve suddenly become breathless and don’t know why, please don’t practise: get medical help.

When should you use it?

Cleveland Clinic suggests practising four to five times a day, for five to 10 minutes at a time, in quiet moments like reading or waiting for the kettle. The American Lung Association suggests 5 to 10 minutes daily, and advises learning when you’re breathing comfortably, not when you’re already out of breath.

Once it’s natural, save it for the moments of strain:

  • Climbing stairs
  • Bending or lifting
  • Walking or exercising

Common questions

How long should I do pursed-lip breathing?

Cleveland Clinic suggests four to five times a day for at least five to 10 minutes a session. The American Lung Association suggests 5 to 10 minutes daily.

What does pursed-lip breathing do?

It slows your breathing and keeps the out-breath longer than the in-breath. It can ease shortness of breath in some lung conditions and help you relax, but it doesn’t treat lung disease.

Can it help with anxiety?

Cleveland Clinic says it can help anyone reduce stress, anxiety or tension. It isn’t a treatment for an anxiety disorder.

Should I do it if I feel dizzy?

No. Stop and rest. Call your emergency number if you have difficulty breathing, sudden or severe breathlessness, chest pain, or dizziness or weakness that doesn’t improve.
