How the circulatory and respiratory systems work together

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Updated 3 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

The respiratory system brings oxygen into your lungs, and the circulatory system delivers it around your body. They meet in the alveoli, where blood drops off carbon dioxide and picks up oxygen. The heart then pumps that oxygen-rich blood out through the arteries, and the veins bring oxygen-poor blood back for another trip to the lungs. Neither system is any use without the other, and you can even feel them talking: your pulse shifts a little with every breath.

How do the circulatory and respiratory systems work together?

Think of it as a delivery business. The lungs are the warehouse where oxygen comes in and waste goes out. The blood vessels are the roads. The heart is the engine that keeps every van moving.

What are the organs of the circulatory system?

This is a common surprise on tests: Cleveland Clinic notes the heart is the system’s only organ. Everything else is a vessel. The lungs belong to the respiratory system, even though blood passes through them.

Parts of the circulatory system
PartJob
HeartThe muscular pump that moves your blood
ArteriesCarry blood away from the heart, including the aorta, the largest
VeinsBring blood back to the heart, including the vena cava
CapillariesThin-walled tiny vessels where oxygen, carbon dioxide, nutrients and waste are exchanged

What is the path of blood through the heart and lungs?

  1. Back from the bodyOxygen-poor blood enters the right side of the heart through the vena cava.
  2. To the lungsThe right ventricle pumps it through the pulmonary artery.
  3. The swapIn capillaries around the alveoli, blood gives up carbon dioxide and collects oxygen.
  4. Back to the heartOxygen-rich blood returns to the left side through the pulmonary veins.
  5. Out to the bodyThe left ventricle pushes it through the aorta, and the loop starts over.

Doctors split the trip in two: the pulmonary circulation is the heart-to-lungs part, and the systemic circulation is the heart-to-body part. Yes, the pulmonary artery carries oxygen-poor blood and the pulmonary veins carry oxygen-rich blood, the opposite of the rest of the body. Arteries lead away from the heart and veins lead back. That’s the rule to remember.

Why does your heart rate change when you breathe?

Your heart rate rises a little as you breathe in and falls a little as you breathe out. The name for this is respiratory sinus arrhythmia. Despite the scary word, it’s normal. Try it: find your pulse at your wrist, breathe slowly, and see if you can catch it.

A 2018 systematic review found that slow breathing increases both this effect and heart-rate variability. It also says the evidence is limited: only 15 studies qualified, and most were small. So: a real, measurable link between the two systems, and one to be modest about.

Common questions

What organs are in the circulatory system?

The heart is the only organ. The rest of the system is blood vessels: arteries, veins and capillaries. The lungs are part of the respiratory system.

Where do the circulatory and respiratory systems meet?

In the lungs, at the capillaries wrapped around the alveoli. That is where oxygen enters the blood and carbon dioxide leaves it.

Which side of the heart pumps blood to the lungs?

The right side. The left side pumps oxygen-rich blood to the rest of the body.

What happens if one of the systems stops working well?

The other is affected, because the two depend on each other. Conditions affecting the lungs can put a strain on the heart, and the reverse can be true. Ongoing breathlessness or chest symptoms are worth checking with a doctor.

Does breathing slowly lower your heart rate?

Your heart rate falls a little on each out-breath, and slow breathing is linked in studies with higher heart-rate variability. The evidence is limited, and breathing exercises don’t treat heart conditions.
