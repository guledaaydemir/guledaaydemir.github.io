About

ATHA means now

Not tomorrow. Not when work calms down. Not once you've built the perfect routine. The app is named after the only moment a breath can actually happen.

Why

Most wellbeing apps ask for too much

They want a twenty-minute session, a course to complete, a streak to defend, a profile to fill in, and an account before you've even seen what's inside. On a good day that's fine. On the day you actually need it — three in the morning, or between two meetings, or after a conversation that didn't go well — it's far too much friction, and the app stays closed.

ATHA was built around the opposite assumption: the best practice is the one short enough that you still do it on your worst day. Sixty seconds counts. Two taps to start. Nothing to set up, nothing to finish, nothing to lose if you skip a week.

Everything else follows from that. The dark interface exists because most people open it at night. The haptics exist so a session works with your eyes shut. The Garden grows rather than scores, because a number that goes down when you miss a day is a reason to avoid opening the app, not a reason to breathe.

Principles

Four decisions that shaped everything

●

Short beats thoroughA minute you actually do is worth more than twenty minutes you plan. Every screen is designed to get out of the way faster than it arrived.

●

Calm is a design constraint, not a themeNo red badges, no urgency, no dashboard of numbers demanding interpretation. If a screen raises your heart rate, it has failed at its job.

●

Your practice is yoursNo account, no ATHA cloud, nothing about your breathing sold or shared. Privacy here is an architectural decision made before the first screen was drawn — not a policy page written afterwards.

●

Honest about what breathing isATHA guides breathing. It does not diagnose, treat or cure anything, and it will never imply otherwise to make a sale.

Who

Built by one person

ATHA is designed, coded, tested, written and supported by Gül Eda Aydemir, an independent iOS developer. There is no team, no investor, no growth department deciding which dark pattern to test this quarter.

That has real trade-offs. Features arrive slowly. Some things you'd expect from a large app aren't there yet. But it also means every decision in ATHA was made by someone who has to live with it — and the person who wrote the code is the person who reads your support email.

ATHA is one of several independent iOS apps built the same way: on-device first, no accounts you don't need, nothing sold about you.

Money

ATHA is free

No subscription. No in-app purchases. No advertising. No data sold to anyone, for any purpose. There is no paid tier that the free version is quietly a demo of.

This matters more than it sounds. An app funded by advertising needs your attention, so it is built to hold you. An app funded by data needs your behaviour, so it is built to record you. ATHA has neither of those pressures, which is why nothing in it is designed to keep you there longer than the breath takes.

If that ever changes, it will be stated plainly on the features page before it happens, and nothing you already rely on will be taken away and sold back to you.

Questions

Things people ask

What does ATHA mean?

Now. The app is named after the only moment a breathing practice can actually happen — not tomorrow, not once things calm down.

Who makes ATHA?

Gül Eda Aydemir, an independent iOS developer. No team, no investors, no growth department. The person who writes the code answers the support email.

Is ATHA really free?

Yes. No subscription, no in-app purchases, no ads, no data sold. Nothing in the app is locked behind a payment, which also means nothing in it is designed to keep you there longer than the breath takes.

Can I suggest a feature?

Yes, and it gets read by the person who would build it. Email the idea — the useful ones tend to describe the moment you were in, not the feature you want.

Is ATHA available in other languages?

English and Turkish at launch, changeable in Settings. More languages are possible; the app was built to support them from the start.

Where can I read the legal detail?

The Privacy Policy covers data handling, and the Terms of Use cover licence, subscriptions and the health disclaimer.
