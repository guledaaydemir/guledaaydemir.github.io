Free breathing apps with no subscription, compared honestly

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Updated 15 Oct 2026 · 8 min read

Disclosure: I’m Gül Eda, and I make ATHA, one of the six apps below. I’ve tried to make this useful anyway: every competitor fact comes from that app’s US App Store page, checked on 15 October 2026, and each one is linked so you can check it yourself. I’ve also listed where other apps are the better choice. Prices and labels change, so look at the store page before you decide.

Quick answer

It depends what “free” needs to mean for you. If you want built-in and simple, Apple’s Mindfulness app (Breathe) is already on your Apple Watch. If you want no in-app purchases at all and a long track record, look at Breathing Zone (its App Store listing notes advertising). If privacy comes first, three apps here carry Apple’s “Data Not Collected” label: Awesome Breathing (free, optional tips), The Breathing App (free core, optional subscription) and ATHA (free, nothing for sale). Of those three, ATHA is the one with an Apple Watch app.

What does “free” actually mean in breathing apps?

Five different things, and the App Store shows all of them as a friendly “Free” button:

  • Built in. Apple’s Mindfulness app. No download, no catch, deliberately limited.
  • Free with ads. You pay with attention (and often data); a one-off purchase removes the ads.
  • Free with a tip jar. Everything works; you can pay what you like if you want to support it.
  • Freemium. A free core, with extras behind a subscription.
  • Free with nothing for sale. No purchases listed at all.

None of these is wrong. Ads and subscriptions are how many good apps stay alive. The point is to pick the trade-off you’re comfortable with, knowingly.

The six apps, one by one

Apple Mindfulness (Breathe)

Choose it if you have an Apple Watch and want something that’s already there: Breathe sessions from 1 to 5 minutes, a breath rate from 4 to 10 breaths a minute, haptics, State of Mind logging and Mindful Minutes in the Health app.

Skip it if you want hold phases (box breathing, 4-7-8), sessions longer than five minutes or anything sleep-specific. Its privacy label reads “Data Not Linked to You”.

Source: App Store: Mindfulness

Breathing Zone

Choose it if you want a focused breathing app with no purchases listed and a long track record: it has been on the App Store since 2010. It covers coherent, box, 4-7-8 and extended-exhale breathing, custom rates and durations, voice and haptic guidance, a dedicated Apple Watch app, Apple Health, and even Apple TV.

Skip it if ads bother you (the age-rating section lists “Advertising”) or you want the app to collect nothing: its label lists analytics, usage and crash data as “Data Not Linked to You”. English only.

Source: App Store: Breathing Zone

Awesome Breathing: Pacer Timer

Choose it if you like tinkering: fully custom inhale, exhale and hold timings, fifteen included programs, free-form or timed sessions, a vibration mode for eyes-closed practice, and Mindful Minutes in Apple Health. It also runs on Apple silicon Macs and Vision Pro, and its label says Data Not Collected. Support is optional tips.

Skip it if you want an Apple Watch app (none is listed) or an interface in a language other than English.

Source: App Store: Awesome Breathing

The Breathing App

Choose it if you want resonance breathing guided mainly by sound, with box, 4-7-8 and personal ratios that take decimals. The developer says core features remain free forever, and the label says Data Not Collected.

Skip it if you don’t want any subscription prompts (premium is $3.99–$4.99 a month or $39.99–$44.99 a year), you need an Apple Watch app (none listed) or the app is for a younger teen (it’s rated 13+).

Source: App Store: The Breathing App

iBreathe: Relax and Breathe

Choose it if you want one app on everything: iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple TV, Vision Pro and Apple Watch. It’s also the most-rated app in this list (nearly 18,000 US ratings), so plenty of people have tried it.

Skip it if privacy matters to you: its label reads “Data Used to Track You” (location, identifiers, usage data and diagnostics), and it shows ads unless you pay $2.99 to remove them.

Source: App Store: iBreathe

ATHA (the one I make)

Choose it if you want help in the moment without choosing anything: tap Breathe now or I need a moment, or pick a moment like Before a meeting, then close your eyes and follow haptic taps. Ten built-in patterns plus your own, an Apple Watch app, widgets and a Live Activity (iOS 26.1 or later), Siri, write-only Apple Health, English and Turkish. Nothing is for sale, there’s no account, and the app makes no network requests (label: Data Not Collected).

Skip it if you need anything from the list below.

Source: App Store: ATHA · ATHA privacy policy

Where ATHA is weaker

In the interest of keeping myself honest:

  • It’s new. Version 1.0 came out in September 2026 and has no ratings on the US App Store yet (16 Oct 2026). The others have years of updates behind them.
  • iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch only. No Mac, Apple TV, Vision Pro or Android app (it’s not verified for Mac).
  • Fewer built-in patterns than Awesome Breathing’s fifteen programs. Ten, plus unlimited custom ones.
  • No heart-rate readout. ATHA writes to Apple Health but reads nothing from it, by design.
  • Two languages. English and Turkish today.
  • Sessions top out at 60 minutes, and there’s no free-form, open-ended mode.
  • The screen has to stay awake. In 1.0, guidance stops if the iPhone auto-locks, so raise Auto-Lock for longer sessions (or use the Watch).
  • No spoken voice or ambient sounds yet. 1.0 guides with haptics and optional tones, and on silent it plays no sound. A spoken voice and ambient sounds are planned for 2.0.
  • Widgets and the Live Activity need iOS 26.1 or later in 1.0, although the app itself runs on iOS 18.6.

How can I check whether a breathing app collects my data?

Open the app’s App Store page and scroll to App Privacy. The headline tells you most of what you need:

  • Data Used to Track You: data may be used to follow you across other companies’ apps and websites.
  • Data Linked to You: collected and tied to your identity.
  • Data Not Linked to You: collected, but not tied to your identity.
  • Data Not Collected: the developer says it collects nothing.

Two caveats. These labels are filled in by developers, so they’re a declaration, not an audit. And a label can change with any update. For ATHA, the privacy policy spells out what the code does: no account, no analytics or ad SDKs, no network requests, and data that stays on your device.

Is Apple’s built-in Breathe app enough?

For a lot of people, yes. If you want one to five minutes of even, guided breathing with taps on your wrist, it’s free, already installed and perfectly good. Reach for a third-party app when you want holds (box breathing, 4-7-8), longer sessions, a sleep-friendly eyes-closed mode, or practice on iPhone without a Watch.

Common questions

What is the best free breathing app with no subscription?

It depends on your trade-off. Apple’s Mindfulness app is built into Apple Watch. Breathing Zone has no in-app purchases listed but its listing notes advertising. Awesome Breathing is tip-supported. ATHA is free with nothing for sale, works on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, and its label says Data Not Collected. (Checked 15 October 2026.)

Which breathing apps don’t collect data?

As of 15 October 2026, three apps in this comparison show Apple’s “Data Not Collected” label: Awesome Breathing, The Breathing App and ATHA. Labels are self-reported and can change with updates, so check the App Privacy section before you install.

Does Apple Watch have a built-in breathing app?

Yes. The Mindfulness app includes Breathe: 1 to 5 minute sessions at 4 to 10 breaths a minute, with haptics, and it logs Mindful Minutes to the Health app. It has no hold phases, so it can’t pace box or 4-7-8 breathing.

Is ATHA really free?

Yes. ATHA has no subscription, no in-app purchases, no ads and no account, and every feature is included. If that ever changes, it will be announced on the website before the update that changes it.
