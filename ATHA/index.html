How it works

Three steps, about a minute

No course to finish and nothing to set up. Open it when you need it.

  1. Pick your moment

    Tap Breathe now, tell ATHA how you feel (stressed, tired, anxious, overwhelmed…) or what’s coming up: Before a meeting, Before sleep, After a hard day. It picks the breath for you.

  2. Follow the breath

    A calm visual grows and settles with each phase, a haptic tap marks every change, and an optional soft tone can mark each phase too. Prefer the dark? Switch to Eyes closed and the screen dims.

  3. Come back tomorrow, gently

    Daily Breath suggests one practice a day, routines send a quiet local reminder, and weekly recaps show your minutes adding up. Missed a day? “A new streak can begin today.”

What it helps with

Built for the moments you reach for your phone

You don’t need a class. You need the next sixty seconds to go a little better.

Fall asleep without a bright, busy screen

When your body is tired but your mind isn’t, a slow breath with a long exhale gives your attention one quiet job to do.

  • 4-7-8 (in 4, hold 7, out 8), Slow Breathing (6 in, 8 out) and Deep Relaxation (5-2-8) are built in.
  • Eyes-closed mode dims the screen; haptic taps (and a soft tone, if you turn it on) tell you when to breathe, so you never have to look. In ATHA 1.0, set Auto-Lock longer than your session, or the cues stop when the screen locks.
  • A Live Activity shows the session on your Lock Screen for sessions of 3 minutes or more (iOS 26.1 or later).

Calm down fast, with nothing to decide

In a tense moment, choosing a technique is one decision too many. ATHA keeps it to one tap.

  • I need a moment: a 3-second “settle in”, then a short guided breath.
  • Extended Exhale (4 in, 6 out), Quick Reset (3 in, 5 out) and Calming Breath (4-6, then a 2-second pause).
  • Start from the Quick Breath widget on your Home or Lock Screen (iOS 26.1 or later), or say “Breathe now with ATHA” to Siri.

Why the long exhale? In a 2023 randomized study, five minutes a day of cyclic sighing (two inhales, then a long, slow exhale) lifted mood more than mindfulness meditation over a month (Balban et al., Cell Reports Medicine). ATHA’s long-exhale patterns share the idea, but they were not what the study tested.

Steady before a meeting, clear between work blocks

Some moments need you calm and awake. Even, balanced rhythms are a popular choice when you want to feel composed rather than sleepy.

  • Box Breathing (4-4-4-4) with its own Box visual and a phase timeline.
  • Coherence (5 in, 5 out, about six breaths a minute) for a 5-minute reset.
  • The Before a meeting and Help me focus moments pick the breath for you.
  • On Apple Watch, follow the taps on your wrist while you sit in the room.

Known issues in 1.0

  • Auto-Lock stops the cues. If your iPhone locks itself during a session, haptics and tones stop. Set Auto-Lock longer than the session, or use Apple Watch.
  • Silent mode mutes ATHA. With the Ring/Silent switch on silent, ATHA plays no sound, so keep haptics on.
  • Widgets and the Live Activity need iOS 26.1 or later. The app itself runs on iOS 18.6.
  • No spoken voice or ambient sounds yet. Phase tones and completion sounds work; a spoken guided voice and ambient sounds are planned for version 2.0.
  • The “Anonymous usage data” switch does nothing. No analytics are sent, whether it is on or off.

In development for version 2.0, not in the App Store yet and with no release date: a one-tap “Calm Down Now” control, a sleep session that slowly fades out, guided multi-day programs and five more languages. This page will say so when they ship.

Questions

Questions people ask

Is ATHA really free?

Yes. ATHA is free to download and every feature is unlocked: no subscription, no in-app purchases, no ads and no trial that runs out.

Does ATHA collect my data?

No. ATHA has no account and makes no network requests, and it contains no analytics or advertising SDKs. Your sessions, moods and notes stay on your device. Apple’s App Store privacy label for ATHA reads “Data Not Collected”.

What is the best breathing exercise to fall asleep?

Any slow pattern where the out-breath is longer than the in-breath, done for two to five minutes with the lights off: for example 4-7-8 (in 4, hold 7, out 8) or 4 in and 6–8 out. Start small: four cycles of 4-7-8 take 76 seconds, and Cleveland Clinic suggests three cycles twice a day. In ATHA, use 4-7-8, Slow Breathing (6-8) or Deep Relaxation in Eyes-closed mode. If you have had trouble sleeping for months, the NHS advises seeing a GP.

How do I calm down fast when anxiety hits?

Make your out-breath longer than your in-breath: in through the nose for 4, out slowly for 6, for one to two minutes, with your shoulders down. The NHS describes slow breathing as a calming technique for stress, anxiety and panic. In ATHA, tap “I need a moment” for a 3-second settle-in and a short guided breath, or open Extended Exhale (4-6). If you have chest pain or trouble breathing, call your local emergency number.

How do I do box breathing?

Breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 4, breathe out for 4 and hold for 4, then repeat for two to five minutes. Many people use it before a meeting or exam because the even rhythm steadies you without pushing you toward sleep. ATHA’s Box Breathing has its own Box visual and phase timeline, and a haptic tap marks each phase.

What is coherent breathing (5-5)?

Breathing in for about 5 seconds and out for about 5, roughly six breaths a minute. The NHS breathing exercise for stress uses the same rhythm (count 1 to 5 in, 1 to 5 out) for at least 5 minutes, ideally every day. In ATHA it is called Coherence.

Can I use ATHA in bed with my eyes closed?

Yes. Eyes-closed mode dims the screen and carries the rhythm with haptic taps and, if you turn on Full Guided Mode, a soft tone at each phase, so keep at least one of them on. Haptics come in three strengths (Light, Medium, Strong) or off. On silent (Ring/Silent switch), ATHA 1.0 plays no sound, so keep haptics on. In ATHA 1.0 the cues stop if your iPhone auto-locks, so for longer sessions set Settings › Display & Brightness › Auto-Lock to longer than the session.

Does ATHA work on Apple Watch?

Yes, on watchOS 10.6 or later. After the first sync, the Watch lists I need a moment, today’s Daily Breath and up to five favourites from your iPhone, so mark a practice as a favourite to see it on your wrist. It guides you with haptic taps and keeps running with your wrist down. Watch sessions appear in your iPhone history.

How do I log mindful minutes in Apple Health with ATHA?

Open ATHA’s Settings, go to Apple Health and turn on Save sessions; completed sessions are written as Mindful Minutes. You can also turn on Share reflections to save them as State of Mind. ATHA only writes to Health and reads nothing.

Which devices and iOS versions does ATHA support?

iPhone and iPad with iOS 18.6 or later, and Apple Watch with watchOS 10.6 or later. In ATHA 1.0, widgets and the Live Activity need iOS 26.1 or later. The app is in English and Turkish. It is not verified for Mac.

Can I create my own breathing pattern?

Yes. In Explore, choose Create your own, set inhale, hold, exhale and hold-out (any phase can be 0), name it and save it. Sessions can run from 1 to 60 minutes.

Is ATHA a medical app?

No. ATHA guides breathing for everyday calm, sleep and focus; it does not diagnose or treat anything. For ongoing anxiety, panic or sleep problems, talk to a doctor. In a crisis, contact your local emergency number.
