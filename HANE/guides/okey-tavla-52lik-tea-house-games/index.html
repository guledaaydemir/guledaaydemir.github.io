Okey, tavla and 52'lik: the table games of a Turkish tea house

By Gül Eda Aydemir · Updated November 16, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer

The classic Turkish tea-house games are okey, a four-player rummy game with 106 numbered tiles; tavla, the Turkish name for backgammon; and card games played with a standard 52-card pack, the 52'lik, such as batak, king and pişti. In the game HANE you run these tables as the çırak: you serve the players, you do not play the games yourself.

Key points

  • Okey uses 106 tiles: 1 to 13 in four colours, twice over, plus two false jokers.
  • The okey (wild joker) is the tile one number above the face-up indicator, in the same colour.
  • Tavla is Turkish backgammon: no doubling cube, a mars scores double, and dice are called in Persian numbers.
  • 52'lik is the ordinary 52-card pack, used for batak, king and pişti.
  • In HANE you hand out the sets and find okey tables their fourth player; you do not play okey yourself.

What games do people play in a Turkish tea house?

Walk into almost any Turkish kahvehane and you will see three kinds of game on the tables: okey, a rummy game played with numbered tiles; tavla, the Turkish name for backgammon; and card games played with a standard 52-card pack, known simply as a 52'lik. Most tea houses keep their own sets, and you ask for one along with your tea.

Games and coffeehouses have gone together for centuries. Ottoman-era accounts already describe customers playing tavla and chess, and the clatter of checkers is still the sound of a neighbourhood kahve. Today a table of four regulars bent over their okey racks is one of the most familiar sights of all.

How do you play okey?

Okey is a rummy game for four players, played anticlockwise with 106 tiles: the numbers 1 to 13 in four colours, two of each, plus two sahte okey (false jokers). In short:

  • The deal. The tiles are mixed face down and built into stacks. Each player takes 14 tiles onto a wooden rack, the ıstaka, where only he can see them; the player who starts takes 15.
  • The indicator and the okey. One tile is turned face up as the indicator (gösterge). The tile of the same colour one number higher becomes the okey, the wild joker; if the indicator is a 13, the 1 of that colour is okey. The two false jokers stand in for the okey tile's own value.
  • A turn. Take either the tile just discarded by the player before you or a new tile from the centre, then discard one tile face up.
  • Winning. Arrange 14 tiles into sets (three or four of the same number in different colours) and runs (three or more consecutive numbers in one colour), discard your 15th tile and lay your hand open. A 1 can follow the 13 or come before the 2, but not both at once: 12-13-1 is a run, 13-1-2 is not. Seven pairs of identical tiles (çift) also wins.
  • Scoring. It varies from table to table. One common system starts everyone at 20 points and takes points off the losers, more when the winner goes out with pairs or by discarding the okey itself.

A popular cousin, Okey 101, deals 21 tiles each and lets players lay down melds during the game; your first meld must be worth at least 101 points.

Why does okey need a fourth player?

Okey is built for four. Every discard feeds the next player in turn, so the game is a chain: what you throw away may be exactly what your neighbour needs. You are always watching the player on your left, whose discards you can take, and the one on your right, who may take yours. Two or three people can play, but the classic tea-house game is four round a table, and three regulars with a set and an empty chair will look around the room for a dördüncü, a fourth.

In HANE, finding that fourth player is literally part of the çırak's job.

How is tavla different from backgammon?

Tavla is simply the Turkish name for backgammon: two players, 15 checkers (pul) each, a board of 24 points and two dice. You move all your checkers round into your home board and bear them off; the first to clear the board wins. A lone checker can be hit and sent back to start again, and a point held by two or more checkers is closed to the opponent. Doubles are played four times.

A few things feel different at a tea-house table:

  • No doubling cube. A normal win scores 1 point. If you finish before your opponent has borne off a single checker, it is a mars and scores 2.
  • Short matches. Games are played to a set score, often 5.
  • Persian numbers. Rolls are called out with old Persian-derived names: yek (1), dü (2), se (3), cihar (4), penç (5), şeş (6). So 6–5 is şeş beş, 6–6 is dü şeş and 1–1 is hep yek.
  • Speed and noise. Experienced players move fast and slap their checkers down hard, and onlookers comment on every roll.

House rules are rarely written down; they are learned at the table, and they vary from place to place. If you sit down to play, ask which rules the table uses before the first roll.

What is 52'lik, and which card games are played with it?

52'lik simply means “the 52 one”: an ordinary pack of playing cards (iskambil), with the suits called maça (spades), kupa (hearts), karo (diamonds) and sinek (clubs). The international 52-card pack is the one in general use in Türkiye, and in tea houses it is the deck behind several favourite games:

  • Batak: a four-player trick-taking game with trumps. Each player gets 13 cards and bids how many tricks he expects to win.
  • King: a four-player game of 20 deals. Each player in turn chooses a contract: either a trump game, where you take as many tricks as you can, or a penalty game, where you avoid certain cards or tricks, such as the queens, the last two tricks or the king of hearts, known as rıfkı.
  • Pişti: a quick capturing game, often played two against two. You take the pile in the middle by matching its top card or by playing a jack; capturing a pile of just one card is a pişti and earns a 10-point bonus. Taking a lone jack with another jack is a double pişti worth 20, and games usually run to 151 points.

What are the manners at a tea-house game table?

Tea-house games are friendly, but they are taken seriously. A few habits will keep you on the right side of the regulars:

  • Wait to be asked. An empty chair at a game table is not necessarily free; it may be someone's seat.
  • Onlookers keep quiet. Watching is welcome; telling a player what to discard is not.
  • Hands off the tiles and cards of a game you are not playing.
  • Keep the tea coming. A game of okey can last a long while and glasses empty fast. Standing the table a round is always appreciated.
  • Lose gracefully. Tempers do flare over a misplayed tile or a lucky roll, and every tea-house keeper knows how quickly a game can turn into a quarrel.
The usta's tip

Want to join a game in a real kahvehane? Don't just take the empty seat at an okey table. Ask the players: “Dördüncü lazım mı?”, “Do you need a fourth?”

Can you play okey in HANE?

No, and it is worth saying clearly: HANE has no okey, tavla or card game for you to play. HANE is a tea-house management game for iPhone and iPad, and you are the çırak. Your job is to serve the players, not to join them.

The games are still part of the house. The tea house has okey, 52'lik and tavla tables, which you add on the Geliştir (upgrades) screen. The çırak takes a set from the games shelf and hands it out, and when an okey table is one player short, he finds its fourth. Meanwhile the rest of the room still wants its tea.

Sometimes a game ends in a quarrel. Then you choose: calm it yourself, which may fail; call the usta, which always works but takes a while; or call the zabıta (municipal police), who walks in from the street. The game pauses while you decide and shows what each choice costs or risks. Waiters you hire can help with serving, but quarrels stay yours.

Questions people ask

How many tiles are in an okey set?

106: the numbers 1 to 13 in four colours, two of each (104 tiles), plus two false jokers (sahte okey). Four players each take 14 tiles, and the player who starts takes 15.

Is tavla the same as backgammon?

Yes. Tavla is the Turkish name for backgammon, with the same board, 15 checkers each and two dice. At Turkish tables there is no doubling cube, a mars (opponent has borne off nothing) scores 2 points, and rolls are often called out in Persian-derived numbers such as şeş beş.

What does 52'lik mean?

52'lik means “the 52 one”: an ordinary 52-card pack of playing cards (iskambil). In Turkish tea houses it is used for card games such as batak, king and pişti.

Can you play okey in HANE?

No. HANE is a tea-house management game: you are the çırak who serves the players. You hand out the okey, 52'lik and tavla sets and find okey tables their fourth player, and you settle quarrels, but you do not play the games yourself.
