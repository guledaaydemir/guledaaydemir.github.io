HANE beginner's guide: tips for your first days as the çırak

By Gül Eda Aydemir · Updated November 16, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer

In HANE you play the çırak: drag anywhere to walk, and stand next to a table, the counter or a shelf for a moment to do the job there. Serve every group in the same order: take the order, call it out at the counter, carry the glasses on the tray to the right table, collect the bill and wipe the table. Shop before the shelves run dry, and spend your first coins on a bigger tray, a bigger basket and more tables.

Key points

  • Drag anywhere to walk; stand next to a table, the counter or a shelf and the çırak does the job.
  • The usta only brews what the çırak has called out at the counter.
  • Shop before the shelves run dry: guests wait while the çırak is at the market.
  • A bigger tray (220 jeton), a bigger basket (180) and more tables save the most running early on.
  • Kadıköy costs 300 jeton and one star; a bank loan costs a tenth of the loan every day, plus interest.

How do you play HANE as a beginner?

You play the çırak, the apprentice in the red fez, in a traditional Istanbul kıraathane (tea house). There are only two controls: drag anywhere on the screen to walk, and stand next to something for a moment. The çırak then does whatever is needed there, from taking an order to wiping a table.

A game day lasts about four minutes. Then comes the day report and a small "Ready?" card, and the game waits until you start the next day. A short guide explains everything the first time; reread it under Settings ▸ How to play.

How do you serve a guest, step by step?

Every group follows the same path, and learning it is most of the game:

  1. Take the order at the table. It goes on the çırak's notepad.
  2. Call it out at the counter. Walk to the usta (the master tea maker) and stand there; the çırak calls the orders out loud.
  3. Load the tray. Lift the ready glasses onto the hanging tray (askılı tepsi). It holds two glasses at first.
  4. Serve the right table. An item can only be put down at the table that ordered it.
  5. Collect the bill once the guests have finished.
  6. Wipe the table. Soap bubbles, a sparkle, and the table is ready for the next group.

At the tables for okey (a tile game), 52'lik (cards) and tavla (backgammon) you also bring the set from the games shelf; see our guide to the three table games.

Why isn't the usta making tea?

Almost always because the order has not been called out: the usta makes only what the çırak calls out at the counter. Taking an order at the table only writes it on the notepad; until you walk back, a megaphone with the waiting items floats over the counter. Stand at the counter for a moment and the usta gets to work.

The other reason: now and then the usta is called away (never on a branch's first day). Then nothing is made until the çırak stands at the counter and brews by hand, or the usta comes back.

What happens on your first day?

The first day is quick and generous. The very first morning starts at the market with the usta's list, guests come in fast, and the first time you do each basic thing (shopping, an order, a tea, a bill, wiping, watering, a cat) the usta slips the çırak a few coins, shown as "+10". On a branch's first two days guests also order almost at once and do not linger over their tea, which makes the loop easy to learn.

When should you go to the market?

Before the shelves run dry, and ideally in a quiet moment. Tea, coffee, bread, kaşar (a mild yellow cheese) and oralet (an instant fruit tea) are used up as you serve; what cannot be made waits. The basket over the scene opens the Market, pulses when stocks run low and shows the servings left.

The catch: while the çırak is at the market he is off the floor, and guests wait unless waiters are serving. The basket holds 4 packs at first, so early on you shop often. Buy before the rush, not during it.

Which upgrades should you buy first?

Upgrades are bought on the Upgrades (Geliştir) screen, which pulses when something new comes within reach. There is no single right order, but three early upgrades save the most walking:

  • A bigger tray. The tray for 4 glasses costs 220 jeton (the game's coins), the one for 6 costs 900. Twice the glasses means fewer trips.
  • A bigger basket. 8 packs for 180 jeton, 12 for 650. Fewer market trips mean less time with the floor unattended.
  • More tables. With no free table, up to four groups queue at the door, their faces turning from smiles to frowns until they leave. Extra tables seat them instead.

Later come chairs, the double teapot set (çift çaydanlık), menu items such as Turkish coffee (25 jeton) and cheese toast (30), and the table games. Upgrades belong to one branch: every new branch starts from nothing.

How do you handle cats, quarrels, rain and flowers?

Each incident opens a decision window that pauses the game and shows what every choice costs or risks, so nothing has to be decided in a rush.

  • Cats (Mırmır, Pamuk, Aslan, Tekir, Karagöz): walk up to see one out, or buy cat food and put a bowl down. A cat fed often feels at home: it is harder to see out, climbs onto the tables sooner and may leave a mess the çırak has to sweep up.
  • Quarrels over a game: calm it yourself (it may fail), call the usta (always works, but takes a while) or the zabıta, the municipal police, who walk in from the street.
  • Rain: hand out umbrellas at the outdoor tables.
  • Flowers get thirsty once a day at a random time. Water them, or they wilt and dent everyone's mood.
The usta's tip

When a golden ring glows under the flowers or a cat, deal with it on your way to the counter. A short detour now saves trouble later.

How do satisfaction and price rises work?

Each branch has a satisfaction score from 0 to 100, and every departing guest pulls it towards how they felt. Under Upgrades ▸ Menu you can raise prices by 10% at most once a day, up to +40%. The first step needs 60% satisfaction, and each further step 10 points more. Every step in force costs each guest 4 points of satisfaction, and prices the guests no longer accept come down by themselves when the day is settled.

Mind the lower limits too: below 40% at the end of a day the zabıta give a warning, and below 25% after a warning they close the branch until a fine is paid. Raise prices only while satisfaction sits well above the next threshold.

How do you open Kadıköy and the other branches?

Open the Map (Harita), which pulses when a branch can be opened with the coins in hand. Kadıköy, the second branch, costs only 300 jeton and one star, so it is within reach in the first few days. Each has its own usta and floor plan:

Should you take a bank loan in HANE?

Only if the instalments fit comfortably into your days. From the map, the bank lends what is missing for a new branch, up to 70% of its price. Then:

  • a tenth of the loan is due every day (send the usta to the bank);
  • 2% interest is added every evening, and another 5% on a day the instalment was not paid;
  • after three unpaid days in a row, the zabıta close the branch the loan paid for until an instalment is paid.

Example: Beşiktaş costs 2,500 jeton. With 1,000 in hand you would borrow 1,500, so 150 jeton fall due every day, and 2% of a 1,500-jeton debt is another 30 jeton of interest an evening. Shopping must still leave room for that, so borrow only what you need.

When should you hire waiters?

Once a branch keeps you running and market trips start costing guests. You can hire up to three under Upgrades ▸ Staff, and the Staff screen (Kadro) shows what each one is doing. Waiters work by themselves at about half the çırak's speed, with their own tray and orders; training makes them quicker and lets them carry more. They keep serving while the çırak is at the market. The cat and the quarrels stay yours.

Why does HANE get busier from day six?

From a branch's sixth day, guests come more often every day: the gaps between arrivals are a fifth shorter on day six and keep shrinking a little each day, down to just over half. More guests mean more running and a fuller till: this is when your upgrades and waiters pay off. And every fourth group is in a hurry and gives up after half the usual wait.

What are the best tips for your first days?

  1. Call out every order at the counter as soon as you have it.
  2. Shop in quiet moments, before anything runs out.
  3. Buy the 4-glass tray and the 8-pack basket early.
  4. Add tables when groups start queueing at the door.
  5. Raise prices only with satisfaction to spare.
  6. Open Kadıköy as soon as you have 300 jeton and a star.
  7. Borrow only as much as the daily instalments allow.
  8. Think about hiring a waiter before day six.

After day three you can also follow the çırak's own savings: read how his kumbara works. More answers are in the FAQ.

Questions people ask

Why is the usta not making tea in HANE?

Because the order has not been called out yet. Taking an order only writes it on the çırak's notepad. Walk to the counter and stand there for a moment so the çırak calls it out, and the usta starts brewing. If the usta has been called away, the çırak has to brew by hand at the counter.

How do I open the second branch, Kadıköy?

Open the Map (Harita). Kadıköy costs 300 jeton and one star and is reachable within the first few days. Like every branch, it opens with a red ribbon that you cut by swiping across it.

What happens if I miss a bank loan instalment in HANE?

That evening 5% more interest is added on top of the usual 2%. After three days in a row without an instalment, the zabıta close the branch the loan paid for until an instalment is paid.

Do waiters handle cats and quarrels?

No. Waiters take orders, serve, collect bills, bring game sets and clean, at about half the çırak's speed, but the cat and the quarrels stay the çırak's job.

Does HANE need an internet connection?

No. HANE plays fully offline on iPhone and iPad, with no ads, no in-app purchases and no account, and the save stays on the device.
