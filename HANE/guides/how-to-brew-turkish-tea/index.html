Tea and coffee

How to brew Turkish tea like an usta (and serve Turkish coffee)

By Gül Eda Aydemir · Updated November 16, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer

Brew Turkish tea in a çaydanlık, a double teapot: boil water in the lower kettle, pour it over black tea leaves in the small pot on top, and let it steep on the lowest heat for about 10 to 15 minutes. Pour the strong brew into a tulip glass and top it up with hot water from below, little for koyu (strong), more for açık (light), until it glows a clear red known as tavşan kanı.

Key points

  • A çaydanlık is a double teapot: water boils below, the tea brews slowly above.
  • Steep on the lowest heat for about 10 to 15 minutes; many tea houses go up to 20.
  • Every glass is mixed to order: more brew for koyu (strong), more water for açık (light).
  • The ideal colour is tavşan kanı, “rabbit's blood”: a clear, deep, reddish amber.
  • Turkish coffee is simmered slowly in a cezve until it foams, then served with water and often lokum.

How do you brew Turkish tea in a çaydanlık?

Turkish tea is brewed strong in a çaydanlık, a double teapot: a large kettle of water below and a small teapot of tea leaves stacked on top. The steam warms the leaves while the water heats; then you pour boiling water over the leaves, let them steep slowly on a low flame, and dilute each glass to taste with hot water from the kettle. Here is the method, step by step.

  1. Fill the lower kettle about two-thirds with fresh, cold water, put it on the heat and set the small teapot on top.
  2. Put dry black tea in the top pot. As a starting point, use about one heaped teaspoon of leaves for each glass you plan to pour. Turkish black tea from Rize is the classic choice.
  3. When the water boils, pour it over the leaves to fill the teapot. Top up the lower kettle with fresh water and set the teapot back on top.
  4. Turn the heat right down. Let the tea steep gently, without boiling, for about 10 to 15 minutes. When the leaves have sunk to the bottom, the brew is ready.
  5. Pour the strong brew into a tulip glass first, then top it up with hot water from the lower kettle: little water for koyu (strong), more for açık (light).
  6. Serve on a saucer with a small spoon and sugar cubes on the side.

How much tea should you use, and how long should it steep?

There is no single official measure; every house has its own. The amount depends on the size of your teapot and on the tea itself: Turkish black tea is usually used more generously than Ceylon-style blends. Start with about one heaped teaspoon per glass, then adjust after a day or two of tasting.

Steeping time matters as much as the amount. Brew too briefly and the tea tastes thin; leave it too long, or on too high a flame, and it turns bitter. Around 10 to 15 minutes on the lowest heat suits most Turkish black tea, and many tea houses let it go as long as 20. Once brewed, tea is best fresh: a pot kept hot for hours loses its brightness and gains bitterness, which is why a good usta brews little and often.

The usta's tip

Lift the lid: when the leaves have sunk to the bottom of the teapot, the tea is ready. Pour the brew first, then the water, and hold the glass up to the light. You are looking for tavşan kanı.

What does “tavşan kanı” mean?

Tavşan kanı, “rabbit's blood”, is the name for the ideal colour of a glass of Turkish tea: a clear, deep, reddish amber that glows when you hold it up to the light. It is not about strength alone. A glass can be dark and murky, or pale and watery; tavşan kanı is the bright, clear red in between, and it is the colour people mean when they praise a well-brewed tea.

Açık or koyu: how strong should Turkish tea be?

Because the brew in the top pot is concentrated, every glass is mixed on the spot. Ask for açık (light) and you get a little brew and plenty of hot water. Ask for koyu, also called demli (strong), and the glass is mostly brew. Neither is wrong; it is purely a matter of taste, and in a good tea house the regulars' preferences are known by heart.

These are exactly the two teas on the menu in HANE: koyu (strong) and açık (light), at 10 jeton each.

Why is Turkish tea served in a tulip glass?

Turkish tea comes in an ince belli bardak, a small “thin-waisted” glass shaped like a tulip, set on a little saucer with a teaspoon. The clear glass shows off the colour, it is usually held by the rim, and its small size means each glass is finished while still hot, so a fresh one can follow. Sugar comes as cubes on the saucer, and the tea is drunk without milk.

UNESCO recognised this way of life in 2022, when it inscribed the “Culture of Çay (tea), a symbol of identity, hospitality and social interaction” of Azerbaijan and Türkiye on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The inscription describes tea as a social practice that shows hospitality and builds social ties, usually served with sweets, sugar or lemon close at hand.

Where does Turkish tea come from?

Turkish tea grows on the steep, rainy hills of the Eastern Black Sea coast, and Rize is its heart: more than half of the country's tea is grown there, with the rest coming from Trabzon, Artvin, Ordu and Giresun. Tea growing in the region was set going by a 1924 law (no. 407) on growing hazelnuts, citrus fruit and tea in Rize and Borçka.

Turks also drink a remarkable amount of it. An FAO study for the Intergovernmental Group on Tea already counted Turkey among the countries with the highest per-person tea consumption in the world, and recent reporting based on Turkish Statistical Institute data puts Türkiye first. Tea only became the coffeehouse's everyday drink in the 19th century; the story is told in what a kıraathane is.

How do you make Turkish coffee in a cezve?

Turkish coffee is very finely ground coffee simmered slowly with water, and sugar if you like, in a cezve, a small long-handled pot, until a thick foam (köpük) rises. It is never filtered: the grounds settle at the bottom of the cup.

  1. For each cup, put a generous teaspoon of finely ground Turkish coffee into the cezve, with sugar to taste: none (sade), a little (az şekerli), medium (orta) or sweet (şekerli).
  2. Add one coffee cup of cold or room-temperature water per serving. Stir well now, because you will not stir again.
  3. Warm it slowly over low heat, for about three minutes. As soon as the coffee starts to rise and foam, lift the cezve off the heat before it reaches the brim.
  4. Spoon a little foam into each cup, then return the cezve to the heat and let it rise once more.
  5. Pour gently so the foam stays on top. Never let it boil, or it turns bitter and loses its foam.
  6. Serve with a glass of water and, often, a piece of lokum (Turkish delight).

The water is traditionally drunk first, to clear the palate. A famous saying goes with the cup: bir fincan kahvenin kırk yıl hatırı vardır, “a cup of coffee is remembered for forty years”. UNESCO inscribed “Turkish coffee culture and tradition” on its intangible heritage list in 2013.

Why is my tea bitter? Quick troubleshooting

ProblemLikely causeWhat to do
Bitter teaSteeped too long, on too high a heat, or kept hot for hoursKeep the flame at its lowest, steep for about 10 to 15 minutes and brew a fresh pot instead of reheating an old one
Weak, pale teaToo few leaves, too short a steep, or water poured before it boiledUse more leaves, wait until they sink, and pour the water at a full boil
Turkish coffee with no foamHeated too fast, stirred on the heat, allowed to boil, or the coffee was stale or not ground finely enoughUse fresh, finely ground coffee, stir only before heating, warm it slowly and lift it off the moment it rises
Coffee boils overHeat too high, or the pot was left unwatchedStay by the stove and lift the cezve as soon as the foam climbs

Questions people ask

How long should Turkish tea steep?

About 10 to 15 minutes on very low heat in the top pot of a çaydanlık; many tea houses go up to 20. Too short tastes thin, while too long or too hot turns it bitter.

What is the difference between açık and koyu tea?

Both come from the same strong brew. Açık (light) is a little brew topped up with plenty of hot water; koyu or demli (strong) is mostly brew with little water. Which one you drink is purely a matter of taste.

Why is good Turkish tea called tavşan kanı?

Tavşan kanı means rabbit's blood. It names the ideal colour of a well-brewed glass: a clear, deep, reddish amber that glows in the light, neither murky nor pale.

How is Turkish coffee served?

In a small cup with its foam on top, together with a glass of water and often a piece of lokum (Turkish delight). The water is drunk first to clear the palate, and the grounds stay at the bottom of the cup.
