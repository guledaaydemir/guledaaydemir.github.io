A day at the tea house

Order, call it out, carry, collect

Drag anywhere to walk. Stand next to something for a moment and the çırak does what is needed there. A day lasts about four minutes, then the tea house waits for you.

Take the order

Walk up to a table. The regulars' orders go onto the çırak's notepad.

Call it out to the usta

The usta brews only what you call out at the counter. Lift the glasses onto your hanging tray.

Carry it to the right table

Each glass goes to the table that ordered it, before their patience runs out.

Collect the bill, wipe up

Take the money, wipe the table until it sparkles, and welcome the next group from the door.

Çırağın Hayali

The çırak has a dream

Every evening Hasan Usta counts the day's takings and hands the çırak his wage. The çırak adds half of the day's tips and drops it all into his kumbara, his piggy bank.

He is saving for a car of his own. It costs 10,000 jeton, hundreds of evenings away, but the dream page shows him walking the long road toward it, flag by flag: 1, 5, 10, 25, 50 and 75 percent.

Run the tea house well, keep the guests happy and the tips coming, and you will see him get a little closer every night.

The neighbourhood

Everyone has a seat at the tea house

Hand-illustrated characters in several poses: they walk in, sit down, wipe, argue and celebrate.

The apprentice

The çırak

That's you. Red fez, quick feet, a hanging tray and a notepad full of orders. Every glass in the house passes through his hands.

The master

Hasan Usta

Keeps the tea brewing at the counter, but only what you call out to him. He can always calm a quarrel; it just takes him a while.

The regulars

The müdavims

Flat caps, big moustaches, strong opinions about okey. Some are in no hurry at all, some are in a great hurry, and now and then one brings a grandchild.

Municipal police

The zabıta

Comes in from the street when a quarrel gets out of hand, or when guests are too unhappy for too long.

Meet the neighbourhood cats

Feed them or gently see them out. A cat fed too often starts to feel at home.

Quick answers

What is HANE?

HANE is a tea-house management game for iPhone and iPad. You play the çırak, the apprentice of a traditional Turkish kıraathane: you take orders, carry tea, collect bills, see the cats out and grow one room into tea houses across Türkiye.

Does HANE have ads or in-app purchases?

No. HANE has no ads and no in-app purchases. Everything is earned by playing.

Does HANE work offline?

Yes. HANE plays fully offline and never needs an internet connection.

What languages is HANE in?

The game is in Turkish and English; you can switch under Settings. This website is also in Spanish.

What is a kıraathane?

A kıraathane (literally a "reading house") is a traditional Turkish coffeehouse and tea house where neighbours meet over tea, conversation and table games like okey and tavla.
