How to post consistently as a creator (without living on your phone)

Consistency isn't about willpower or a longer idea list. It's about removing the daily decisions that sit between “I took some photos” and “it's posted”. Here's a weekly loop that does exactly that, a 7-day sprint to set it up, and an honest toolkit, starting with the free apps already on your phone.

By Gül Eda Aydemir, indie iOS developer · Updated 15 Oct 2026 · 13 min read

Quick answer

Run one weekly loop instead of deciding every day: capture a little daily, cull right after you shoot, batch-make a week of posts in one sitting, post on fixed days, and spend 15 minutes reviewing on Sunday. Pick a cadence you can keep for 8 weeks (3 feed posts a week is a solid start) and keep a “minimum post” for rough weeks.

Full disclosure: I make Solo.Swipe, DUMP, Solora, Solo.Shots and ATHA, which all appear below. Every step works with the Camera, Photos, Notes and Calendar apps you already have. My apps just remove friction, and I list exactly what each one costs.

Why is it so hard to post consistently?

Usually not because you're short of ideas. It's because every post needs a dozen small decisions (which photo, which order, which caption, when) and you're making all of them from scratch, on your phone, at the end of a long day.

The usual culprits:

  • The camera roll is a mess, so finding “that one shot” takes ages.
  • Every post starts from a blank page.
  • You're waiting for a perfect post, so nothing goes out.
  • There's no fixed posting time, so posting competes with everything else.

The fix is to make each of those decisions once a week, in a batch, instead of once per post.

How often should you post?

As often as you can keep up for two months. For most people starting out, that's about 3 feed posts a week. A rhythm you can sustain beats a burst you abandon.

There is data behind “more is more, up to what you can manage”. Buffer analysed more than 2 million posts from over 100,000 Instagram accounts and found that accounts posting 3–5 times a week grew followers faster and got more reach per post than accounts posting once or twice a week, and posting even more helped further. Buffer's own recommendation was 3–5 posts a week as the sustainable middle ground. It's one company's analysis of its users, so read it as a direction, not a law.

A starter mix for 3 posts a week: one photo dump or carousel, one short video, and one flexible post (a tip, a behind-the-scenes shot or a before/after). Once that feels easy for a month, add a fourth.

What is the weekly creator loop?

It's five steps that turn a week of moments into a week of posts: capture, cull, make, post and review. Each step has a fixed slot, so nothing waits for motivation.

  1. CaptureEvery day · 2 min. Shoot little and often.
  2. CullAfter each shoot · 10 min. Keepers into one album.
  3. MakeOnce a week · 60 min. Batch all posts.
  4. PostFixed days · 10 min. Plus 15 min of replies.
  5. ReviewSunday · 15 min. Keep what worked.

Step 1: What should you capture during the week?

Shoot little and often, and shoot for the post, not just the moment. For anything worth posting, grab five shots: the wide view, a detail, hands doing the thing, the place, and a person (or the finished result). Those five already make a carousel.

  • Wide: where are we?
  • Detail: the texture, the label, the one small thing
  • Action: hands making, pouring, holding, fixing
  • Place: light, background, the mood
  • Person or result: you, a friend, or the finished thing

You don't need an app for this step. Keep a running shot list in the Notes app with your content pillars at the top (more on those below), and use the Camera you already have.

If your content is a change over time (a training block, a renovation, a plant, a pregnancy, hair growth), make it a series. Step 2: How do you cull photos fast after a shoot?

Do it within a day, while you still remember which shots were good: delete the obvious misses, then put the keepers in one album named after the post. A ten-minute cull now saves a lot of scrolling later.

  1. Open the shoot (one day in Photos).
  2. First pass: delete blurry, eyes-closed and near-identical shots. Keep only the best of each burst. (Not sure a blink shot is safe to bin? Here's when to delete closed-eye photos.)
  3. Second pass: put the 5–15 best into an album named after the post, like “Market Saturday”.
  4. Favorite the one you'd use as the cover.

You can do this in Photos. Tap Select, choose the keepers and use Add to Album. It's slower with hundreds of shots, but it works.

Step 3: How do you batch a week of posts in one sitting?

Block one hour, open the albums from your week, and make every post back to back: pick, order, crop, caption, save. Making three posts in a row is much quicker than making one post three times, because you only get set up once.

  1. 5 min: choose this week's posts, one per album.
  2. 30 min: build them. Put your strongest image first and keep one aspect ratio per post (here's the carousel size guide, and six rules for picking and ordering photos).
  3. 15 min: write all the captions in one note: a hook line, one useful detail, a question.
  4. 10 min: save everything in posting order, ready to go.

Step 4: When should you post?

On fixed days you choose in advance, at a time when you can stay around for 15 minutes to reply to comments. The best posting time is the one you'll actually keep.

  • Put your posting days in your calendar like appointments (for example Monday, Wednesday and Saturday at 7 pm)
  • Post from the album you saved in order: select all, paste the caption, share
  • Stay 15 minutes to reply, then close the app

If you made the post in DUMP, its Instagram button hands off to Instagram's composer with the slides already waiting in their album, in order.

Step 5: What should you check in a weekly review?

Three things, in 15 minutes: what worked (saves, shares, replies), what was easiest to make, and what you'll post next week. Choose next week's posts before you finish.

  • Which post got the most saves or shares? Make another one like it.
  • Which post was quickest to make? That's your “minimum post”.
  • Did you hit your cadence? If not, lower it rather than feel guilty.
  • Pick next week's three posts and the shoots they need.
  • Delete last week's leftover outtakes so the next cull starts clean.

What are content pillars, and how many do you need?

Content pillars are the 3–4 recurring topics your account is about. They kill the blank page: instead of “what should I post?”, you ask “which pillar is due?”. With 3 posts a week and 3 pillars, each pillar gets one post.

What should you post when you have nothing to post?

Post your week. A photo dump of ordinary moments, a before/after or a behind-the-scenes shot needs no new shoot and still gives people a reason to stop scrolling.

  • A weekly photo dump (DUMP's Sunday “Your week” draft does the picking)
  • A before and after: a room, a dish, an edit, a month of training
  • Behind the scenes of your last post
  • One tip you'd tell a friend, as a 3-slide carousel
  • A “this or that?” question with two photos
  • What you'd do differently about something you posted months ago
  • A monthly timelapse from a Solo.Shots series
  • An older post that still holds up, re-shared with a fresh caption

When are these apps the wrong choice?

When your workflow already lives somewhere else. Some honest cases:

  • You shoot on a dedicated camera and edit in a desktop editor. Cull there, not on your phone.
  • Your content is mostly video. DUMP can't trim clips yet, so cut Reels and TikToks in a video editor.
  • You need a shared calendar or client approvals. Solora is a personal planner with no sharing and no sync between devices; a team tool fits better.
  • You want to find every similar photo across years. Solo.Swipe's Duplicates mode (a Pro feature) only pairs photos taken in the same second at the same size, so it won't catch every near-duplicate.

Questions creators ask

How many times a week should I post on Instagram?

Start with what you can sustain for 8 weeks, which for most people is about 3 feed posts a week. Buffer's analysis of more than 2 million posts found that accounts posting 3–5 times a week grew faster than those posting once or twice, and posting more helped further, but only post more if you can keep the quality up.

How do I batch content as a beginner?

Shoot once, then make several posts in one sitting. Start small: one shoot at the weekend, one hour on Sunday to make three posts, and fixed posting days during the week.

What should I post when I have no ideas?

Post your week as a photo dump, a before and after, or the behind the scenes of something you already made. Rotating 3 content pillars also means you never start from zero.

How do I stay consistent when I'm busy or burned out?

Lower the cadence instead of stopping, and keep a “minimum post” you can make in 10 minutes, such as a weekly photo dump. A smaller schedule you keep beats a bigger one you quit.

Do I need to pay for apps to post consistently?

No. Photos albums, the Notes app and a calendar cover the whole loop. The free versions of Solo.Swipe, DUMP and Solora add speed; their paid tiers mostly matter at higher volume (more than 10 slides per post, 50 deletions a day or 4 roles), or if you want DUMP's extra templates, fonts and filters, no ads in Solo.Swipe, or Solora's calendar-aware AI day planning.
